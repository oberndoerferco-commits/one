First, a correction

Last pass I told you luxury copy is short — one idea per line, confidence through brevity. That rule is real, but it belongs to a different house: Aesop, Jil Sander, the Nordic minimalists. It is not how the maisons you actually sit beside write.

Hermès writes an object is never an accessory; it is a necessary punctuation, and calls a piece a tribute to the hand of the cabinetmaker and the upholsterer, where every joint and every stitch bears witness to uncompromising excellence. That is a long, warm, subordinate-clause sentence. Loro Piana Interiors works the same way — sensory, unhurried, material-first.

You were right that the page was too short. The Italian–French maison register is fuller and slower than what I gave you. That is what this pass fixes.

How the houses in your sector actually write

Grouped by the move each one makes. The pattern that matters to you is the third.

HermèsMaison & art de vivre

Philosophical, sensory, unhurried. Frames the handmade irregularity as the point: there is always been a degree of imperfection, because it’s the work of a hand, not a machine. Objects inhabit a space with their singular presence.

Take: permission to write long, and to name the hand.

Louis VuittonObjets Nomades

A trunk-maker moving into furniture — your exact manoeuvre. They legitimise it with one named historical commission: the Bed Trunk produced in 1874 for French explorer Pierre Savorgnan de Brazza. Not “we have heritage” — one object, one client, one year.

Take: the Miramare is your Brazza bed trunk. Name it, date it.

FretteMilano, 1860

The closest structural match to your story: a Lombard house whose whole prestige is we were chosen. They never say “prestigious clients.” They say: Official Purveyor to the Italian Royal Family, 1881; crests for more than five hundred European royal families; the altar of St Peter’s; the dining car of the Orient Express.

Take: institutions, dates and numbers do the boasting. Adjectives can’t.

BaxterBrianza, 1990

Leather furniture house that earns the right to leather by lineage: the founders descend from a family of master saddle-makers. A young company — 1990 — that never pretends otherwise, and is credible anyway.

Take: you don’t need an old date. You need a straight line from one craft to the next.

PromemoriaLake Como, 1988

Same move: the Sozzi family made a living by repairing aristocratic carriages in the 19th century. The origin is humble and specific, and that is precisely what makes it land.

Take: specific and modest beats grand and vague.

Poltrona FrauTolentino, 1912

Owns its material by naming it: Pelle Frau®, a proprietary full-grain hide with a named process — immersion dyeing. Place, date, technique, trademark.

Take: consider naming your hide or your finish. An unnamed material is a commodity.

Loro PianaInteriors

Pure tactility, almost no brand-shouting: the drape of a jacket, the softness under the hand, the sheen of a fabric in natural light. The material is the brand.

Take: describe how a thing behaves under a hand, not how good it is.

Serapian · ValextraMilano, 1928 & 1937

Your immediate Milanese neighbours. Both trade on a named local quality — the Milanese hand, the connoisseur’s rigour — and on invented techniques with dates (Serapian’s Mosaico, 1947).

Take: “Milano” in your name is a claim. Cash it with method, not adjectives.

The Miramare, properly understood

You have been under-using this. It is not “a hotel” — it is a documented belle-époque palace on a coast that Europe’s courts wintered in. All of this is on the record and none of it is currently on your page.

FactWhy it earns its place
Opened in the 1870s as the Hôtel des Îles Britanniques The original name tells you exactly who Sanremo was built for. Better than any adjective about “history”.
Became the Continental Palace, then took the name Miramare in 1935 Three names across sixty years. A building that has been re-made before — which is what your commission is.
White, Liberty (Art Nouveau) in style; Murano glass; reopened five-star in 2018 Concrete visual and material anchors that sit beside your own materials paragraph.
Empress Maria Alexandrovna, wife of Tsar Alexander II, wintered in Sanremo 1874–75; gave the town its palms; the seafront Passeggiata Imperatrice is named for her The Russian court is why that coast looks the way it does. One clause of this is worth a paragraph of “timeless elegance”.
Alfred Nobel spent his last five years in Sanremo; the town still sends flowers to the Stockholm Nobel banquet each 10 December Held in reserve — too good to crowd into this page, ideal for the Miramare Collection or a journal entry.
“The Lady in Grey”, painted 1894 by Filippo Grosso for Queen Margherita of Savoy, hangs in the reception rooms A commissioned object that stayed in the building for over a century. That is the argument for commissioning furniture, made by the hotel itself.

Miramare The Palace — Sanremo, Liguria

Heading

It began with our trunks in the lobby

Body

The hotel opened in the 1870s as the Hôtel des Îles Britanniques, in the years when Sanremo filled each winter with the Russian and English courts. It became the Continental Palace, and took the name Miramare in 1935. White, Liberty in style, still hung with Murano glass.

Our pieces went into its public rooms and stayed a season. Then the hotel asked whether we would make the rest — the seating, the tables, the things a room is actually built from. Saying yes committed the house to a discipline it had not practised before.

Why. This is the Frette method: a first paragraph of pure documented fact, so the second paragraph — the commission — lands on a reader who now understands what building chose you. Note that the boast is gone entirely and the story is far more impressive for it.
