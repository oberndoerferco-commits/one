Why it doesn’t read as luxury

Not one bad sentence — five habits, each of which quietly costs the page authority.

  1. 01

    The headline announces instead of implying

    A hotel asked us to furnish it is the sentence a founder says on a podcast. It’s a boast in the passive-aggressive register — vague about who (“a hotel”), loud about the flattery. Houses name the place and let the reader do the impressing.

  2. 02

    It reaches for the worn phrases

    The same attention to detail, handcrafted in Italy, bespoke furniture. These are category descriptors, not claims. Every Brianza workshop with a website says all three. Specificity is the only thing a reader can’t get elsewhere.

  3. 03

    The register breaks three times

    Reverent opening → transactional headline → sustainability disclosure (a byproduct of the meat industry) → e‑commerce button. Each is fine somewhere. Together on one scroll they read as four different writers.

  4. 04

    The closing metaphor is broken

    Our furniture belongs not only in your hands, but in the spaces you live in. Furniture does not belong in your hands. The line was written for leather goods and carried across; it collapses the moment you picture it.

  5. 05

    The last click contradicts the page

    Nine sections of made-to-commission furniture, then Shop the Collection into /collections/home-accessories — trays at €129. The reader is promised a commission and handed a coaster.

Five rules that hold the voice steady

Useful past this page — the same habits are worth applying to the jewellery and trunk descriptions.

Nouns over adjectives

“Full-grain calf from Tuscany, galvanised brass” tells a reader more than “exquisite materials” ever can. Adjectives ask to be believed; nouns can be checked.

Understate the boast

Give the reader the fact and let them draw the flattering conclusion. A hotel commission is impressive; saying so out loud makes it less so.

One idea per line

Luxury copy is short because it is confident. Two clauses joined by a dash usually means the writer wasn’t sure which half was the point.

Never name the emotion

Don’t write “timeless” — describe the thing that lasts. Don’t write “elegance” — set the object down and stop talking.

Keep one register per page

Decide whether a page is an essay, a spec, or a shop, and let the other two live elsewhere. Sustainability facts, dimensions, and CTAs each have a right room.

Punctuate like a house

Em dashes, not hyphens. Real quotation marks. It is a small thing that separates a maison from a Shopify default, and the page currently gets it wrong eleven times.
