Where the site stands, what the field does, and the finished shape

The copy pass gave the house a voice. Underneath it, the store is still built like a generic Shopify catalogue: colours listed as separate products, a menu that says “Home” and “Featured products”, and a home page that never says the house also makes rooms. This review sets the site against nine houses in the sector and finishes the plan.

Every serious house in this sector does three things the store does not yet do. It shows one design per page with the colours as swatches. It puts Bespoke and the house’s story one click from the home page, as a peer of the shop categories. And it keeps the menu to what a customer would actually say out loud: bags, trunks, small leather goods, home.

The product page, by contrast, is already ahead of most of the field: lead time per piece, gifting, care, delivery and materials in accordions, the packaging story below. What it lacks is a colour choice, which is a catalogue problem, not a page problem.

Six patterns that recur

  • Category-first menus. The shop items are the things a customer would name: bags, small leather goods, travel, home. Story pages sit together under one item (“Maison”, “Discover”, “About”). No house has “Home” or “Featured products” in its menu.
  • One design, one page. Colour is a swatch on the card and a selector on the page, never a second product. Serapian, Valextra, Bennett Winch and Frank Clegg all do this; it is also what Shopify’s own product model expects.
  • Bespoke is a peer of the shop. A nav item, a block on the home page, and a form that asks what kind of request it is. Serapian’s form offers Mosaico / Personalisation / Made to Measure / Bespoke; Promemoria leads its home page with it.
  • A stated promise. Serapian: 2-year warranty and appointments. Bennett Winch: lifetime promise. Frank Clegg: lifetime guarantee. The leather-goods houses all say, in one line, what happens after the sale.
  • Editorial in the menu. Valextra, Métier and Bennett Winch put the journal in the nav; craft pages are linked from the product page, not only from the footer.
  • Two selling models, cleanly separated. Leather-goods houses sell online with prices. Home and furniture houses (Giobagnara, Promemoria, Poltrona Frau) sell by enquiry and showroom. Oberndörfer straddles both, which is fine — but the site should say which model applies to which piece, and the product page now does.
Audit

Findings on the live site and the Claude theme, in priority order. P1 changes the experience; P3 is polish.

What the store does today

P1

Colours are separate products, so the catalogue reads as repetition

128 of 129 listings have a single variant with the option named “Title”. Eighteen designs are listed once per colour: two watch-box models nine times each, the 3-Place Watch Box six times, the Crocodile Wallet six times, the Mini Crochet Handbag six times. The “All” grid opens on six identical watch boxes; the briefcase page’s “You may also like” shows four more.

Effect. The store looks three times larger and three times less considered than it is. Filtering by colour is impossible. Every peer site shows the design once with swatches.
Fix. Merge each family into one product with a Colour option (images per variant, swatches on the card), and redirect the old handles so search rankings and any links keep working. This is a scripted migration; it needs your sign-off because it changes URLs on the live store.
P1

The menu is a list of Shopify objects, not a list of what you make

Main menu today: Home · Collections (New In, Featured products, Bags, Trunks, SMALL LEATHER GOODS, HOME ACCESSORIES, TRAVEL, Oberndörfer x Trax NYC, EYEWEAR) · About us (About us, Contact, FAQ) · Bespoke (Bespoke, Art of Living) · Custom & Limited Editions. Case is mixed, two items repeat their own parent, and “Featured products” is a merchandising feed shown as a department.

Collections underneath. Watch boxes are counted under both Trunks (43) and Travel (43). Three catch-all collections overlap (COLLECTIONS 127, Featured products 85, Collections 2 42). Six jewellery and golf collections from a previous catalogue are still live and reachable by URL. Rules match on title text (“Bag”, “Box”) rather than product type, and product types themselves are inconsistent (“sofa”, “Furniture”).
Fix. The proposed menu is in the blueprint below — one edit in Shopify, but it changes the live site, so it waits for your word. Collections should be rebuilt on product type, with one Home & Living collection for furniture, trunks-as-tables, trays and poufs.
Done

The home page never said the house makes rooms

Between the product grids sat a section headed “Find your perfect fit / Discover the best of both worlds” with a before-and-after slider comparing the logo to a photograph. Nothing on the page led to Bespoke or Art of Living, which are the two things that separate this house from a bag brand.

Applied to the Claude theme. That section is now “Made to order — Every piece is also a starting point”, with two tiles (the atelier, the trunk table at the Miramare) and two buttons: Begin a commission and Art of Living. The hero button reads Discover the collections; the editorial block’s Discover more is now The house; the closing band asks “Looking for a leather, a colour or a size you don’t see?” with Write to the atelier.
Done

The product page had no route for the customer who wants something slightly different

The page is strong: lead time per piece, “Handmade in Italy / Shipping / Made to order” facts, four accordions, the packaging story. But a customer wanting the briefcase in blue Togo, or the pouf in a colour not listed, had only the footer.

Applied to the Claude theme. Directly under the buy buttons: “Prefer a different leather, colour or lining? Most pieces can be made to order. Write to the atelier and we will confirm what is possible and the lead time.” The link carries the product handle to the contact page.
P2

Product photography lacks a sense of scale

Galleries are generous (median five images, up to thirteen) but there is no in-hand or in-room frame on most pieces. Baymard’s testing finds 42% of shoppers try to judge size from the images; the dimensions line helps, a photograph with a hand, a desk or a wrist helps more. One such frame per design, shot once and reused across colours, is enough.

P2

The live site still runs the old copy

The live theme shows “MADE IN ITALY — SUSTAINABLE LUXURY” in all caps and the pre-rewrite About block; every rewrite so far lives only in the unpublished Claude theme. Nothing here reaches a customer until that theme is published. The blueprint ends with a publishing order.

P3

Product descriptions speak in two voices

The watch box: “the velvet is what actually protects the watches, since it won’t scratch a case back the way a harder lining would.” The briefcase: “an essential creation that reflects the Maison’s exceptional artisanal craftsmanship.” Roughly half the catalogue is in the first voice. After the merge there will be about 68 descriptions to bring into line, not 129.

P3

No stated promise after the sale, and the journal is hidden

Returns and free DHL are stated; nothing says what happens in year five. Serapian says two years; Bennett Winch says lifetime. A house that makes by hand can credibly say “We repair what we make” — a line for the product page facts and the footer, if you are prepared to honour it. The journal has 16 articles, seven published and nine drafted, and appears only in the footer.

House style

A second look at the story pages on 3 September, after reading the bespoke and story pages of Serapian, Valextra, Moynat and Giobagnara at desktop width, and the earlier notes on Hermès, Métier and Bennett Winch.

How the story pages look and read

The field splits in two. Serapian and Valextra sell the service with adjectives: “design the creation of your dreams”, “the ultimate expression of exclusivity and artistry”, a headline in capitals that promises an experience, and a button in every chapter. Moynat, Hermès and Bennett Winch do the opposite: small tracked capitals for the label, a calm serif or a quiet grotesk for the sentence, body copy that states what is done and how, and a text link rather than a button. The second group is where a small house with real workshops should stand, because concrete sentences are the one thing a large house cannot buy. The pages already spoke that way; what they lacked was a consistent visual grammar to match, and a Bespoke page that said what happens after the customer writes.

What the competitors do well, and what was taken

SerapianExplains the processMock-up for approval before anything is made; initials; no returns on bespoke; one persistent “Request your Bespoke”. Taken: a four-step “How a commission works” and one primary button per page.
MoynatTracked capitals, text linksEyebrows in small spaced capitals; actions as underlined text (“Book an appointment”) rather than boxes. Taken: the eyebrow rule and link-style chapter actions.
ValextraServices as headed chaptersEach service a chapter with a photograph of the tool, not the product. Taken: chapter photographs of the work (moulds, hammers, the bench). Not taken: the bold grotesk headlines, the small grey body text, a button in every chapter.
GiobagnaraWhitespaceText set in a serif and left to float. Not taken: third-person brand prose (“a brand that is distinctive for elegantly designed creations”) and pages without a way to act.

The grammar, now applied to every story page

1EyebrowInter, 12px, capitals, loose tracking. Names a place, a material or a chapter: MIRAMARE THE PALACE — SANREMO, LIGURIA; HARDWARE; 02 · THE DRAWING.
2Chapter headMarcellus, 32px, sentence case, and it says something: “Furniture, to the measure of the room”, “The hide, accounted for”. The h3 preset is now the heading face, so the Inter-bold capitals that had crept into the image chapters (IT BEGAN WITH OUR TRUNKS IN THE LOBBY) are gone everywhere at once.
3StatementMarcellus, 24px, centred, one or two lines, used once to open a page or a section: “Made once, for one person.”, “How a commission works”.
4BodyInter 16px, left aligned beside a photograph, centred only under a statement. Chapter text sits at the vertical centre of its photograph instead of being stretched from the top corner to the bottom.
5ActionsOne primary button per page, at the close. Inside chapters an underlined text link, “Begin a commission”, that goes to the same form.
6RhythmHero → statement → chapters alternating photograph left and right → a row of facts or steps → photographs in pairs → close band with the one button. Art of Living keeps its Riviera blue; every other story page sits on the page ground with the sand band for the close.

Writing rules, restated for the story pages

  • A heading is a sentence with a claim in it, in sentence case. Labels stay in the eyebrow.
  • Second person where the reader acts (Bespoke, Care); first person plural for what the house does. Never third person about the brand.
  • Concrete nouns and numbers instead of adjectives: “4–6 weeks”, “solid brass, galvanised in gold, palladium or black PVD”, “nothing is cut until you have approved them”.
  • Say what happens next and what it costs. Bespoke now states the four steps, the timings, that a bespoke piece cannot be returned, and that repairs are quoted per piece from a photograph.
  • Captions under photographs say what is in them, in the eyebrow style, and nothing more: “The trunk that became a table”.
