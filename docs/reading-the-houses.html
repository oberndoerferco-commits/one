The one habit all three share

None of them sells. Not one sentence on a Loro Piana, Hermès or Brunello Cucinelli product page tells you how the object will make you feel, or who you will be while carrying it. The copy states what the thing is, what it is made of, and one fact about how it was made. The desire is expected to be produced by the object and the photograph. The words are there to confirm the object is what it appears to be.

That is the whole discipline, and it is a negative one. What follows is mostly a catalogue of things they refuse to write.

Never

A verb aimed at the reader. No Introducing our, no Shine bright with, no Discover. The product, the yarn or the detail is the subject of every sentence.

Never

Second person. The reader is never addressed, never told what the piece is perfect for, never given an occasion.

Never

Self-praise as a claim. No masterpiece, exquisite applied to their own work, unparalleled, timeless elegance as a summary.

Never

A benefit list with bolded lead-ins. No Premium Materials:, no Product Highlights:. Specifications appear as flat labelled values, separate from the prose.

Never

Search terms inside the sentence. No leather tote bag for women and men. The category is carried by the product name and the navigation, not smuggled into the description.

Always

Origin stated flat at the end, as a fact and not a boast: Made in France. Made in Italy.

Loro Piana

Quarona, Piedmont / cloth first

Loro Piana is the most useful of the three for us, because its naming is a system rather than a tradition. Every title is built from the same four slots, and the slots are always in the same order.

  • New Piuma V-Neck Cashmere Tapioca

Model

A proper noun that belongs to a silhouette, not to a category. Roadster is a jacket, a sweater and a half-zip. Open Walk is an ankle boot and a loafer. The name survives the material change, so a customer who liked one piece can find its relatives.

Object

The plainest possible noun. Sweater. Loafer. Jacket. Never luxury knit, never a silhouette adjective doing the naming work.

Material

The fibre, and where the house owns it, the trademark: The Gift of Kings®, Storm System®. A named material is an asset that survives seasons.

Colour

Never a plain colour word. Tapioca, Nougat, Stone Dove, Sandstone, Pine Bark, Shamrock, Caviar, Optical White. The colour name is the only place in the whole title where the house permits itself any poetry.

What their description does

Inspired by the iconic jacket of the same name, the Roadster sweater is designed for those who are always en route, at the wheel and beyond. It is spun from ultra-soft cashmere yarns in a two-tone motif that runs diagonally across the garment. Sculpted with a high zipped neckline, this versatile layer complements the apparel of classic car drivers.

Loro Piana, Roadster sweater

Three sentences, three jobs. The first places the piece in the house's own family, which is only possible because the model names are reused. The second is construction. The third is use, described as a fact about the garment rather than an instruction to the reader. Note that even the softest word in it, ultra-soft, is attached to the yarn, not to the experience of wearing it.

Hermès

Paris / harness first

Hermès takes the opposite route on names and an even stricter one on description. A name is a single proper noun with no category word attached at all, and the size is a number after it.

  • Kelly
  • Birkin
  • Constance
  • Evelyne
  • Picotin
  • Lindy
  • Bolide
  • Della Cavalleria
  • Oran
  • Calvi
  • Kelly 25

You cannot tell from Kelly that it is a bag, or from Oran that it is a sandal. That is deliberate: the name is an entry in a house vocabulary customers are expected to learn, which is why the same names have been earning for fifty years. The one place the category appears is the description, and it appears as the first word.

Reversible leather strap in Epsom calfskin. Buckle in guilloched palladium plated metal.

Formula

[Object] in [material], then any single distinguishing detail. No verb. Frequently no complete sentence at all.

Adjectives

Effectively none, and never one that praises. Epsom is a leather, not a compliment. The only evaluative word that appears in the specimens above is iconic, and it is describing a shape the customer already recognises.

Specs

Labelled values on their own lines, outside the prose: Heel height: 1 cm, Metallic finish: Palladium plated, Made in Italy. Nothing measurable is ever buried inside a sentence.

Effect

The restraint is the signal. A house that explains why its bag is special is a house that suspects you might not agree.

The line we can already hit

Our own normalised apparel copy is already at this standard: "A cotton T-shirt in black, with the house crest embroidered at the centre of the chest." That is the Hermès sentence exactly. The problem is that only one lane of the catalogue speaks this way.

Brunello Cucinelli

Solomeo, Umbria / cashmere first

Cucinelli is the counter-example, and worth reading precisely because it breaks the rule the other two follow. There are almost no proper nouns in the catalogue. The product name is a plain description, in sentence case, with no colour in it at all.

  • Cashmere sweater with monili
  • Cashmere and silk Diamond yarn scarf
  • Cashmere Feather yarn cardigan
  • Nordic jacquard turtleneck sweater
  • Cashmere jersey tailored trousers

Two details make this work rather than read as generic. First, the proprietary nouns sit on the yarn, not the garment: Feather yarn, Diamond yarn, monili. Second, colour is never in the title, because colour is a variant of one product rather than a separate product. Where Loro Piana buys distinctiveness with model names, Cucinelli buys it with material names and spends the rest of its personality in the description.

This timeless cashmere sweater combines the beauty of knitwear, endless comfort and elegant details. Inspired by rib knitting, a precious shiny monili embroidery stands out on the crew-neck, adding a delicate sparkle in true Brunello Cucinelli style.

Brunello Cucinelli, cashmere sweater with monili

The refined, iconic yarn of the Brunello Cucinelli collections elevates the essential feel of this new cashmere sweater. The garment features relaxed proportions, with dropped shoulders and slightly generous sleeves, for extraordinary comfort. A precious monili embroidery completes the sweater with a feminine sparkling note.

Brunello Cucinelli, cashmere sweater

Subject

Always the garment, the yarn or the detail. The garment features, a monili embroidery stands out, the yarn elevates. Never we, never you, never our.

Warmth

This is the warmest of the three voices, and the warmth is still never pointed at the reader. It is spent on the material.

Length

Two to three sentences. There is no fourth sentence, and no bullet list under it.

Caution

The one house habit not to copy: timeless, precious, refined and iconic carry weight for a house whose name has meant one material for fifty years. On a younger catalogue the same words read as filler. Take the sentence structure, leave the adjectives.

The grammar, extracted

Our apparel lane already resolved this in August. The Crest T-Shirt, The Monogram Puffer Jacket, The Milano T-Shirt: proper-noun model, plain object noun, colour after a hyphen. That is the Loro Piana slot system with an English definite article. It does not need reinventing, it needs extending to the other 200 listings.

  • The model name is fixed to the silhouette, so it survives a change of leather. The Mirror Handbag in Ostrich is already correct. Brown Ostrich Bag is the same idea done wrong: the material has eaten the name.
  • The object noun is the plainest available word. Handbag. Backpack. Wallet. Tote.
  • Material appears only when the same model exists in more than one, and it appears as in [Material], never as a leading adjective.
  • Colour is last, after a hyphen, and it is a named colour rather than a plain one wherever the lane can carry it: Cognac and Tortoise already do this, Grey and Brown do not.
  • No factory codes, no untranslated generics, no category keywords.

Descriptions: four moves, in this order

1  Object

One sentence in the Hermès form: what it is, in what material, in what colour. A structured trunk bag in calf leather, in black.

2  Build

One or two sentences on the single construction fact that explains the price. A rigid core. A goat-leather interior. Hand-burnished edges. Choose the one that is true and unusual, not the three that are true and expected.

3  Time

One sentence on how the material behaves with use, or on the house signature the piece carries. This is the Cucinelli move, and it is the only place warmth belongs.

4  Facts

Composition, dimensions, origin, compliance. Flat, labelled, unsentenced. Calf leather, Alcantara lining. 39 × 32 × 14.5 cm. Made in Italy.

Two house rules this has to respect

Prices are stated once and calmly, so no description mentions value, worth or investment. And every factual claim, lead time and compliance line has to match what the policy pages say, because a contradiction between a product page and a shipping page is how trust is lost.

Applied

Four live listings, rewritten against the grammar. Marked text is what the three houses would not write.

A name library

Loro Piana names from its own world: the roads it drove, the walks it took. Hermès names from its history. The catalogue already leans Milanese, and the apparel lane already uses Milano as a model name, so the register is set. Scope

Jewellery sits outside catalogue and merchandising work under the house standard, so nothing here is proposed for the rings, necklaces or bracelets. The spelling corrections are the exception worth raising separately, since Rubin and Saphire appear in titles a customer reads before anything else.

Before a listing goes live

  1. Read the first sentence alone. It should say what the object is and what it is made of, and nothing else. If it opens with a verb aimed at the reader, rewrite it.
  2. Search the copy for "you", "your", "our" and "we". None of the three houses uses any of them on a product page.
  3. Delete every adjective the house applied to itself. Exquisite, masterpiece, unparalleled, premium, refined. Keep adjectives that describe a material's behaviour, since those are checkable.
  4. Move every number out of the prose. Dimensions, weights, carats and lead times belong in the facts block, on their own lines.
  5. Check the name against the silhouette. If the same shape exists in another leather or colour, both listings carry the same model name.
  6. Cut the occasion. No listing tells a customer where to take the thing or what it is perfect for.
  7. Confirm the claim matches the policy pages. Lead time, made-to-order status, CITES documentation and returns say the same thing everywhere they appear.
  8. Spell the material and the city. Ruby, sapphire, nubuck, Valenza.
