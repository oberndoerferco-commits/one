# The standing plan

Written 15 September 2026. Revisit when the first ten real orders have landed.

## Where the business actually stands

| | Twelve months to 15 Sep 2026 |
| --- | --- |
| Sessions | 8,601 |
| Cart additions | 158 |
| Reached checkout | 139 |
| **Completed orders** | **1** |
| **Revenue** | **€10** — a test order |

The store is not broken. Payments clear, DHL Express ships free worldwide,
products are available for sale. The infrastructure is fine.

What is missing is qualified demand, and proof that a stranger can safely send
this house €720.

## What the data says to do

**1. Traffic is mostly not human.** 2,754 of 4,837 sessions in six months were
"direct / United States" with no purchase intent. Customer signups come from
`storebotmail.joonix.net`. Until the numbers are filtered, every decision made
on them is made on noise. → `performance-analyst`

**2. One channel already works.** A single blog post — *Why Real Leather is
More Sustainable than Eco Leather* — drew 365 sessions in 90 days, unattended,
since 2024. Paid has delivered nothing. → `editorial-director`

**3. Ten finished articles sit unpublished.** They are written. They cost
nothing to ship. Publish them spaced out, each linking to products it genuinely
serves. → `editorial-director`

**4. 139 people reached checkout and none came back.** There is no email
capture and no abandoned-checkout recovery on the warmest audience this
business has had. → `client-relations`

**5. 269 products is too many at zero revenue.** Pick the 15–20 that can
realistically make the first sales — €110–€800, good photography, a real search
story — and make those pages excellent. → `merchandising-manager`

**6. The top of the catalogue cannot be sold from a cart.** €34,250 trunks and
€27,000 sofas need an enquiry path, a dossier, and a person. That is the
majority of the catalogue's value sitting behind a button nobody will press.
→ `client-advisor`

## Order of work

Do these in sequence. Each one makes the next worth more.

| # | Move | Agent | Why now |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Filter the analytics so numbers mean something | `performance-analyst` | everything downstream depends on it |
| 2 | Publish the article backlog, spaced | `editorial-director` | free inventory on the proven channel |
| 3 | Email capture + abandoned checkout | `client-relations` | stop losing the warm ones |
| 4 | Fix the 15–20 product pages that can sell | `merchandising-manager` | traffic without proof converts at zero |
| 5 | Build the commission path for the top end | `client-advisor` | unlocks the catalogue's real value |
| 6 | Atelier footage — the hand at work | `art-director` | the strongest proof a leather house owns |

## What to measure

Not sessions. Sessions are the number that has been lying.

- **Filtered human sessions from search** — is the content engine pulling?
- **Cart additions per 100 human sessions** — do the pages persuade?
- **Checkout completion rate** — currently 0%. The single number that matters.
- **Email addresses captured** — the leading indicator for a considered purchase.
- **Enquiries on pieces over €2,500** — the top end's equivalent of a cart add.

## The one thing only the owner can do

**Name the leather.** Poltrona Frau has Pelle Frau®. Serapian has Mosaico. An
unnamed hide is a commodity; a named one is proprietary, and it changes every
product page in the catalogue at once. No one in the house can decide this; `atelier-director` can only make it easy by establishing what the leather actually is. It was already
identified in the copy review as the highest-leverage change available.
