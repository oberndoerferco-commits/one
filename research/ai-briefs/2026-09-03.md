# Oberndörfer AI Brief — 2026-09-03

**Artifact:** https://claude.ai/code/artifact/f1fb694c-5efb-4ad1-8d05-a8c28e4b70a2

First edition. Baseline of where AI stands for a craft leather house, what the
competition is doing with it, and what to post. Later briefs report only what changed.

## TL;DR

1. **Hold one line: AI may stage, animate, translate and organise — it may never
   depict the hand, the artisan, or the grain of the leather.** Gucci (Milan, Feb 2026)
   and Valentino (Dec 2025) both used generative imagery in front-facing campaigns and
   were called "cheap" and "lazy" by their own audiences. Hermès went the other way and
   commissioned a human illustrator for its website. For a house whose entire claim is
   "made by hand around Milan", the downside is asymmetric.
2. **From 2 August 2026, EU AI Act Article 50 requires disclosure of AI-generated
   content.** You sell into the EU. Labelling costs nothing in reach — no major platform
   penalises the AI label — but an unlabelled AI image that gets detected via C2PA does
   cost trust.
3. **The cheapest real win is room-scene staging for the furniture.** A DTC sofa brand
   replaced €4–6k-per-product location shoots with AI room scenes and measured conversion
   within 5% of professional photography. Your sofas, poufs and trunk-tables are exactly
   this case — one real studio shot of the piece, many rooms around it.

## 1. The rule for this house

The judgement that matters is not "which tool" but "where the line sits". Sorted by risk:

| Use | What | Why it is safe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Yes** | Room staging for sofas, poufs, trunk-tables | The piece is a real photograph; only the room is generated. No claim about the hand. |
| **Yes** | Background extension / reframing a real photo to 9:16 | The object is untouched. |
| **Yes** | Subtitles, dubbing atelier videos into EN/IT/DE/中文/العربية | Widens reach; the voice and the hands are real. |
| **Yes** | Copy drafts, product attributes, alt text, customer replies (drafted, you send) | Nobody buys leather for the alt text. |
| **Yes** | Upscaling and cleanup of your own photography | Restores detail; invents none. |
| **Careful** | Animating a real product still into a 5s hero clip | Fine for a slow pan or a light sweep. The moment the leather flexes or the brass warps, it is a lie about the material — discard the take. |
| **Careful** | AI-assisted colourways / bespoke previews for a client | Label it "simulation" in the conversation. LVMH does exactly this: AI proposes variations, artisans make the real object. |
| **No** | Generated hands, artisans, ateliers, or workshop scenes | This is precisely what Gucci was punished for. |
| **No** | Generated leather grain, patina, stitching or hardware presented as a product | It misrepresents the material you actually sell, and it is the one thing your buyer inspects. |
| **No** | AI models wearing or holding the bags | Same category as above, with a person attached. |

**Disclosure practice.** Where a published image or video contains generated pixels, say
so plainly — "room rendered digitally; the piece is photographed" is a sentence that
costs you nothing and reads as confidence. Article 50 wants machine-readable marking from
the generator plus deployer transparency for anything published; most tools now embed
C2PA credentials by default, so leaving the label on is easier than stripping it.

Your Higgsfield account already exposes Seedance 2.0/2.5, Kling 3.0, Veo 3.1, Wan 3.0 and
FLUX 3 Video behind one interface, plus Genjutsu (motion transfer, object replacement) and
a Marketing Studio preset for TikTok/Reels-shaped product ads. It is on the free plan with
**0 credits** — nothing can be generated until that changes.

### Image

- **Nano Banana Pro / Nano Banana 2** (Google) — the leader in multi-reference identity
  locking: feed 3–5 photos of the same trunk from different angles and it holds the object
  consistent across generated scenes. Best for iterative, conversational edits.
- **Seedream 5.0** (ByteDance) — most photorealistic product output, native 4K without
  upscaling, and specifically strong on complex materials: brushed metal, glass, soft
  fabrics. Relevant: brass hardware and nabuk.
- **FLUX.2**, **GPT Image 2** — general workhorses; GPT Image 2 for anything with text in
  the image.
- **Photoroom / Claid / Caspa** — furniture-specific staging products, if you would rather
  have a workflow than a model.

**Caveat worth stating:** every one of these will happily invent stitching that does not
exist. For a catalogue image the test is not "does it look good" but "would the client who
receives the piece recognise it". Use them for the room, not the object.

### Commerce

**Shopify Sidekick** is free on every plan including Basic — no per-query limits. The
Winter '26 "RenAIssance" edition (150+ updates) turned it from a chat assistant into
something that executes multi-step tasks with full store context: campaigns, discount
codes, product attributes, segments. Also new: Agentic Storefronts, Horizon AI, Knowledge
Base, AI-generated product attributes. This is already paid for; it is the least
glamorous and highest-return item on this page.

## 3. Competitors & peers

- **Hermès — refuses front-facing AI.** Commissioned artist Sarah Martinon to illustrate
  its website over several months rather than generate visuals, and refuses to put
  customer service on chatbots. *Lesson: the refusal is itself the marketing. You are
  small enough to make the same promise credibly, and to say it out loud.*
- **LVMH — AI everywhere except the object.** An internal "AI Factory" serves Dior, Louis
  Vuitton and Tiffany with forecasting, personalisation and creative production; LV's
  design team generates hundreds of pattern and colour variations, creative directors
  select, artisans make the real thing. *Lesson: the model that works — AI upstream of the
  hand, never in place of it.*
- **Gucci — punished for it.** AI-generated imagery for its PRIMAVERA show at Milan
  Fashion Week (Feb 2026) drew comments calling it cheap and lazy, and out of step with a
  brand built on Italian craftsmanship. **Valentino** got the same reaction for a labelled
  AI video for the DeVain bag (Dec 2025) — note that labelling did not save it. *Lesson:
  disclosure protects you legally, not reputationally. The content itself has to be
  defensible.*
- **Research finding worth knowing:** consumers tie luxury authenticity to *perceived
  effort*. When a luxury brand discloses AI in an ad, the ad is judged less authentic —
  the disclosure penalty is real and specific to luxury. This is the argument for keeping
  AI in staging and logistics, where no effort claim is being made.
- **Furniture houses (Poltrona Frau, Minotti, B&B Italia):** could not verify any public
  AI configurator or AR programme from these three. Their sites would need checking
  directly. Meanwhile smaller furniture sellers are visibly moving: one brand reported a
  47% conversion lift and $180k/yr saved switching to AI product photography; a DTC sofa
  brand measured AI room scenes within 5% of professional shots on conversion. *Lesson:
  the volume end of furniture is going AI for lifestyle imagery. Your differentiation is
  not resisting that for the room — it is refusing it for the object.*

## 4. Post ideas for the coming week

Instagram now ranks Reels by watch time and completion first, then **sends per reach**,
then likes — DM sends carry 3–5× the weight of likes for reaching non-followers. Carousels
are rewarded on saves. So: Reels to be found, carousels to be kept.

1. **Hand-burnishing an edge, 20s, no music, real sound.** Reel + TikTok. The ASMR
   leathercraft format is doing 900k–1.2M likes for anonymous workshop accounts; you have
   the same footage and a real house behind it. *AI: none. This is the asset.*
2. **"Same trunk, four rooms."** Carousel. One real photograph of the mini trunk, staged
   into four interiors. Caption states the rooms are rendered. *AI: Nano Banana Pro or
   Seedream, room only.* Saves-optimised.
3. **Patina at 0, 6 months, 3 years.** Carousel or Reel. Three real photographs of the same
   piece. If you don't have the 3-year frame, ask a client for it — that request is itself
   a post. *AI: none.*
4. **The tannery answer.** Reel, founder to camera, 30s: where the hides come from, why
   byproduct, what CITES paperwork means. *AI: subtitles + dubbing to EN/IT/DE.*
5. **Watch box, one continuous take.** Reel. Opening the 8-place box, lid to lining, no
   cuts. Trunk-maker's presentation logic is the story. *AI: none; upscale only.*
6. **"What full-grain means."** Carousel, 5 frames, macro shots of pores and markings vs.
   corrected grain. Educational carousels are the format Instagram rewards on saves.
   *AI: none.*
7. **Trax NYC jewellery trunk, hero clip.** 5s Reel: slow push-in on the real product
   still, red Alcantara catching light. Cross-post with the partner. *AI: Seedance 2.0
   image-to-video from your own photograph — discard any take where the brass or the
   leather deforms.*
8. **Bespoke, in progress.** Story series → Highlight. Sketch, hide selection, cutting,
   stitching, finished piece for one real commission. The Miramare story is the proof this
   works; document the next one as it happens. *AI: none.*

Two of these (2, 7) use AI and both keep it off the object. That ratio is the point.
