## 1. What each product produces, from what input, in which languages (incl. EU availability)

### Takeaway
Almost the whole market is ambient-audio first. You record the consult on a phone or desktop and get a draft SOAP note, plus client summaries/discharge, call summaries and record summaries. Typed or text input is marginal:
- after-the-fact dictation
- typed context fields
- document/handwriting uploads for summarisation
- one competitor-reported "text or voice" claim for Digitail

No vendor reviewed positions itself as microphone-free. Consolidation is under way: Instinct bought ScribbleVet (16 Jan 2026) and IDEXX bought CoVet (16 Sep 2026). Most PIMS vendors now bundle a native ambient scribe: Covetrus Pulse, Digitail, Vetspire, Shepherd, ezyVet.

### Inferences
- Text-first, no-microphone documentation is effectively uncontested as a primary mode among named vendors. The nearest overlaps are:
  - Talkatoo's AI Assistant (typed/dictated details → reports)
  - ScribbleVet's summariser of uploaded handwritten notes
  - VetRec's narrated after-the-fact records
  - Digitail "text or voice" (claimed by a competitor)
  - Heidi's dictate mode
- The broader output set the target product plans (prescription, vaccination entry, consent form, estimate from the approved record) is mostly not offered by scribes. Partial overlaps:
  - PetsApp billing prompts
  - VetNotes billable-item detection
  - Digitail voice-to-invoice
  - Talkatoo dosage instructions
  - Covetrus "Scripts" product suggestions
- Referral letters exist at CoVet, Heidi (two vet referral templates) and Tandem. ScribbleVet has only an "rDVM email" option.
- For EU positioning, the realistic rivals are CoVet (now IDEXX), Heidi, Tandem (vet integrations not live) and Lupa (UK). Major US scribes VetRec, Scribenote and ScribbleVet state US hosting, which creates GDPR international-transfer work for EU clinics.

## 2. Pricing, free tiers and trials (with dates)

### Takeaway
Standalone scribes cluster at about $79–$200 per DVM/month. Common terms:
- techs and support staff free
- 14–30-day trials
- annual discounts

Free tiers have become normal: Scribenote Free (Jun 2025), Heidi Free, Tandem Free. PIMS vendors give native scribes away or bundle them: Covetrus Pulse free since Jan 2025, PetsApp free for UK clinics since Nov 2025, Digitail Growth plans. Price alone is therefore a weak differentiator.

### Inferences
- The going rate for an unlimited standalone vet scribe in Oct 2026 is about $99/DVM/month on annual billing:
  - VetRec $99
  - CoVet $99
  - Scribenote $79–99
  - ScribbleVet $150 annual
- Free and bundled offers (Covetrus, PetsApp UK, Scribenote Free, Heidi Free, Tandem Free) put a floor of $0 under basic SOAP generation.
- A text-first product probably needs to justify its price on trust, auditability and downstream documents rather than undercut on price.

## 6. Vet sentiment: reviews, Reddit/VIN, trade press, adoption surveys

### Takeaway
Adoption is rising fast, and documentation is the top AI use case.
- Digitail/AAHA 2026 (US/Canada, n=1,730): 83.7% of vet professionals use AI; 62.8% of AI users use it for clinical documentation/scribes.
- Instinct 2026: 48% of general practices use AI.
- VIN polls: AI-record use rose from 3.5% to 17.5% between Jul 2024 and Sep 2025.

Praise centres on hours saved, going home on time, ER throughput and integration. Complaints centre on:
- invented or wrong drug and vaccine entries
- other-pet contamination
- omissions in history
- verbosity and irrelevant asides
- regressions after updates, crashes and upload failures
- cancellation and billing friction

Accuracy/reliability is the top concern in every survey found: 67.4% (Digitail/AAHA 2026), 70.3% (2024), 54% (CoVet 2026).

### Cited Findings

**Adoption surveys**
- Digitail/AAHA 2026 benchmark (n=1,730 self-reported, US and Canada; fielded 27 Jul–12 Aug 2026; article 6 Oct 2026):
  - AI use 83.7%, up from 39.2% in 2024; 88.3% of users use AI daily or weekly
  - clinical documentation (scribes/SOAP) is the top use, at 62.8% of AI users
  - concerns: reliability/accuracy 67.4%, data security/privacy 59% (rising), lack of training 47.1%
  - optimists 56.3%, skeptics 22.7%; fear of job displacement 20% (37.2% in 2024)

## 7. Peer-reviewed or independent evaluations of veterinary AI scribes (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
I found no peer-reviewed study that measures the accuracy of a veterinary AI scribe. The key independent veterinary paper is a transparency audit, Brundage, Frontiers in Veterinary Science, 5 Mar 2026. It found that generative/ambient vet AI vendors almost never publish validation:
- median weighted transparency score 0%
- 2.1% publish peer-reviewed or accuracy metrics
- 0% report confidence intervals

The best error-rate evidence comes from human medicine. An Aug 2026 audit found verified failures in 31.3% of notes from three commercial ambient scribes, with omission errors being under-detected. That human-medicine evidence is the most rigorous comparator available.

### Cited Findings
- **Brundage D.**, "A systematic audit of transparency and validation disclosure in commercial veterinary artificial intelligence", Frontiers in Veterinary Science 13 (2026), published 5 Mar 2026. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/veterinary-science/articles/10.3389/fvets.2026.1761038/full)
  - **Sample**: 71 commercial clinical vet AI products (47 generative/ambient, 19 imaging, 5 specialised), North America, audited 17–24 Nov 2025.
  - **Method**: 25-point Veterinary AI Transparency Index; single rater; κ>0.90 on a 20% re-audit.
  - **Results**:
    - mean transparency 6.4%; 63.3% of vendors disclosed none of the metrics
    - risk-weighted mean 1.8% for generative vs 13.1% for imaging
    - peer-reviewed/accuracy metrics: 2.1% of generative vendors vs 36.8% of imaging
    - independent test set: 2.1% vs 26.3%
    - confidence intervals: 0% vs 15.8%
    - generative-tool median weighted score: 0.0%
  - **Arguments**: omission errors are harder to detect than commission errors, so human-in-the-loop review "may be less effective than assumed". Vendors are layering "Proactive Consultant" features (differentials, triage) on top of documentation tools.
  - **Proposals**: veterinary model cards ("nutrition label") and "VetAI-Trust" third-party accreditation.
  - No vendors are named.
- **Fox et al.**, "One note in three: a verified census of three deployed AI scribes…", arXiv 2608.31017, 31 Aug 2026. Human medicine (UK primary care and US ambulatory). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31017)
  - three unnamed commercial ambient scribes on the same 142 consultations; 565 notes
  - 31.3% of notes (95% CI 27.0–35.6) had a verified failure; 24.8% excluding invented identity/dates
  - failures clustered in allergies/medications, invented patient identity, and history recorded as examination
  - omissions were 23.1% of verified errors, vs 54–86% in prior audits
  - measured failure rates swing from 28% to 97% depending on the audit instrument
  - errors remain common in clinician-signed notes
  - preprint, not peer-reviewed
- **Digital Health (SAGE)**, 20 Nov 2025, Cleveland Clinic primary-care QI pilot. Human medicine; n=10 physicians; unnamed scribe not integrated with Epic. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12638734/)
  - typing fell significantly (p=0.03)
  - burnout and after-hours EHR time did not improve significantly over 4 weeks
  - issues with speaker identification and complex multi-problem visits
- Vendor-run "evaluations" with no independent review:
  - Lupa benchmark (12 Aug 2026) — [Lupa](https://lupapets.com/blog/veterinary-ai-scribe-benchmark/)
  - ScribbleVet "competitive trials" (91% good/excellent) — [FAQ](https://instinct.vet/blog/scribblevet-faq-veterinary-ai-scribe/)
  - HappyDoc "99.8%" — [HappyDoc](https://www.happydoc.ai/)

### Inferences
- The evidence vacuum is a positioning opening. A text-first tool could publish:
  - a per-sentence source-attribution rate
  - an "unsupported content" rate, which is about zero by design
  - a missing-field detection rate
  - all on a defined test set with confidence intervals
- That would meet the Frontiers "model card" bar that, per the audit, essentially no generative vet tool currently meets.
- The human-medicine finding that review does not catch many errors supports a design where the vet authors every sentence and can see where it came from, rather than reviewing machine-written prose.

### Gaps
- No JAVMA, Veterinary Record, JSAP or Frontiers paper measuring vet-scribe note accuracy or time savings was found. The search budget ran out before journal-specific searches could be completed, so a targeted PubMed check is still advisable.
- The University of Florida–ScribbleVet and Cornell–VetRec academic partnerships may have produced evaluations, but none were found or opened.
