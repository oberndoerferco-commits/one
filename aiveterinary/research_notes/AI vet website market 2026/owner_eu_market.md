# Owner-facing pet health websites and apps in Italy and DACH (state as of 2026-10-18)

Scope: owner-facing products only (information, AI triage, tele-vet, insurer apps, digital records/passports, reminders, registry owner portals) in Italy, Germany, Austria, Switzerland. Vet-facing software is excluded unless an owner app is the client side of a vet platform (Petleo, Digitail). All pages below were opened on 2026-10-18 unless a page date is given. "Accessed 2026-10-18" means the page itself carried no date. Older information is marked "[older]".

Method note for the report writer: the shared web-search budget ran out partway through. After that only direct fetches of known URLs were possible, so some named products could not be located (listed under Gaps). Several PDFs came back as binary and were text-extracted locally (FNOVI, Tierärztekammer Westfalen-Lippe, Quixa, Abruzzo/Ministry report, EBCD). Store ratings are from Apple App Store pages for the named storefront only. I could not open any Google Play pages, because I had no app IDs once search was gone.

## 1. Which products lead in Italy and DACH, by category?

### Takeaway
No product covers the whole job in either market. In Germany, FirstVet leads tele-vet because pet insurers bundle it, and small GOT-billed rivals (HaustierDocs, Pfotendoctor, Dr. SAM) compete on 24/7 access. Petleo (500,000+ animals) and vetevo (5,434 App Store ratings) lead owner health records. In Italy the market is fragmented and Italian-only. Barkibu leads insurer apps by review volume (17,029 Trustpilot reviews). Health-record apps are small (MyVet has 112 ratings; Teradog and Paw.net have 11 and 7), and tele-vet comes mostly bundled in insurance (Quixa/AXA "My Vet") rather than as a standalone brand. National registries (SINAC, Heimtierdatenbank, AMICUS, TASSO/FINDEFIX) hold identity data, not health or vaccination records.

### Inferences
- In Germany, the default way owners reach a tele-vet is through their insurer. FirstVet's partner list covers most digital pet insurers, so a new site cannot win on "video vet" alone. It can win by making those consultations better: a brief to bring, and records to share.
- In Italy, no tele-vet brand comparable to FirstVet showed up in the pages I could open. Italian owners get vet access through insurance bundles (Quixa/AXA, Barkibu) or small apps with few ratings. The space for a trusted Italian-language owner hub looks open.
- Petleo is the closest DACH competitor to aiveterinary's booklet and pre-visit brief. It is DE/EN/IT, has records, and does digital intake before visits, but it depends on the owner's practice being connected to it.

## 2. What each product actually offers: features, languages, AI use and labelling, vet involvement, emergency handling

### Takeaway
Across the pages I opened, no major Italian or DACH vet brand offers an owner-facing AI symptom checker. AI shows up behind the scenes instead: Dalma uses it for claims, Petleo for vet documentation, Digitail for clinic-enabled intake. The one owner-facing exception, animundo's "KI-Coach", gives no labelling or limits on its store page. Tele-vet is human-only, usually limited to advice and triage, and German providers say they cannot prescribe without a prior exam. Emergency handling is mostly a line telling owners to go to a clinic. Most products work in one language; Petleo (DE/EN/IT) and AMICUS (DE/FR/IT) are the exceptions.

### Cited Findings
**Tele-vet scope, vet involvement, emergencies**
- FirstVet DE: video with licensed vets; the treatment record is emailed afterwards; referral to a practice if needed; a symptom browser by body area (eyes, fever, ticks). No AI is mentioned and no emergency protocol is described. Site in German (accessed 2026-10-18). — [FirstVet DE](https://firstvet.com/de/)
- HaustierDocs: says to go to the nearest clinic immediately for serious accidents, bleeding, breathing difficulty or poisoning. Vets can give first-aid guidance by video and point to open emergency services. Video prescriptions are not allowed unless the vet has examined the animal before. Covers dogs, cats, rodents and exotics. No AI (accessed 2026-10-18). — [HaustierDocs](https://www.haustierdocs.de/)
- Pfotendoctor: dogs and cats only; second opinions, prevention and nutrition; a separate emergency-vet finder is linked; owners without a camera can dial in by phone with a PIN. No AI (accessed 2026-10-18). — [Pfotendoctor](https://www.pfotendoctor.de/)
- Allianz DE lists what telemedicine can cover: advice, virtual examination, second opinion, post-surgery telemonitoring and teletriage. It states "Keine Verschreibung von Medikamenten möglich" and says acute cases still need a practice (accessed 2026-10-18). — [Allianz Telemedizin](https://www.allianz.de/gesundheit/tierkrankenversicherung/telemedizin/)
- DA Direkt: video for a first assessment, with a treatment recommendation and a detailed report; acute emergencies go to a local vet immediately (accessed 2026-10-18). — [DA Direkt](https://da-direkt.de/tierversicherung/onlinetierarzt)
- Quixa "My Vet" (IT, 15 Apr 2026): an urgent call is transferred to the vet service, which decides whether to continue by phone or video. If a medicine is needed, a "ricetta medica bianca" is sent to the nearest pharmacy. A separate 24/7 information service "non fornisce diagnosi o prescrizioni". Information portals, social networks and forums do not count as telemedicine [older; published in FNOVI's "30 Giorni", Feb 2021 issue]. — [FNOVI Telemedicina (PDF)](https://mail.fnovi.it/sites/default/files/Telemedicina.pdf)

### Inferences
- aiveterinary could be the first EU owner tool to say plainly that it is AI-assisted, does not diagnose and is vet-reviewed. That matches FNOVI's teletriage line ("no diagnosis communicated") and would contrast with animundo's unlabelled KI-Coach.
- "What to do in an emergency" is a one-line disclaimer almost everywhere. A structured, printable emergency and red-flag guide in EN/IT/DE, with links to national emergency finders, is an easy point of difference.
- Records apps rely on manual entry (vetevo, MyVet, Teradog, Paw.net) or on sync with a connected clinic (Petleo, Digitail). An owner-held booklet that imports the paper document fills a hole between those two models.
- Quixa's "supporto lettura referti" (help reading vet reports) shows Italian insurers see demand for help understanding vet documents. aiveterinary's vet-reviewed explainers and pre-visit brief address the same need without needing an insurance policy.

### Gaps
- I could not determine the languages actually supported inside apps where the store's language list conflicts with the description (vetevo, animundo, VetOnline24).
- I could not confirm German legal limits on remote prescribing from a primary legal source (TÄHAV, GOT). The "no prescribing" statements come from Allianz and HaustierDocs. I also did not open the Italian ricetta elettronica veterinaria (REV) rules.
- Two AI symptom-check tools, PawCheck and "Pet App: Tracker & Care", appeared only in search results and were never opened, so they are not cited.

## 3. Trust signals shown

### Takeaway
Tele-vet providers in Germany lean on named vets, German hosting badges ("Made & Hosted in Germany"), DSGVO icons and insurer logos. Insurers show regulator numbers (IVASS/RUI, ORIAS) and Trustpilot or app-store scores. Content portals are weakest: AniCura's large article bases name no authors, and tiermedizinportal.de shows no review dates and carries affiliate links. Trustpilot scores can mislead (FirstVet 3.1 alongside 92% five-star ratings). Nobody I checked showed Ordine or Kammer registration numbers for their vets.

### Cited Findings

Its online specialist count is inconsistent on the page ("57" vs "über 80"). It shows a "Made & Hosted in Germany" label, an innovation award and three testimonials (accessed 2026-10-18). — [HaustierDocs](https://www.haustierdocs.de/)
- Pfotendoctor lists named vets with titles (no licence numbers; some names repeat), DSGVO, SSL and "made and hosted" badges, insurer partner logos and a Trustpilot link with no score shown (accessed 2026-10-18). — [Pfotendoctor](https://www.pfotendoctor.de/)
- FirstVet DE shows "approbierte" vets, an "Unsere Tierärzt:innen" link (no names on the homepage), a 4.9 App Store rating, "1 Million" consultations, "98%" satisfied, a Berlin address and a "Bekannt aus" logo strip (accessed 2026-10-18). — [FirstVet DE](https://firstvet.com/de/)

## 5. Owner pain points and unfilled gaps

### Takeaway
The clearest pain points: tele-vet slots too few or delayed (FirstVet waits of 48 h to 3 days, while insurers require a video consult first); small preventive budgets (€70 to €100); records apps that are clunky (no backdating, can't link a treatment to a visit) or crash on document upload; and a structural risk that a lost paper passport also loses the vaccination history, because German chamber guidance tells vets not to copy over third-party entries. No product I found imports a photo of the paper EU passport, libretto or Impfpass. None offers multilingual travel help, or an owner-held record that crosses borders and clinics.

### Inferences
- **Gap 1, paper-to-digital import.** None of the records apps I checked (Petleo, vetevo, MyVet, Teradog, Paw.net, 11pets, Digitail, animundo) advertises scanning or photo import of the EU passport, Italian libretto or German Impfpass. aiveterinary's photo import is a real first.
- **Gap 2, lost-passport insurance.** Replacement vets may refuse to copy over vaccinations they did not give, and record-keeping is required for only 3 years under the 2026 rules, or 10 years under the older German guidance. A dated photo archive of every passport page, plus a "how to rebuild proof" guide (contact the original vet, which records to request), meets a real need. The archive is supporting evidence only and is not legally valid.
- **Gap 3, multilingual cross-border travel help.** Everything I found works in one language, apart from Petleo (DE/EN/IT) and AMICUS (DE/FR/IT). The new rules (Reg. 2026/131 from 22 Apr 2026, new passport model, transponder country code from 2028) create a need for a plain EN/IT/DE travel checklist.
- **Gap 4, clinic-agnostic pre-visit brief.** Pre-visit intake exists only inside clinic platforms (Petleo, Digitail AI intake). Owners whose vet uses another system, or who book a tele-vet slot, have nothing. A printable brief also answers the long-questionnaire complaint about FirstVet, because owners prepare the information before the slot.
- **Gap 5, honest triage.** Owners stuck in tele-vet queues of 48 h or more need "is this urgent?" guidance. Under FNOVI's framing this must not communicate a diagnosis, so vet-reviewed red-flag lists with clear AI labelling fit.
- **Gap 6, Italian quality bar.** Italian record apps have few ratings (7 to 112) and VetOnline24 has 2.7 stars, so a well-built Italian-language product meets little established competition.

### Gaps
- I found no forum threads (Reddit, gutefrage.net, forum.alfemminile) about lost passports or libretti, and no reviews that state "lost paper passport" as a complaint. That pain point rests on regulatory guidance, not on observed owner complaints.
- I did not open LAVES (Niedersachsen) guidance, which search snippets suggested allows transferring vaccinations (the PDF returned 404). The German position may differ by Land.
- The Mimikama fact-check on a viral "online register with QR code from April 2026" claim returned 403, so I could not verify what owners were told.
- I opened no Google Play reviews.
- Unverified Swiss legal friction: a search snippet from tierimrecht.org (about 2020) quoted a Swiss lawyer saying insurers may refuse to pay for online consultations by non-Swiss vets. I never opened the source, so this is not a finding.

## 6. Official moves toward digital pet passports and digital vaccination records (EU, Italy, DACH)

### Takeaway
There is no digital EU pet passport, and none is scheduled. From 22 Apr 2026 the EU replaced its pet travel rules (Reg. 2026/131, 2026/132, Impl. Reg. 2026/705) with a new passport model, and blank passports are explicitly paper-only. The big digital step is Regulation (EU) 2026/1818 (published in the OJ on 10 Aug 2026). It requires microchipping and registration in national databases that must work together, an EU index database, a free online verification token for online ads, and a pet-traveller pre-notification database. These arrive slowly: national databases from 31 Aug 2030, index and verification from 31 Aug 2031, traveller database from 31 Aug 2036, and private owners' duties for dogs from 31 Aug 2036 and for cats from 31 Aug 2041. Nationally, Italy's SINAC has a digital-ID citizen portal and stores health events. Austria's Heimtierdatenbank supports ID Austria self-registration. Switzerland's AMICUS is dogs-only. Germany still has no national mandatory registration; a 2024 draft-law approach (linking existing registers via HABS) remains unconfirmed.

### Cited Findings
**EU pet travel rules (apply from 22 Apr 2026)**
- Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131 of 20 Jan 2026 sets animal health requirements for non-commercial movement of pets. Published OJ L 27 Mar 2026, applies from 22 Apr 2026, and replaces Reg. 576/2013 (which applied until 21 Apr 2026). Art. 11: the passport is signed by the owner and issued in the Member State where the owner lives. Art. 7: transponders without a country code implanted before 1 Jan 2028 are accepted. Art. 8: rabies vaccination at least 21 days before travel. The visible text mentions no online registration, QR code or electronic passport. — [EUR-Lex 2026/131](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2026/131/oj/eng)
- Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/132 of 20 Jan 2026 amends Reg. 2019/2035 on traceability of dogs, cats and ferrets, and applies from 22 Apr 2026. Art. 71: the passport follows the model in Annex I of Impl. Reg. 2026/705; the passport number is the ISO country code plus a unique code. Art. 71b: competent authorities supply blank passports "in paper form only" to authorised vets. Vets keep records for at least 3 years. From 1 Jan 2028 transponder codes must start with the ISO 3166 code of the Member State where the animal was first identified. Old passports stay valid: 2003/803 model if issued before 29 Dec 2014; 577/2013 model if issued before 22 Apr 2026. The regulation says nothing on databases or electronic passports. — [EUR-Lex 2026/132](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2026/132/oj/eng)
- Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/705 of 20 Mar 2026 sets the model identification documents (Annex I: passport for intra-EU moves). OJ 27 Mar 2026; applies 22 Apr 2026. Art. 6 transition: passports on the old 577/2013 model count as compliant if issued before 1 Jan 2028; old-model animal health certificates issued before 1 Oct 2026 stay valid until 31 Mar 2027. The visible text has no digital, QR or machine-readable elements (only part of a 747k-character page was read; the passport model is embedded as an image). — [EUR-Lex 2026/705](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2026/705/oj/eng). Possible tension: 2026/132 Art. 71(4) refers to old-model passports "issued before 22 April 2026", while 2026/705 Art. 6 refers to "before 1 January 2028". Read together, old-format stock may be usable until the end of 2027. Verify the exact article text before publishing.
- The European Commission's pet movement page points to Delegated Reg. 2026/131 for non-commercial travel and mentions no digital passport (accessed 2026-10-18). — [EC pet movement](https://food.ec.europa.eu/animals/movement-pets_en)

**EU welfare and traceability regulation**
- Regulation (EU) 2026/1818 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 17 Jun 2026 "on the welfare of dogs and cats and their traceability", published OJ L 10 Aug 2026. Main provisions:
  - Art. 20: a single injectable transponder per animal; the vet registers it in a national database within 2 working days.
  - Art. 23: national databases; the Commission runs an index database.
  - Art. 21(5): a free, public online system to verify the identification, registration and ownership of advertised animals, which generates a token.
  - Art. 26(4): a Union pet traveller database for pre-notifying non-commercial entries from third countries.
  - Art. 33: general application from 31 Aug 2028; Art. 21(3) and 23(1) from 31 Aug 2030; Art. 21(5) and 23(3)–(4) from 31 Aug 2031; Art. 26(4) from 31 Aug 2036.
  - Art. 20(7): identification duties apply to operators placing animals on the market from 31 Aug 2030, to pet owners for dogs from 31 Aug 2036 and for cats from 31 Aug 2041.
  - Recitals: owner data kept 5 years after death or 25 years after first registration; sellers must give proof of identification and registration; verification tokens kept 18 months.
  - Source: [EUR-Lex 2026/1818](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1818/oj/eng)
- The European Parliament adopted it on 28 Apr 2026, after political agreement in Nov 2025. Implementing and delegated acts are still to come. — [PETworldwide, 30 Apr 2026](https://www.petworldwide.net/content-1/news/2026/04/30/free/eu-adopts-landmark-rules-for-dogs-and-cats.html)
- Il Sole 24 Ore (11 Aug 2026) reported publication on 10 Aug 2026. Buyers must receive the pet's vaccination status and known conditions. — [Il Sole 24 Ore English](https://en.ilsole24ore.com/art/dogs-and-cats-eu-rules-to-ensure-animal-welfare-and-traceability-AJrqVaj)
- Commission roadmap presented on 3 Jun 2026 (DG SANTE Head of Unit Andrea Gavinelli):
  - a "major study on genetics and digital solutions" starting summer 2026;
  - "digital frameworks" in 2028–2029;
  - "a centralised index database and verification system is slated for 2031, followed by a dedicated Pet Travellers' Database in 2036";
  - Member States' "national pet databases must be built or modernized by 2030 and achieve full cross-border interoperability by 2031".
  - Source: [EBCD final report, June 2026 (PDF)](https://ebcd.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Final-report-%E2%80%98The-new-EU-rules-on-the-welfare-and-traceability-of-dogs-and-cats-%E2%80%93-Delivering-effective-implementation.pdf)

### Inferences
- The paper passport stays the legal travel document for the foreseeable future: blank passports are paper-only from 2026, and no digital-passport act is announced. aiveterinary's booklet should be framed as a companion and backup to the paper passport, never as a replacement. Copy along the lines of "keep the paper passport; this helps you never lose what is in it" is safer and more credible.
- Owners will not get one cross-border view of their pet's record from the authorities before 2030 to 2031, when national databases and interoperability arrive. Even then, the EU systems cover identity and ownership, not vaccination history. A local-first, owner-held health record therefore stays useful for at least five years, and probably beyond.
- New rules to explain to owners in EN/IT/DE, all good content opportunities: the new passport model from 22 Apr 2026; transponders needing a country code from 1 Jan 2028; old passports staying valid; a Union verification token for anyone buying a pet online from 2031; mandatory chip and registration for owned dogs from 2036 and cats from 2041 (earlier where national law already requires it, as in Italy for dogs and Austria).
- Italy's SINAC already holds health events and has a SPID-based citizen view. If it opens to all regions, it could become the reference record for Italian owners. aiveterinary could later offer import from a SINAC "scheda sanitaria" export, if one exists (not verified).
