## 1. Italy: dispensing to pet owners under D.Lgs. 7 dicembre 2023, n. 218

**Act.** D.Lgs. 7 dicembre 2023, n. 218, *Adeguamento della normativa nazionale alle disposizioni del regolamento (UE) 2019/6…*, GU Serie generale n. 2 of 3 Jan 2024, in force 18 Jan 2024.

**Art. 37 (Cessione dei medicinali veterinari)**, text in force from 18 Jan 2024; Normattiva shows it unchanged in the version in force on 23 Oct 2026:

> 1. Il medico veterinario, nell'ambito della propria attività, può consegnare all'allevatore o al proprietario degli animali medicinali veterinari della propria scorta, anche da confezioni multiple in frazioni distribuibili singolarmente, ove disponibili sul mercato, corredate di foglietto illustrativo o altro supporto elettronico fruibile per ogni singola frazione, allo scopo di avviare la terapia prescritta in modo da garantire la tutela immediata del benessere animale.
> 2. Il medico veterinario di cui al comma 1, fatti salvi gli obblighi di registrazione posti a carico dei proprietari e detentori di animali destinati alla produzione di alimenti ai sensi dell'articolo 108 del regolamento, registra, entro sette giorni, lo scarico delle confezioni o delle frazioni consegnate nel sistema informativo della tracciabilità, secondo la specifica funzione.

Related provisions in the same act:
- **Art. 33** (stock in clinics). The *direttore sanitario* is the responsible vet. They name the vets who may access the stock and keep those names up to date in SINAC (c. 2). Human medicines kept in stock **may not be handed to owners** (c. 6). For medicines used on non-food animals there is a stock check every six months, and the traceability system must be updated by the 5th day of the following month (c. 9).
- **Art. 34 c. 5**: the same ban on handing over human medicines for vets without a clinic (*attività zooiatrica*).
- **Art. 42 c. 30**: a vet who does not comply with art. 37 pays an administrative fine of **€2,600–15,500**. **c. 35**: handing over human medicines from stock costs €8,000–50,000.
- **Art. 44**: D.Lgs. 193/2006 is repealed from 18 Jan 2024.
- An annex lets pharmacies dispense REV prescriptions through the electronic-prescription infrastructure (SAC/SAR) of Sistema TS, which is connected to the traceability system. This is the only mention of Sistema TS in the act. It concerns pharmacies, not expense data.

**Sources**
- Normattiva, art. 37 in force: https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:decreto.legislativo:2023-12-07;218~art37 (opened 23 Oct 2026; page states "vigente al 23/10/2026", "Testo in vigore dal: 18-1-2024")

**Confidence: high.** The open question is how the "specifica funzione" works in practice in the Vetinfo traceability system for pet clinics. That needs the Ministry's operating manual, which I did not open.

## 2. Italy: vets and Sistema Tessera Sanitaria (expense data)

**Answer: mandatory.** Vets are on the list of parties who must send data. Sending is not voluntary.

- **D.Lgs. 21 novembre 2014, n. 175, art. 3 c. 4**: a MEF decree names further parties who must send data on deductible or detractable expenses. **c. 5-bis**: if data is not sent, sent late or sent wrong, the fine is **€100 per communication, up to €50,000**. There is no fine if the corrected data is sent within 5 days. If the data is sent correctly within 60 days of the deadline, the fine drops to a third (max €20,000). Normattiva shows the text in force on 23 Oct 2026 (version in force from 13 Jan 2024).
- **D.M. MEF 1° settembre 2016, art. 2** (*Trasmissione telematica delle spese veterinarie sostenute dalle persone fisiche*, GU n. 214 of 13 Sept 2016):
  > Ai fini della elaborazione della dichiarazione dei redditi da parte dell'Agenzia delle entrate, gli iscritti agli albi professionali dei veterinari inviano al Sistema Tessera Sanitaria i dati delle spese veterinarie sostenute dalle persone fisiche a partire dal 1° gennaio 2016, riguardanti le tipologie di animali individuate dal decreto del Ministero delle finanze 6 giugno 2001, n. 289.
- **D.M. RGS 16 settembre 2016**, art. 1 lett. u and **art. 2 c. 2**: vets send the expense data as shown on the tax document, "comprensivi del codice fiscale riportato sulla tessera sanitaria", plus any refunds. Art. 2 c. 4: they request their Sistema TS credentials from MEF.
- **Calendar for 2026 expenses** (Sistema TS note of 5 Jan 2026, "spese sanitarie e veterinarie"): the deadline is **1 Feb 2027** (DM 29/10/2025). From 2025 the deadline is annual.
- The Sistema TS rules page lists the 29 Oct 2025 MEF decree (GU n. 261 of 10 Nov 2025), which also covers veterinary expenses.

**What this means for the product.** The owner's codice fiscale is needed for the REV and for the invoice. It is also the key under which the expense reaches the 730 precompilato. The product does not send anything to Sistema TS; the clinic's PIMS or the vet does.

**Sources**

## 3. Italy: L. 23 settembre 2025, n. 132: implementing decrees

**The law.** L. 132/2025 was published in GU n. 223 of 25 Sept 2025. It entered into force on **10 Oct 2025**, as the Normattiva act page states ("Entrata in vigore del provvedimento: 10/10/2025"). Normattiva also says "Ultimo aggiornamento all'atto pubblicato il 26/06/2026"; that update changed art. 19 (national strategy), in force from 31 Dec 2025.

**Health-sector decrees the law calls for:**
- **Art. 9**: a Ministry of Health decree, due within 120 days, on processing personal data for AI research and experimentation. *I found none.* The Normattiva "atti attuativi" panel is empty, and a search found neither a GU publication nor a Garante opinion on such a draft.
- **Art. 10**, which inserts art. 12-bis into D.L. 179/2012: one or more Ministry of Health decrees on AI solutions supporting the purposes of the FSE (*fascicolo sanitario elettronico*), and an AGENAS AI platform for care. *I found no decree.*
- These provisions are about human health care (SSN, FSE, AGENAS). None of them sets a duty for veterinary practice.

**Legislative decrees under art. 24 (AI Act alignment), deadline 10 Oct 2026:**
- **D.Lgs. 9 settembre 2026, n. 160** (GU n. 214 of 15 Sept 2026, in force 30 Sept 2026): police use of AI, civil and criminal liability. The chapter headings show nothing on health care. Only the Normattiva summary page was opened.
- **D.Lgs. 7 ottobre 2026, n. 179** (GU n. 234 of 8 Oct 2026): powers of the national authorities (AgID, ACN) and AI in training. It has no entry-into-force clause, so the ordinary 15-day *vacatio* applies: it is in force from 23 Oct 2026. Relevant to vets:
  - **Art. 47**: the ordini's initial and continuing training must include AI literacy. That covers how the systems work, the law (the AI Act and L. 132/2025) and ethics, including "gli obblighi informativi verso il cliente". The national councils have 6 months to adapt their rules (c. 5).
  - **Art. 51**: ECM for health professionals must include AI courses; the ECM commission sets a share of the three-year credit within 6 months. Veterinarians take ECM, so this reaches them too.
  - **Art. 49**: AI use may affect the *equo compenso* parameters. These parameters are due within 12 months.
  - Nothing in D.Lgs. 179/2026 changes art. 13 of L. 132/2025, the duty to tell clients about AI use.

## 4. Switzerland

### 4a. Retention of records (GST)

- **GST Standesordnung** (adopted by the Delegiertenversammlung on 23 June 2022, in force 1 July 2022), 4.1 e): "Die Tierärztin führt eine hinreichende Dokumentation über die in Ausübung ihres Berufes gemachten Feststellungen und die getroffenen Massnahmen." **It sets no retention period.** The only period in the document is ten years for the files of the GST's own disciplinary proceedings (Verfahrensordnung, Art. 7 para. 2), which is not a rule for practices.
- **TAMV (SR 812.212.27), Art. 29**: the documents under Arts. 10, 19 and 26–28 are kept for **three years**, and at least until any ongoing proceeding ends; longer periods in other acts take precedence. These articles cover the medicine records of anyone who dispenses medicines for **farm animals** (Art. 25) and the treatment journal. Art. 27 para. 2 adds that a vet who dispenses for both farm animals and pets must show each share in the records.
- **OR Art. 958f**: business books, accounting vouchers, the annual report and the audit report are kept for **ten years**, counted from the end of the business year. This covers invoices.
- Not opened: cantonal health laws, which can set periods for *Krankengeschichten*.

### 4b. AMICUS: duties of the vet (Tierseuchenverordnung, TSV, SR 916.401, as of 1 Jan 2026)

- Art. 17 para. 1–3: dogs are chipped within 3 months of birth and before any handover. Only a vet with a cantonal licence and a practice in Switzerland may chip, and the chipping data listed in the article (a–j) is collected.
- Art. 17a para. 2: microchips with country code Switzerland are supplied only to such vets, who must own a reader.
- Art. 17b: an imported dog's chip is checked by a vet within 10 days of import; the article lists the data to collect.
- Art. 17c para. 1: the vet enters the chipping or check data in the dog database. Para. 2: the vet *may* enter a dog's death for the owner.
- Art. 17d: owners, buyers and sellers record a sale, acquisition, or handover of more than three months within **10 days**. Owners record a death within **10 days**, and report name and address changes to the municipality within 10 days.
- Art. 17l: owner data is deleted ten years after the death of the person's last dog.

### 4c. Dog bites (Tierschutzverordnung, TSchV, SR 455.1, as of 1 Feb 2026)

- **Art. 78 para. 1** (as amended 20 Dec 2024, in force 1 Feb 2025): vets, doctors, shelter managers, pet-care providers, dog trainers and customs officers *must* report to the cantonal office any incident in which a dog (a) has injured people or animals significantly, or (b) shows excessive aggression. Para. 2: the cantons may extend the duty to others.
- Art. 79: the cantonal office checks the report, may order measures, and records it in ASAN.

## 5. Austria

### 5a. Standesregeln and records

- **Tierärztegesetz (TÄG), BGBl. I Nr. 171/2021**, RIS version of 23 Oct 2026:
  - § 27: general duties. Vets practise conscientiously, in line with veterinary science and the law, and keep up continuing training.
  - § 28: duties when treating animals. Avoid conduct that lowers the profession's standing; tell the keeper the pros, cons and **costs** of the options; answer on request which medicines were given or prescribed; first aid may not be refused; withdrawal from a treatment must be announced in time.
  - § 29: duty of confidentiality (Geheimhaltungspflicht).
  - § 31: advertising limits. § 32: fee schedule.
  - § 16 para. 3: the Kammer issues guidelines on practice standards (*Ordinationsrichtlinien*).
  - § 22: vets may process personal data for documentation, billing, reports and information under GDPR and the DSG.
  - § 41 para. 1 Z 14: failing to meet the TÄG's recording duties is an administrative offence.
  - **The TÄG sets no general retention period for treatment records.** The one period in it, **seven years**, is in § 7 para. 5 Z 6 and applies only to vets working across the border under the freedom to provide services.
- **Tierarzneimittelgesetz (TAMG), BGBl. I Nr. 186/2023**, RIS version of 23 Oct 2026:
  - § 66 para. 2: the medicines documentation is kept **seven years**, shown to the authority on request, and deleted after 30 years at the latest.
  - § 68 para. 1: every dispensing from the practice's own pharmacy (*Hausapotheke*), pets included, is recorded with date, species, keeper's name and address, quantity, and product name with **authorisation number**. Batch, diagnosis, dose and withdrawal period are added only for **food-producing** animals (para. 2).
  - § 53: the prescribing vet keeps prescriptions for **seven years**.

### 5b. Bite reports

Full research, a table for each Land and the sources: `docs/25-at-bite-report.md` (23 Oct 2026). In short:

- The TÄG contains no duty for vets to report bites.
- **No Land obliges private vets, doctors, keepers or the public to report a bite.** I read the dog provisions of all nine Länder: Wiener Tierhaltegesetz, NÖ Hundehaltegesetz, Oö. Hundehaltegesetz 2024 with the Hundehalteverordnung, Salzburger, Steiermärkisches, Kärntner, Vorarlberger and Burgenländisches Landessicherheitsgesetz, and the Tiroler Landes-Polizeigesetz. In each, the Gemeinde (Bürgermeister, Magistrat) acts once it learns of facts. It declares the dog bissig, auffällig or gefährlich and imposes measures: muzzle and leash, Sachkunde or Hundeführschein, a behavioural evaluation (OÖ), a Wesenstest (Sbg), a permit (Sbg, Bgld), or seizure.
- Duties exist only for officials:
  - Tirol LPG § 6a para. 4: the Amtstierarzt reports a dog he assessed as auffällig.
  - Tirol LPG § 28 para. 2 and § 23 para. 3: the police and the penal authority report on-duty observations of a dog injuring or endangering someone.
  - Bgld LSG § 30: the police report an auffällig dog.
  - OÖ HHVO § 5 para. 3: the examiner of the Alltagstauglichkeitsprüfung reports aggression shown during the exam.
- Salzburg (§ 19 para. 3) and Burgenland (§ 22 para. 1) act on a **written notice backed by facts** from anyone. That is where a voluntary vet letter fits. TÄG § 29 (confidentiality) must be cleared first.
- **Correction:** Wiener Tierhaltegesetz § 8 is not only about wild animals. Para. 5 makes the Hundeführschein mandatory for a "bissig" dog (as defined in § 2 para. 3). It also orders seizure, and euthanasia ex lege, after a bite that seriously injures or kills a person. It still puts no reporting duty on anyone.
- **Federal, rabies only:**
  - EpiG §§ 1–3: a bite injury from a rabid or rabies-suspected animal goes to the Bezirksverwaltungsbehörde (Gesundheitsamt) within 24 h. Vets are among those obliged (§ 3 para. 1 Z 9).
  - TGG 2024 § 36: vets report suspected animal diseases.
  - TGG 2024 § 48: a rabies-suspected dog or cat is isolated. If vaccinated, it is observed for ≥ 10 days, with a vet exam at the start and at the end.
  - The ÖTK vaccination guideline (2017) still cites the old TSG.
