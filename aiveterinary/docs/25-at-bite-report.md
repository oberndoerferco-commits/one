## Short answer

- **No Land puts a bite-reporting duty on private vets, doctors, keepers or the public.** Austria has nothing like Swiss TSchV art. 78.
- In every Land the **municipality** acts once it learns of a bite. The authority is the Gemeinde, or the Bürgermeister in Tirol and Vorarlberg. In Wien it is the Magistrat or, for § 8 para. 5–7, the Landespolizeidirektion. The municipality then declares the dog "auffällig", "bissig" or "gefährlich" and imposes measures.
- **Only two duties exist, and both fall on officials.**
  - **Tirol**: the Amtstierarzt must report a keeper whose dog he has assessed as auffällig. The police and the penal authority must report what they observe on duty about a dog injuring or endangering a person or an animal.
  - **Burgenland**: the police must report what they observe on duty about an auffällig dog.
- **OÖ** adds two narrow duties in its regulation. The examiner of the Alltagstauglichkeitsprüfung must report aggression shown during the exam to the Gemeinde. The keeper must volunteer past incidents to the vet who does the behavioural evaluation.
- **Federal law has one duty that reaches vets.** Under the Epidemiegesetz, a vet who learns in practice of a **person bitten by a rabid or rabies-suspected animal** must report it within **24 hours**. The report goes to the Bezirksverwaltungsbehörde (Gesundheitsamt) and gives the person's name, age and address.
  - Separately, the Tiergesundheitsgesetz 2024 obliges vets to report a suspected animal disease such as rabies.
  - TGG § 48 sets the post-bite rule for **rabies-suspected** dogs and cats. They are isolated under veterinary supervision. If the dog is vaccinated and its keeper is known, it is observed for at least 10 days, with a vet exam at the start and at the end.
  - This applies only on suspicion of rabies, not to every bite.
- **Salzburg and Burgenland** act on a **written notice backed by facts** ("mit bestimmten Tatsachen belegter schriftlicher Hinweis"). Anyone may send one, a vet included. No one has to. That written notice is the only place in Austrian law where a vet-written bite letter clearly fits.

## Österreichische Tierärztekammer (ÖTK)

- **Ordinationsrichtlinie** (published 5 Jun 2023, in force 6 Jun 2023) and **Berufsordnung, version 2026**: nothing on bites, aggressive dogs or reporting.
- **Leitlinien zur Impfung von Kleintieren** (as of May 2017), "Rechtliche Fragen zur Tollwutimpfung":
  - There is no legal duty to vaccinate dogs and cats against rabies.
  - Vets must report suspected rabies (it cites § 17 para. 1 **TSG**) to the Bürgermeister or the nearest police station, and also to the Bezirksverwaltungsbehörde.
  - Instead of killing, a vaccinated suspect dog or cat may be isolated and observed. The vaccination must be at least 4 weeks before the contact (Erlass BMGFJ-74700/0026-IV/B/6/2007).
  - **This text is out of date.** On 23 Oct 2026 a RIS title search for "Tierseuchengesetz" in consolidated federal law returned **no hits**. The rules now in force are TGG 2024 §§ 36 and 48 above (in force since 1 Jul 2024).
- Public site search on tieraerztekammer.at: "Hundebiss" gave 3 hits, "Bissverletzung" 4. All are press or news items (bite prevention, Sachkunde courses, Salzburg). None is a guideline or a reporting rule. The members' area was not accessible.

## What this means for the product

- In Austria a bite letter from the vet is **voluntary**. No Land and no federal act obliges a private vet to report an ordinary bite.
- The useful addressee is the **Gemeinde** (in Tirol and Vorarlberg the Bürgermeister; in Wien the Magistrat or LPD).
- In **Salzburg (§ 19 para. 3)** and **Burgenland (§ 22 para. 1)** the law names exactly the input a vet could give: a written notice backed by facts. In the other Länder the Gemeinde acts once facts "become known", so a notice works the same way in practice.
- **Confidentiality comes first.** TÄG § 29 binds the vet. A voluntary notice without the keeper's consent needs a legal basis. I did not check the exceptions to § 29. A lawyer should settle this before the product offers an AT notice.
- **The rabies path is separate.**
  - A person bitten by a rabid or rabies-suspected animal: Gesundheitsamt within 24 h (EpiG). The content is about the **person**.
  - A rabies-suspected animal: Bezirksverwaltungsbehörde (TGG § 36), then isolation and observation (TGG § 48).
- **Criteria the vet must not decide for the authority:**
  - NÖ: "schwer verletzt";
  - OÖ: "verletzt / wiederholt oder schwer verletzt";
  - NÖ, OÖ and Bgld: "ohne selbst angegriffen oder provoziert";
  - Wien: "bissig".
  
  Each is a legal test that the Gemeinde applies.
