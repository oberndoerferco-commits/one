# Win on proof, not on AI features

As of 18 October 2026, no single website is "the best AI veterinary website". The benchmark is a composite assembled from five leaders, each strong in one area. **Joii/Vet-AI** sets the bar for owner triage: a labelled AI triage chat, a human vet about four minutes away and registration numbers in the footer. **PetMD** sets the editorial bar with named DVM writers and reviewers, dates and an editorial policy. **Petleo** and **vetevo** set the records bar in the German-speaking (DACH) market. **Vets Now** shows how a free tool can feed a funnel. On the vet side, **CoVet, Provet, Curala and Scribenote** set the bar for documentation: ambient scribes with one-click write-back to the practice system, security attestations and published sub-processor lists. aiveterinary.org already beats all of them on several things nobody else offers: a microphone-free documentation assistant where every sentence is linked to its source, photo import of the paper EU passport, *libretto* or *Impfpass*, an owner-held record that crosses borders, and trilingual travel help. It trails on the one thing every leader has and it does not: **proof**. All 135 articles are unreviewed drafts. No vet uses the tool. The eval set has never been run. No structured data tells search engines who reviewed what. No triage tool or calculator gives owners a reason to arrive. The good news for a zero-budget project is that the market's weakest point is also proof. Only **2.1% of generative vet-AI vendors publish any accuracy metric** ([Brundage 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/veterinary-science/articles/10.3389/fvets.2026.1761038/full)). No owner-facing triage product has an independent validation. The EU's own Article 50 guidelines now reward named, genuine human review. aiveterinary can win by turning its honesty into published evidence. Ten of the eighteen improvements ranked at the end can be built now. The other eight need the founder, and the most important of them is recruiting reviewing vets.

## The composite leader combines five strengths no one site holds

The owner side of the market splits into products that use AI and products that people trust, and only one product does both well. Joii (Vet-AI, UK) runs a Gemini-based multi-agent triage system. It has a free symptom checker for "over 160 common conditions", "24/7 vet chat – replies in ≅ 4mins" and "Smart AI-powered triage", and states that "every consultation is handled by real, licensed vets" ([Joii](https://www.joiipetcare.com/)). Its footer shows its RCVS practice number and names vets with their RCVS numbers. The company says it keeps "60 vets online", runs models in "ghost mode" with medical sign-off before launch, and has every case reviewed weekly ([Diginomica](https://diginomica.com/how-vet-ai-deployed-ai-pet-healthcare-10-weeks-without-hallucinations); [VetClick](https://www.vetclick.com/news/vet-ais-proprietary-llm-outperforms-gemini-and-chatgpt-in-independent-veterinary-benchmarking-p11439.php)). Insurers distribute it for free: Waggel credits it with flagging **more than 4,700 potentially life-threatening emergencies** since July 2025 ([Waggel](https://www.waggel.co.uk/blog/post/ai-helps-fast-track-pet-emergencies)). Even Joii's evidence is vendor-run, though: its "81% clinical accuracy" comes from 48 simulated conversations it announced itself.

PetMD sets the editorial ceiling. Its pages carry a writer plus a DVM reviewer, a date, a table of contents and an FAQ, and it promises that new content is "written or reviewed by a veterinarian", with two editors and a medical review round ([PetMD editorial guidelines](https://www.petmd.com/editorial-guidelines)). Its weak spots matter for a challenger. It has **no statement on AI-generated content**, and on all three articles measured the visible "Updated" date is older than the date in its structured data ([PetMD](https://www.petmd.com/dog/general-health/dog-throwing-up-yellow-bile)). In continental Europe the records category belongs to Petleo, with "über 500.000 Tiere", DE/EN/IT and practice sync ([Petleo](https://vet.petleo.net/en/tierarztpraxen)), and to vetevo, with 4.8 stars from 5,434 ratings ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/de/app/id1414838326)). Both depend on manual entry or on a connected clinic. Neither imports the paper document. The free-tool funnel belongs to Vets Now, whose chocolate calculator sends low-risk results to a video consult and high-risk results to an emergency-clinic finder ([Vets Now](https://dog-chocolate-calculator.vets-now.com/)).

On the vet side, the leaders converge on a template. The product records the consultation and drafts a SOAP note. It writes the note back to the practice management system (PIMS), the practice software, with one click. It costs about **$99 per vet per month** with a free tier or trial (VetRec, CoVet and Scribenote pricing pages: [VetRec](https://www.vetrec.io/pricing), [CoVet](https://www.co.vet/pricing), [Scribenote](https://www.scribenote.com/pricing)). Trust pages carry SOC 2, ISO 27001 or, at Scribenote since September 2026, ISO/IEC 42001 ([Scribenote](https://scribenote.com/blog/scribenote-becomes-the-first-veterinary-software-company-certified-to-iso-iec-42001)). Provet publishes the market's best sub-processor list, naming its language-model and speech providers and where they run ([Provet](https://www.provet.com/legal/third-party-data-processors)). In DACH, Curala sells EU hosting, "Zero Retention" of audio and a permanent free tier of 10 consultations a month, then €35–85 per user per month ([Curala](https://www.curala.at/preise)).

## Owners want help deciding when to go, from someone with credentials

The demand is clear even though most of the data comes from vendor surveys. In April 2026, **43% of 2,000 US owners** had used AI tools for pet health. Yet 71% named vets as their most trusted source, and professional credentials were the top trust cue at **57%**, against 11% for AI-generated answers ([Digital Information World](https://www.digitalinformationworld.com/2026/09/the-growing-role-of-artificial-intelligence-in-pet-care-decisions.html), funded by a pet-food maker). In a second vendor survey, **70.6%** would use a pet health app only if licensed vets helped build it. "Sharing information with a vet" was the most wanted feature (40.1%), and 61% wished they had better symptom records for vet conversations ([WFTV/Stacker](https://www.wftv.com/news/survey-more-than-80/T2OQE5VEMI737ACAQ4VXSNFVBM/)). In the UK, 10% of owners now *routinely* use AI for pet advice, and the RSPCA calls the trend a possible "ticking time bomb" ([Vet Times](https://www.vettimes.com/news/business/digital/time-bomb-warning-issued-over-pet-owners-ai-use)). Owners do not want a vet replacement. They want help with the decision *before* the vet, and they want a named professional to stand behind it.

That decision is where general chatbots fail in a documented way. In a retrospective study of canine emergency cases, ChatGPT correctly prioritised **80–90% of critical cases but over-triaged about 60% of non-urgent ones** ([Wong et al., Vet Record](https://doi.org/10.1002/vetr.6126)). Owners notice over-triage and resent it. A Joii reviewer wrote: "Every single time the app says 'go see the vet'. It's pointless" ([Apple reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/gb/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1436958921/sortby=mostrecent/json)). A 2026 test of a public pet chatbot found both under- and over-triage, and safety warnings that were "not consistently provided" ([Chawla et al.](https://thesai.org/Downloads/Volume17No8/Paper_100-Evaluating_AI_Based_Veterinary_Symptom_Assessment_Chatbots.pdf)). In Germany the need is acute. FirstVet reviewers report waits of 48 hours or more, and insurers that require a video consult first ([Trustpilot](https://de.trustpilot.com/review/firstvet.com/location/90386934-05f1-46f5-84b9-e8ca16430e0a)). A calibrated, rule-based urgency guide with time windows ("go now", "call today", "watch for 24 hours and log it") fills the gap between a long tele-vet queue and an over-cautious chatbot. Building it from rules rather than a language model has two benefits. It keeps the site out of Article 50(1) chatbot disclosure. It also respects the FNOVI line that teletriage communicates no diagnosis ([FNOVI](https://mail.fnovi.it/sites/default/files/Telemedicina.pdf)). The trade-off is real, though: a rule tree covers far fewer presentations than Joii's 160 conditions. It should cover the common emergencies well before it tries to be broad.

Search results show the shape the content must take. In a DuckDuckGo snapshot (Google required JavaScript and could not be read), the top-ranking symptom pages run about 1,000–2,500 words, with a **median of about 1,520**. They are structured as causes, then what to do, then when to see a vet. About half promise triage in the title ("When to worry", "*quando preoccuparsi*", "*Notfallzeichen*"). Calculator pages took 3 of the top 10 places for "dog ate chocolate" ([DDG snapshot](https://html.duckduckgo.com/html/?q=dog+ate+chocolate&kl=us-en)). Italy is the softest market. Several Italian #1–#3 pages have no vet reviewer and no sources, and a 2019 article ranks #1 for "*gatto non mangia*" ([amoreaquattrozampe](https://www.amoreaquattrozampe.it/gatti/gatto-non-mangia-7-cause-possibili-rimedi/39915/)). aiveterinary has 58 Italian drafts. They become a real advantage once a *medico veterinario* signs them.

The technical rules changed in 2026, and leaders have not caught up. Google dropped the FAQ rich result on 7 May 2026 and says AI Overviews need "no special schema" ([Google changelog](https://developers.google.com/search/updates); [AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)). The markup that still matters is Article or MedicalWebPage with `author`, `reviewedBy` and `lastReviewed`, and it must match the visible text ([Google structured data policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies)). Only MSD, webvet and hundeo use MedicalWebPage. aiveterinary's built pages carry **no JSON-LD and no `x-default` hreflang**. Microsoft's guidance adds one design rule that affects the urgency banner directly: "Don't hide important answers in tabs or expandable menus" ([Microsoft](https://about.ads.microsoft.com/en/blog/post/october-2025/optimizing-your-content-for-inclusion-in-ai-search-answers)). Performance is already a strength. A built article is about 21 KB with one script, lighter than every leader measured.

The booklet's moat is regulatory, and it will last. Blank EU passports are paper-only from 22 April 2026 ([Reg. 2026/132](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2026/132/oj/eng)). The new welfare and traceability regulation brings national databases only from 2030 and an EU index from 2031, and those cover identity, not vaccination history ([Reg. 2026/1818](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1818/oj/eng)). A German veterinary chamber's guidance (2020, under the old rules) told vets to refuse to copy third-party vaccinations into a replacement passport ([TÄK Westfalen-Lippe](https://www.tieraerztekammer-wl.de/fileadmin/user_upload/tak/02_Fuer_Tieraerzte/02.1_Aktuelles/02.1.1_Archive/02.1.1_Archiv_Fuer_Tieraerzte_Aktuelles_2020/Anlage_2_zum_Erlass_Heimtierausweis.pdf)). So a dated photo archive of every passport page, plus a guide to rebuilding proof, meets a real need. It must be framed as a companion to the paper passport, never a replacement. That pain point rests on regulation, not observed complaints: no forum threads about lost passports were found.

## Text-first documentation is uncontested; the missing piece is evidence

No vendor in the US, the UK, DACH or Italy offers microphone-free documentation as its main mode. Petleo.ai, Curala, ReportAssistant/intelliVET, Provet and Tandem all start from audio ([vet tools notes; Curala](https://www.curala.at/ki-dokumentation-tierarztpraxis); [Petleo](https://vet.petleo.net/en/petleo-ai)). The gaps in coverage are large. Provet's scribe supports Italian but **not German** ([Provet](https://www.provet.com/product/provet-ai)). Tandem's Vetera, easyVET and dr.veto integrations are "Not Live" ([Tandem](https://tandemhealth.ai/product/integrations/vetera)). Italy's largest local PIMS, Smart Vet, has no AI ([Smart Vet](https://www.smartvet.it/)). Having no microphone is a selling point under DACH law, not just a design choice. Recording the spoken word without consent is a criminal offence under § 201 of the German criminal code ([§ 201 StGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stgb/__201.html)) and Article 179ter of the Swiss one ([Fedlex](https://fedlex.data.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/54/757_781_799/20261001/de/html/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-54-757_781_799-20261001-de-html.html)). Competitors and clinics therefore run QR-code consent steps and reception forms ([Vetline](https://www.vetline.de/eine-ki-die-mitschreibt-wie-gross-ist-die-zeitersparnis)).

The design also answers the failures users complain about. A two-star ScribbleVet review lists drug names "I've never heard of or used", vaccines "inappropriate… for the species" and notes about "unrelated pets" ([Apple reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6461720107/sortBy=mostRecent/json)). In human medicine, an August 2026 audit of three commercial ambient scribes found verified failures in **31.3% of notes**, many of them in notes clinicians had already signed ([Fox et al., preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31017)). A tool that writes nothing the vet did not type, and links each sentence to its source, rules out invented content and other-pet content by construction. Its missing-information flags go after omissions, which reviewers catch least well. The claim "adds nothing" is not unique on its own: Curala already says it is "*ausdrücklich darauf ausgelegt, nichts zu ergänzen*". What no vendor offers is visible source links per sentence, and flags for fields the law requires.

Those flags are where the country packs earn their place. The German veterinary dispensing regulation (TÄHAV) has applied in its new version since 1 January 2025. It requires electronic records to stay "*unveränderlich*" and sets specific prescription and antibiogram documentation ([TÄHAV § 16](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/t_hav_2025/__16.html); [§ 13](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/t_hav_2025/__13.html)). The German fee schedule (GOT) requires each invoice to state the diagnosis or reason for consultation and the item numbers ([GOT § 7](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/got_2022/__7.html)). German trade press already tells practices to put "*Pflichtfelder für Wirkstoff, Dosis und Dauer*" in their templates. No AI vendor advertises checking them.

What the tool lacks is everything that makes a buyer believe it. German trade press frames the buying decision as a seven-point checklist that ends in a **20-case stopwatch test** ([Vetline](https://www.vetline.de/eine-ki-die-mitschreibt-wie-gross-ist-die-zeitersparnis)). Reviewers praise write-back integration and pan copy-paste. Provet's owner, Nordhealth, describes standalone scribes as "Feature, not platform… must plug into the PMS" ([Nordhealth](https://mb.cision.com/Public/20636/4384407/971605e4f2a0e9e0.pdf)). The tool's price is not the problem: free tiers exist at Scribenote, Heidi, Tandem, Covetrus and Curala. The problems are that its evaluation set has never been run, no vet has used it, and its output reaches a PIMS only as pasted text.

## The 2026 rules reward the transparency aiveterinary already practises

Three rule changes in 2026 turn honesty into an asset. First, the Commission's final Article 50 guidelines (20 July 2026) explain the exemption from labelling AI-assisted public-interest text. Human review must be a "deliberate examination of the substance", and "fact-checking the accuracy of the content is a minimum requirement". "Any substantive AI intervention" after sign-off voids the exemption. The editorially responsible person must be publicly named ([Guidelines C(2026) 5054](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131215), paras 134–138). Pet health touches "public health" and "consumer safety", which the guidelines name as public-interest topics. Until a vet reviews them, aiveterinary's drafts must stay labelled, as they are today. After review, the site needs a verifiable review log and a technical guarantee that no AI edits follow sign-off. Second, the German Federal Veterinary Chamber (BTK) now tells vets to inform owners about AI-supported content. It also says AI-generated content in vet reports must be "*eindeutig als solche gekennzeichnet*" ([BTK leaflet](https://www.bundestieraerztekammer.de/d.php?id=9946)). That argues for keeping an "AI-assisted" note on German articles even after review. Third, two Italian measures apply to vets. Article 13 of Italy's AI law (Law 132/2025) requires professionals to tell clients about AI use "*con linguaggio chiaro, semplice ed esaustivo*" (reported via [Assolombarda](https://www.assolombarda.it/servizi/diritto-di-impresa/informazioni/intelligenza-artificiale-art-50-ai-act-e-art-13-legge-132-2025-a-confronto); official text not opened). D.Lgs. 179/2026, in force from 23 October 2026, makes AI training part of professional orders' continuing education ([GU PDF](https://storagehub.homnya.net/cmsimage/2026/10/20261008_234.pdf)).

On the vet tool, the open legal question is Article 50(2) machine-readable marking. It applies from 2 August 2026, with a grace period to **2 December 2026** for systems already on the market. The guidelines exempt systems that "extract and structure data, but do not summarise it", and a narrow internal-documentation case. That exemption likely fails for owner summaries and condolence letters that leave the clinic ([Guidelines](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/131215), paras 65, 87, 92). Embedding provenance metadata in every export is a cheap hedge, and a lawyer should confirm the classification. No competitor answers AI Act questions in public. CoVet's trust centre lists "Does CoVet follow the EU AI Act?" with the answer hidden ([CoVet Trust Center](https://trust.mycroft.io/covet)). aiveterinary's existing public AI Act section is a lead it should press home.

One caution cuts the other way. A local-first booklet avoids most GDPR exposure, and planned Supabase accounts bring it back: controller duties, a processor contract (DPA), possibly a data protection impact assessment (DPIA), and EU-region hosting. The EU–US Data Privacy Framework survives as of October 2026, but its appeal (C-703/25 P) is pending ([Gawel Legal](https://gawel.ch/insights/64-transatlantic-data-transfers-dpf/)). Owner accounts with paid features could also bring the site into the European Accessibility Act's scope, which currently exempts microenterprises that provide services ([Directive 2019/882](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj/eng)). Accounts should stay optional, and sync should be opt-in.

Two items were left out on purpose. A third-party attestation (SOC 2, ISO 27001 or ISO 42001) is table stakes for US scribes, but it costs time and money that pilot evidence would earn back faster. It belongs after F3. An owner-facing LLM chat would trigger Article 50(1) disclosure and RCVS-style scrutiny. It would also invite exactly the over-triage owners complain about, so it should wait until the rule-based guide and a vet panel exist.

## Conclusion

The market has split along a line that favours aiveterinary. Well-funded leaders compete on capture (ambient audio, integrations, insurer distribution), where a zero-budget site cannot follow. They leave verification almost empty: named review, published method, source-linked output and honest AI labels. The EU's 2026 rules move value toward that empty space. Under the Article 50 guidelines, named, fact-checking human review is what lifts the duty to label AI-assisted content. The BTK and Italy's Article 13 require client-facing AI disclosure. The site's existing design choices (labelled drafts, no microphone, nothing the vet did not write, a public AI Act page) already match that direction. What is missing is the evidence that turns those choices into claims a vet or an owner can check.

That changes the order of work. The highest-leverage engineering is plumbing that makes review verifiable: hashes, JSON-LD and a published eval protocol. Features come after. The highest-leverage founder action is recruiting a handful of registered vets, because every other trust signal in this market depends on it. Two uncertainties remain. Owner-demand figures come mostly from vendor-funded US surveys, and Italy has no recent data at all. No Google results or AI Overview citations could be observed. A first reviewed Italian cluster plus Search Console data would test the "soft Italian market" thesis within weeks, more cheaply than any further desk research.
