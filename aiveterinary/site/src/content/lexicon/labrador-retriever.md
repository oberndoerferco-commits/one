## How long do Labradors live?

About 12 to 13 years, depending on the study. A 2024 UK study of more than 43,000 Labrador Retrievers found a median lifespan of 13.1 years (McMillan et al.). UK vet records give slightly lower figures: in VetCompass data, the median age at death was 12.0 years (McGreevy et al.), and VetCompass life tables put a Labrador's life expectancy at birth at 11.77 years (Teng et al.). The Royal Kennel Club gives the breed's lifespan as over 10 years.

Coat colour may matter. In the VetCompass study, chocolate Labradors lived a median 10.7 years, against 12.1 years for black and yellow dogs, and had more ear infections: 23.4% of chocolate dogs, against 12.8% of black and 17.0% of yellow ones.

The most common causes of death in that study were musculoskeletal disorders and cancer.

## How big does a Labrador get?

The FCI breed standard gives an ideal height at the withers of 56–57 cm for males and 54–56 cm for females, and describes a strongly built, very active dog, "which precludes excessive body weight". Adult Labradors under UK vet care weighed 33.0 kg on average: 35.2 kg for males and 30.4 kg for females.

## What health problems do Labradors have?

In UK vet records of 2,074 Labradors, the most common problems were (McGreevy et al.):

- **Ear infections** (otitis externa): 10.4%
- **Overweight or obesity**: 8.8%. Neutered males were more often overweight than entire males (11.4% against 4.1%).
- **Degenerative joint disease** (arthritis): 5.5%

**Elbow dysplasia** is a known inherited problem: the UFAW genetic welfare database estimates that 13–21% of Labradors have a fragmented coronoid process, a fault in the elbow joint that leads to arthritis, often starting in the first year, and that can cause lifelong pain.

Overweight is one of the breed's most common problems, and the breed standard itself rules out excessive body weight. If your dog limps or is stiff, see [limping in dogs](/animals/dog-limping); for ear trouble, see [ear infections in dogs](/animals/dog-ear-infection).

## Which health tests matter for a Labrador puppy?

The Royal Kennel Club recommends that, before breeding, both parents have their hips and elbows scored under the BVA/RKC schemes, an eye examination, and DNA tests for several inherited diseases, among them centronuclear myopathy, exercise-induced collapse, macular corneal dystrophy, progressive retinal atrophy (prcd-PRA), skeletal dysplasia (SD2) and Stargardt disease. Ask to see the parents' results, and look them up in the Royal Kennel Club's Health Test Results Finder.

## How much exercise does a Labrador need?

A lot. The Royal Kennel Club lists the Labrador as a large breed that needs more than 2 hours of exercise a day and is best suited to a large house with a large garden. The FCI standard describes it as a keen lover of water.

## Is a Labrador right for me?

The breed standard describes a good-tempered, adaptable dog and a devoted companion, "intelligent, keen and biddable, with a strong will to please". That only tells you what breeders aim for: every dog is an individual. Plan for a lot of daily exercise, weekly grooming of a shedding coat, careful feeding to prevent weight gain, and a check of the parents' health tests.
