## How long do Maltese live?

Often a long time. A 2024 UK study of 2,687 Maltese found a median lifespan of 13.1 years; this did not differ statistically from crossbred dogs (12.0 years) (McMillan et al.). The Royal Kennel Club and the PDSA give the breed's lifespan as over 12 years.

## How big does a Maltese get?

Very small. The FCI breed standard gives a height at the withers of 21 to 25 cm for males and 20 to 23 cm for females, and a weight of 3 to 4 kg. The body is about a third longer than the dog is tall.

## How do you care for a Maltese coat?

The FCI standard describes a dense, glossy, silky coat that is very long and straight, falling to the ground like a cape, with no undercoat. The colour is pure white; a pale ivory tinge is permitted. The Royal Kennel Club says the coat does not shed but needs grooming every day.

## What health problems do Maltese have?

**Heart:** the UFAW genetic welfare database says Maltese are predisposed to patent ductus arteriosus (PDA), the most common inherited heart defect of dogs. Before birth a blood vessel lets blood bypass the lungs; it should close at birth. If it stays open, the left side of the heart is overloaded and heart failure can follow, with tiredness, coughing, fast breathing and collapse. Some puppies show signs as young as eight weeks. PDA is usually treatable, and in most cases curable, if it is found and treated early, so ask your vet to listen to your puppy's heart at its first check.

**Other conditions** listed by the PDSA include slipping kneecaps (luxating patellas), hydrocephalus (fluid on the brain), portosystemic shunt (an abnormal blood vessel that bypasses the liver), dental problems, heart disease, Chiari malformation and syringomyelia (a painful condition with fluid-filled cavities in the spinal cord), cataracts and entropion (inward-turning eyelids). Small dogs that skip or hold up a back leg may have a slipping kneecap: see [limping in dogs](/animals/dog-limping).

## Which health tests matter for a Maltese puppy?

The Royal Kennel Club lists eye testing under the BVA/KC/ISDS Eye Scheme as best practice before breeding. The PDSA mentions the BVA/Kennel Club scheme for Chiari malformation and syringomyelia. Ask about the parents' eye results and medical history.

## Is a Maltese right for me?

The FCI standard describes a lively, affectionate, very calm and very intelligent dog. That only tells you what breeders aim for. The PDSA says Maltese can be reserved with strangers, so early socialisation matters. The Royal Kennel Club gives up to 30 minutes of exercise a day and says the breed can live in a flat, but plan for grooming every day.
