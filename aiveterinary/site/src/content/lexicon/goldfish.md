## How long do goldfish live?

Much longer than most people think: the RSPCA says goldfish are often believed to live about five years, but they can live for up to 25 years.

## How big do goldfish get?

Far bigger than the 12 cm many people expect: the RSPCA says some goldfish reach over 30 cm. Common goldfish are the hardiest; many fancy varieties are more delicate because of selective breeding (RSPCA).

## Can goldfish live in a bowl?

No. The RSPCA says each goldfish needs at least 60 litres of water, because small volumes don't provide a stable environment, and that no fish should be kept without a well set-up, maintained filter.

- **Germany:** the veterinary animal-welfare association TVT points out that the German agriculture ministry's expert report on keeping ornamental fish names 54 litres as the minimum for keeping fish, because water quality stays reasonably stable from that size. The TVT says no fish species *has* to be kept in a tank smaller than that.
- **Italy:** some towns ban bowls outright. Verona's animal-welfare regulation bans round tanks and tanks with curved walls, and sets a minimum of 30 litres.
- **Bigger is better.** The RSPCA advises only buying a tank larger than about 40 litres (45 × 30 × 30 cm), and allowing 2 litres of water for every 0.5 cm of goldfish length.

## What temperature do goldfish need?

Goldfish are cold-water fish: the RSPCA says to keep them within 10–21 °C, so a heater isn't normally necessary. The MSD Veterinary Manual says goldfish do fine in water at room temperature.

Don't put the tank by a window: the RSPCA warns that sun can raise the water temperature dangerously high. Use a thermometer and check it every day (RSPCA).

## Do goldfish need a friend?

Not necessarily: the RSPCA says goldfish don't need the company of other fish and can be kept on their own. If you keep more than one, the RSPCA advises not mixing single-tailed and twin-tailed varieties, because the slower twin-tailed fish often miss out on food. Each extra fish needs its own space.

## What do goldfish eat?

Not tropical fish food: the RSPCA says goldfish love chopped vegetables such as peas and spinach, and shouldn't be fed tropical fish flakes. Give protein-rich foods such as brine shrimp or daphnia only in small amounts.

The RSPCA recommends feeding two or three times a day, only as much as the fish can eat in two to five minutes, and says it is better to underfeed than overfeed, because poor water quality harms fish more than a shortage of food. It doesn't recommend holiday "feeding blocks".

## How do I set up a goldfish tank?

Set up and run the filter before the fish arrive. The RSPCA strongly advises "fishless cycling", which takes at least two weeks, testing the water every other day; after that, change about 30 % of the water every week.

The MSD Veterinary Manual advises testing pH, ammonia, nitrite and nitrate with a test kit every two weeks, and quarantining valuable new fish for at least 30 to 60 days before they join the others.

## How do I know my goldfish is healthy?

A healthy fish eats quickly and eagerly, breathes with rhythmic gill movements, swims freely and evenly, and has bright, clear eyes, smooth scales and fins held away from the body (RSPCA).

Warning signs listed by the RSPCA include a sunken or swollen belly, scales that stick out, pale patches, tiny white spots on the fins, gills or skin, a milky eye, a fish sinking to the bottom or swimming to one side, and constant gulping at the surface (a sign of oxygen shortage). The MSD Veterinary Manual adds changes in swimming, lethargy, not eating, slow or fast breathing, ulcers and weight loss.

## When should a goldfish see a vet?

When your fish shows signs of illness that good water doesn't fix. The RSPCA says most fish diseases are caused by poor water quality, so test the water first, and contact a specialist fish vet for help with diagnosis and treatment.

Yes, there are vets for fish. The MSD Veterinary Manual recommends working with a knowledgeable fish vet, notes that more complicated diseases need veterinary treatment, and that some treatments, such as injections or surgery, can only be done by a vet. Ask your practice whether it treats fish, or who it refers to.

## Can I release my goldfish into a pond or river?

No. Goldfish are native to East Asia, and released or escaped pet goldfish now form non-native populations on every continent except Antarctica, according to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, which rates their risk as "high".

The same assessment says goldfish change habitats through their feeding and movement, may eat the eggs of native fish, can bring parasites into new waters and can carry several fish viruses listed by the World Organisation for Animal Health. The RSPCA also advises against releasing fish into outdoor ponds, because they can harm wildlife and spread disease.

## Rules in the EU, Italy and Germany

**EU:** the sources we opened name no EU-wide rule just for keeping goldfish; the national rules on releasing animals below apply.

**Italy:** according to ISPRA, the Italian environment agency, DPR 102/2019 confirms the ban on releasing non-native species and populations into the wild (article 12, paragraph 3 of DPR 357/97); exceptions need an authorisation from the environment ministry (MASE). Town rules can go further: Verona's animal-welfare regulation bans round or curved-walled tanks, sets a minimum of 30 litres, and bans giving animals away as prizes or gifts in public places and at fairs.

**Germany:** under § 40 of the Federal Nature Conservation Act (BNatSchG), releasing animals into the wild needs permission from the competent authority. The 54-litre figure above comes from the federal expert report that the TVT quotes; it is guidance, not a law.
