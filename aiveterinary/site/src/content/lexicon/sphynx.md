## How long do Sphynx cats live?

The evidence is thin, and it points two ways. VetCompass life tables for cats that died in the UK in 2019 gave the Sphynx a life expectancy at birth of only 6.68 years, the shortest of the 12 breeds analysed, against 11.89 years for crossbred cats (Teng et al.). That figure rests on just 18 deaths, and the authors say there is scarce peer-reviewed information on how long Sphynx cats live and that more research is needed. The GCCF, the UK's main cat registry, gives a lifespan of 15 years or more.

## How big does a Sphynx cat get?

Medium-sized, with surprising weight for its size (GCCF). Females weigh 6 to 8 lb (about 2.7–3.6 kg), males 8 to 11 lb (about 3.6–5.0 kg); males are generally about 25% larger.

## How do I care for a Sphynx's skin?

The Sphynx is not completely naked: the body is covered with a fine down, said to feel like peach skin or chamois, with some hair on the nose, feet, tail and ears. It has no whiskers or eyelashes (International Cat Care). Kittens are very wrinkly and keep some wrinkles, especially on the head (GCCF).

Without fur, the skin's natural oils are not absorbed, so they build up on the skin. The GCCF and the PDSA advise:

- **bathing regularly** to remove the oils; most Sphynx seem to enjoy it, the GCCF says
- **cleaning between the wrinkles**, where dirt and oil collect (PDSA)
- **cleaning the ears and feet**, which get dirty with wax; use a cotton bud only on the outer ear, never inside the ear canal (GCCF)

International Cat Care reports that the breed is prone to skin problems, including more Malassezia yeast on the skin, ear infections and two rarer skin diseases (figurate erythema and maculopapular cutaneous mastocytosis); the PDSA lists urticaria pigmentosa, a skin condition with crusty sores. If the skin becomes red, crusty, greasy-smelling or itchy, see your vet.

## Do Sphynx cats get cold or sunburnt?

They are not delicate, but they need some care with temperature and sun.

**Cold:** a Sphynx feels warmer to the touch than other cats, but its body temperature is no higher (GCCF). The GCCF says, contrary to popular belief, it does not need extra heating, which would only make it sweat more; an ordinary cat bed with a blanket it can wrap up in is enough, and Sphynx will find a blanket or a warm body to curl up with when it gets cold. Because their bodies work hard to keep warm, they usually need slightly more food than other breeds; ask your vet how much.

**Sun:** any cat can get sunburnt, but cats with white, thin or no hair are at higher risk and can burn severely; repeated burns can lead to skin cancer (International Cat Care). The GCCF and the PDSA advise keeping Sphynx as indoor cats, going out only under supervision, ideally on a harness in an enclosed garden or a run. If you use sunscreen, International Cat Care says to use only one made for pets and checked as safe for cats. Severe sunburn, with red, sore skin, needs a vet.

## What health problems do Sphynx cats have?

**Heart:** International Cat Care says hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM), a thickening of the heart wall, is strongly suspected to be inherited in the breed, and that other heart defects such as mitral valve dysplasia also occur. The GCCF says HCM affects young to middle-aged Sphynx and is often found at a routine health check. Teng et al. cite a French screening study in which 20.2% of Sphynx cats had HCM and 14.0% had a heart defect from birth. Ask for the parents' heart scan results.

**Muscles:** inherited myopathy causes muscle weakness that starts between 3 and 23 weeks of age, a head that cannot be held up properly, head bobbing and tiring easily. Affected cats may have trouble swallowing and can choke on food. With good care they can live a normal lifespan, but the risk of choking remains (UFAW).

**Other problems** International Cat Care lists are a movement disorder (paroxysmal dyskinesia), eye sequestrum (a dark patch on the cornea), uterine infections and urate bladder stones.

A Sphynx is not hypoallergenic: cat allergies usually come from a protein in saliva, not the fur (GCCF, International Cat Care).

## Is a Sphynx cat right for me?

The GCCF describes the Sphynx as affectionate, sociable and intelligent, sweet-tempered and easy to handle, a cat that hates being alone and should have feline company, ideally another Sphynx. Plan for regular baths, ear and skin care, protection from the sun and regular heart checks.

Think about the coat, too. International Cat Care's position is that "for a cat, a coat is essential" and that hairless cats should not be bred.

## Rules in Germany

German law bans breeding vertebrates when it is to be expected that the offspring will suffer pain, suffering or harm because inherited body parts or organs are missing, unsuitable or altered (§ 11b Tierschutzgesetz). The law describes the harm and does not list breeds.
