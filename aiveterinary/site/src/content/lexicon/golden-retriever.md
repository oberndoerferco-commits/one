## How long do Golden Retrievers live?

A 2024 UK study of more than 11,500 Golden Retrievers found a median lifespan of 13.2 years, longer than the 12.0 years of crossbred dogs in the same study (McMillan et al.). The Royal Kennel Club gives the breed's lifespan as over 10 years.

## How big does a Golden Retriever get?

The FCI breed standard gives a height at the withers of 56–61 cm for males and 51–56 cm for females. It describes a symmetrical, balanced, active and powerful dog with a kindly expression; it sets no weight.

## What health problems do Golden Retrievers have?

The UFAW genetic welfare database lists these among the breed's inherited problems:

- **Hip dysplasia:** the hip joint of the growing puppy develops loosely and later turns into painful arthritis. It is the most common joint problem of large dogs. Figures for Golden Retrievers vary between studies; one US study found it in up to 73%. Signs include lameness, stiffness, an abnormal gait and reluctance to get up or to run and play.
- **Atopic dermatitis:** an allergic skin disease, common in Golden Retrievers, that causes mild to severe itching. Scratching damages the skin further and leads to infections. Signs usually start before 3 years of age and last for life, with ups and downs; treatment is lifelong.

The database also lists elbow problems (humeral condylar osteochondrosis) and an underactive thyroid (hypothyroidism) for the breed. For an itchy dog, see [itching in dogs](/animals/dog-itching); for a dog that limps, see [limping in dogs](/animals/dog-limping).

## Which health tests matter for a Golden Retriever puppy?

The Royal Kennel Club recommends, as a minimum before breeding, hip and elbow scoring under the BVA/RKC schemes, an eye examination including gonioscopy for pectinate ligament abnormality (PLA), and a DNA test for progressive retinal atrophy (GR_PRA2). Best practice adds DNA tests for ichthyosis (ICT-A) and a second form of progressive retinal atrophy (GR_PRA1). Ask to see both parents' results.

## Is a Golden Retriever right for me?

The FCI standard describes a biddable, intelligent dog with natural working ability, kindly, friendly and confident. The Royal Kennel Club lists it as a large breed that needs more than 2 hours of exercise a day, grooming more than once a week for a medium-length, shedding coat, and a large house with a large garden. Every dog is an individual, so meet the dog and, if you can, its parents.
