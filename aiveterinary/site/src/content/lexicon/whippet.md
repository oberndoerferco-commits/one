## How long do Whippets live?

Longer than most dogs. A 2024 UK study that included 4,971 Whippets found a median lifespan of 13.4 years, against 12.0 years for crossbred dogs (McMillan et al., supplementary data). The Royal Kennel Club and the PDSA give the breed's lifespan as over 12 years.

## How big does a Whippet get?

Medium-sized and light. The FCI breed standard gives a desirable height at the withers of 47 to 51 cm for males and 44 to 47 cm for females, and describes a balance of muscular power and strength with elegance and grace, "built for speed and work", with no exaggeration. It gives no weight; the PDSA gives a typical weight of 11 to 18 kg. The coat is fine, short and close and can be any colour except merle.

## What is a Whippet like?

The FCI standard calls the Whippet "an ideal companion", highly adaptable in domestic and sporting surroundings, with a gentle, affectionate, even disposition. That is the breeding goal, not a promise for every dog. The Royal Kennel Club says Whippets enjoy the comforts of domestic life. The PDSA adds that they form strong bonds, dislike being left alone and can suffer from separation anxiety, so it recommends a Whippet only where someone is around most of the day. Whippets have a high prey drive: the PDSA advises a tight grip on the lead outside secure areas, a secure garden, and not keeping them with smaller pets.

## How much exercise does a Whippet need?

The Royal Kennel Club gives up to an hour a day. The PDSA says at least an hour, split into a shorter morning walk and a longer one later, with plenty of chances to run off lead in a secure area, because a really good run is in their nature. Whippets can reach about 35 mph (PDSA).

## What health problems do Whippets have?

**Skin and injuries:** the PDSA describes Whippets as beautiful but quite delicate. Their skin is easily cut on thorns, twigs and other sharp objects, and they can injure bones when running on uneven ground, so check off-lead areas for hazards. For lameness after a run see [limping in dogs](/animals/dog-limping).

**Eyes, heart and immune system:** the PDSA lists progressive retinal atrophy, a gradual loss of sight over months or years (it advises that parents are screened), mitral valve disease, a weakening of a heart valve, and immune-mediated problems, where the body reacts against its own cells.

**"Bully" Whippets:** a US study found a variant of the myostatin gene, which normally limits muscle growth, in Whippets. Dogs with one copy were more muscular and among the fastest in racing; dogs with two copies were grossly over-muscled, the so-called bully whippet. The authors note that little is known about the health effects of carrying the variant (Mosher et al.).

## Which health tests matter for a Whippet puppy?

The Royal Kennel Club lists prioritising genetic diversity as good practice before breeding and does not list specific screening schemes or DNA tests; the breed has a breed club health scheme. The PDSA advises screening parents for progressive retinal atrophy. Ask the breeder what tests the parents have had.

## Is a Whippet right for me?

Plan for a gentle, affectionate dog that wants company most of the day, an hour of exercise with safe off-lead running, and weekly grooming of a short coat that sheds. Their thin skin is easily cut, so check them after runs and keep rough play with other dogs in check.
