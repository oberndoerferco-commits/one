## How long do Maine Coons live?

The GCCF, the UK's main cat registry, gives a life expectancy of 10 to 15 years. UK vet records give lower averages: VetCompass life tables for cats that died in 2019 put a Maine Coon's life expectancy at birth at 9.71 years, against 11.89 years for crossbred cats (Teng et al.). An earlier VetCompass study, with only 14 Maine Coons, found a median age at death of 11.0 years and counted the breed among those with reduced longevity (O'Neill et al.).

## How big does a Maine Coon get?

Big. It is the largest domestic breed recognised by the GCCF, with a long, rectangular body, a broad chest and large, tufted paws. The GCCF gives a size of 10–20 lb (about 4.5–9 kg); males can weigh up to 18–20 lb. Maine Coons are slow to mature and can take up to four years to reach full size.

## What health problems do Maine Coons have?

The GCCF calls the Maine Coon a generally healthy breed affected by two hereditary health issues:

- **Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM):** the heart muscle thickens, so the heart pumps less blood with each beat, which can lead to heart failure. According to the UFAW genetic welfare database, around 34% of UK Maine Coons carry the gene variant that causes it. Cats with HCM but without heart failure usually look normal; a vet may hear a murmur or an abnormal rhythm. Heart failure makes breathing difficult, and blood clots that block a blood vessel cause severe pain.
- **Hip dysplasia:** there is no simple gene test, but breeding cats can be X-rayed to check the hips.

International Cat Care also lists spinal muscular atrophy in Maine Coons among the inherited disorders of cats.

Most cats do not show signs of heart disease until it is advanced, says International Cat Care. The most common sign of heart failure is difficult or rapid breathing; a blood clot (feline aortic thromboembolism) causes sudden paralysis of one or both back legs with severe pain. Contact your vet if you see these signs, and keep up regular check-ups, which can find problems before signs develop.

## Which tests matter for a Maine Coon kitten?

There is a DNA test for HCM in Maine Coons, and cats used for breeding can have an ultrasound scan of the heart (GCCF). International Cat Care recommends that cats with HCM on a heart scan, and cats with two copies of the gene variant, are not bred from, and that breeding cats have a heart scan every year. For Maine Coons imported onto its active (breeding) register, the GCCF requires eight DNA tests, HCM among them, with the swab and microchip checked by a vet. Ask to see the parents' results.

## Is a Maine Coon right for me?

The GCCF describes the Maine Coon as a "gentle giant": affectionate, playful and high-energy, needing plenty of room and a very strong climbing frame. Its long, thick coat needs regular grooming to prevent knots and mats, with heavy shedding in spring, and its large ears should be checked regularly. Watch its weight: the breed can tend to become overweight.
