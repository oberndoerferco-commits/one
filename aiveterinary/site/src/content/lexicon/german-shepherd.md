## How long do German Shepherds live?

About 10 to 11 years. A 2024 UK study of nearly 20,000 German Shepherds found a median lifespan of 11.3 years (McMillan et al.). In UK vet records (VetCompass), the median was 10.3 years, and the most common causes of death were musculoskeletal disorders (16.3%) and being unable to stand (14.9%) (O'Neill et al.). The Royal Kennel Club gives the breed's lifespan as over 10 years.

## How big does a German Shepherd get?

The FCI breed standard sets a height at the withers of 60–65 cm and a weight of 30–40 kg for males, and 55–60 cm and 22–32 kg for females. In UK vet records, adult males had a median weight of 40.1 kg and females 34.8 kg.

## What health problems do German Shepherds have?

In UK vet records, the most common problems were ear infections (7.9%), osteoarthritis (5.5%), diarrhoea (5.2%), overweight or obesity (5.2%) and aggression (4.8%); aggression was more common in males (O'Neill et al.).

The UFAW genetic welfare database lists several inherited problems:

- **Hip dysplasia:** the hip joint develops loosely and turns into painful arthritis. Studies report it in 18–49% of German Shepherds, and the breed has a higher risk than Dobermanns, Labradors and Rottweilers.
- **Degenerative myelopathy:** a slow, incurable disease of the spinal cord. It is not painful, but it leads to swaying, weakness and paralysis of the hind legs, usually from about 8 to 9 years of age. Most owners choose euthanasia within a year of diagnosis.
- **Anal furunculosis:** long-lasting inflammation and ulcers around the anus that make passing faeces painful; 84% of cases are seen in German Shepherds.

The RSPCA also names German Shepherds among the breeds bred with sloped backs, which it links to mobility problems, joint problems and chronic pain.

If your dog limps or struggles to get up, see [limping in dogs](/animals/dog-limping) and talk to your vet early.

## Which health tests matter for a German Shepherd puppy?

The Royal Kennel Club recommends hip and elbow scoring under the BVA/RKC schemes for both parents before breeding, plus an eye test and a DNA test for degenerative myelopathy. Ask to see the parents' results.

## Is a German Shepherd right for me?

The FCI standard describes a well-balanced, self-assured dog that is good-natured (unless provoked), attentive and willing to please, bred as a companion, guard, protection, service and herding dog. The Royal Kennel Club lists it as a large breed that needs more than 2 hours of exercise a day, grooming more than once a week and a large house with a large garden. Each dog is an individual, so meet the dog and, if you can, its parents.
