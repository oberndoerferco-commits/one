## How long do Staffordshire Bull Terriers live?

About as long as the average dog. A 2024 UK study that included 36,216 Staffordshire Bull Terriers found a median lifespan of 12.0 years, the same as for crossbred dogs (McMillan et al., supplementary data). VetCompass life tables, built from UK vet records of 2,364 Staffies, gave a life expectancy at birth of 11.33 years (Teng et al.). The Royal Kennel Club gives the breed's lifespan as over 12 years.

## How big does a Staffordshire Bull Terrier get?

Small to medium, but heavy for its size. The FCI breed standard describes a smooth-coated, muscular, active and agile dog "of great strength for his size", with a desirable height at the withers of 35.5 to 40.5 cm. It gives a weight of 12.7 to 17 kg for males and 11 to 15.4 kg for females.

## What is a Staffie like?

The FCI standard describes a dog "traditionally of indomitable courage and tenacity", highly intelligent and affectionate, especially with children, bold and fearless. The standard lists aggressive or overly shy dogs as disqualifying faults. That tells you what breeders aim for, not how every dog will be.

The PDSA describes lively, energetic, very people-focused dogs that do best when someone is home most of the time; Staffies are known to suffer from separation anxiety and to chew when bored. They have a strong prey drive, so take care with cats and small pets, and they can be strong-willed, so reward-based training and early socialisation help.

## How much exercise does a Staffie need?

The Royal Kennel Club gives up to an hour a day. The PDSA says at least an hour, split into a few walks with time to run off lead in a secure area, plus play and training for the mind. A secure garden matters: Staffies can dig and jump.

## What health problems do Staffordshire Bull Terriers have?

**Mast cell tumours:** this is a cancer of immune cells, usually in the skin. The UFAW genetic welfare database says Staffordshire Bull Terriers are predisposed. The tumours look very variable and can be mistaken for harmless lumps; they range from benign to malignant. The mean age at diagnosis is around 8 years, but they can appear at any age, even in puppies, and there is no test to predict which dogs will be affected. In a VetCompass study of 168,636 dogs in England, Staffies had 4.2 times the odds of a mast cell tumour diagnosis compared with crossbred dogs (Shoop et al.). Have any new lump checked by your vet.

**Eyes and metabolism:** DNA tests exist for hereditary cataract (HC-HSF4) and for L-2-hydroxyglutaric aciduria (L-2-HGA), a metabolic disorder that affects the nervous system (Royal Kennel Club, PDSA).

**Skin and joints:** the PDSA lists skin allergies, skin disease caused by demodex mites, certain cancers and hip dysplasia. For itchy skin see [itching in dogs](/animals/dog-itching); for stiffness see [limping in dogs](/animals/dog-limping).

## Which health tests matter for a Staffie puppy?

The Royal Kennel Club lists prioritising genetic diversity as good practice, and as best practice DNA tests for hereditary cataract and L-2-HGA, elbow scoring under the BVA/KC Elbow Dysplasia Scheme and eye testing under the BVA/KC/ISDS Eye Scheme, including litter screening. Ask to see the parents' results.

## Can I bring a Staffordshire Bull Terrier into Germany?

No. The federal Hundeverbringungs- und -einfuhrbeschränkungsgesetz names four breeds: Pitbull Terrier, American Staffordshire Terrier, Staffordshire Bull Terrier and Bull Terrier. Dogs of these breeds and their crosses with each other or with other dogs may not be brought into Germany from another EU country or imported from a country outside the EU (§ 2 together with the definitions in § 1). The law allows the federal government to set exceptions by regulation. It covers bringing dogs into Germany only; the rules for keeping dogs are made by each German state and are not covered here. If you plan to move or travel with a Staffie, check with the authorities before you go, and see [travelling with pets in the EU](/animals/travelling-eu-pets).

## Is a Staffordshire Bull Terrier right for me?

Plan for an energetic, strong dog that wants company most of the day, an hour or more of exercise and training daily, and only weekly grooming of a short coat that sheds. Check new lumps promptly, ask for the parents' DNA, eye and elbow results, and check the law before taking the dog abroad.
