## How long do Poodles live?

Longer than most dogs. A 2024 UK study of 6,427 Poodles found a median lifespan of 14.0 years, against 12.0 years for crossbred dogs (McMillan et al.); that study did not separate the sizes. The Royal Kennel Club gives over 12 years for each of the Standard, Miniature and Toy, and the PDSA gives 12 to 14 years.

## How big does a Poodle get?

That depends on the size. The FCI breed standard recognises four sizes with one standard: Standard Poodles over 45 cm up to 60 cm at the withers (with 2 cm tolerance), Medium over 35 to 45 cm, Miniature over 28 to 35 cm and Toy over 24 to 28 cm, ideally 25 cm. Smaller sizes must keep the same proportions and show no sign of dwarfism. The Royal Kennel Club recognises three of them: Standard, Miniature and Toy.

## How do you care for a Poodle's coat?

The FCI standard describes two coat types: curly, with fine, woolly, frizzy and elastic hair in even curls, and corded, with cords at least 20 cm long. Accepted colours are solid black, white, brown, grey and fawn, and the current FCI standard (2024) also lists parti-coloured and tan-marked patterns. The Royal Kennel Club says the coat does not shed but needs grooming every day, and the PDSA adds that it often needs clipping and shaping by a professional groomer periodically through the year. The traditional trim comes from the breed's past as a water retriever (Royal Kennel Club).

## What health problems do Poodles have?

**Addison's disease (hypoadrenocorticism):** the UFAW genetic welfare database reports a survey of 1,722 Standard Poodles in which 8.6% had it. The adrenal glands make too little hormone, causing tiredness, poor appetite, vomiting, weakness and weight loss, often on and off at first. It is highly heritable (0.75), but the genes are unknown and there is no test.

**Eyes:** UFAW says hereditary cataract affects over 60 breeds, including Poodles of all types, which have a higher risk than dogs overall. The lens becomes cloudy, sight is lost and the dog may go blind; complications such as uveitis and glaucoma are painful. The Royal Kennel Club also lists DNA tests for progressive retinal atrophy (PRA) in all three sizes.

**Other conditions** listed by the PDSA include slipping kneecaps, Legg-Perthes disease, epilepsy, bloat, hip dysplasia, von Willebrand's disease (a bleeding disorder), Cushing's disease, intervertebral disc disease, dry eye, an underactive thyroid, distichiasis and entropion.

## Which health tests matter for a Poodle puppy?

They differ by size (Royal Kennel Club). **Standard:** DNA tests for PRA (rcd4 and prcd-PRA) and von Willebrand disease (vWD1), eye testing and hip scoring, all as best practice; imported dogs must be tested for both PRA forms. **Miniature:** DNA tests for osteochondrodysplasia (a skeletal growth disorder) and prcd-PRA, eye testing and hip scoring as best practice. **Toy:** DNA test for prcd-PRA as good practice, with eye testing and hip scoring as best practice. Ask to see the parents' results.

## Is a Poodle right for me?

The FCI standard describes a dog renowned for its loyalty, able to learn and be trained, which makes it a particularly pleasant companion; it mentions a friendly, joyful and loyal character. That only tells you what breeders aim for. The PDSA says Poodles need around an hour of exercise a day and love to run, swim and retrieve. Plan for daily grooming and periodic professional clipping whichever size you choose.
