## How long do Yorkshire Terriers live?

Longer than most dogs. A 2024 UK study of 14,579 Yorkshire Terriers found a median lifespan of 13.3 years, against 12.0 years for crossbred dogs (McMillan et al.). In a VetCompass study of UK vet records from 2016, the median age at death was 13.56 years, and the most common causes of death were brain disorders (9.8%) and kidney disorders (8.7%) (O'Neill et al., 2025). VetCompass life tables put a Yorkshire Terrier's life expectancy at birth at 12.54 years, the second highest of 18 breeds (Teng et al.). The authors of the 2025 study see the breed's long life as a sign of robust overall health.

## How big does a Yorkshire Terrier get?

Small. The FCI breed standard gives a weight of up to 3.2 kg and describes a very compact, neat dog with an upright carriage. Many pet Yorkies are heavier: adults in UK vet care had a median bodyweight of 5.06 kg, with a range of 1.01 to 15 kg (O'Neill et al., 2025).

## What health problems do Yorkshire Terriers have?

In UK vet records of 3,308 Yorkshire Terriers in 2016, the most common problems were (O'Neill et al., 2025):

- **Dental (periodontal) disease**: 21.1%
- **Overgrown nails**: 6.5%
- **Anal sac impaction**: 4.0%
- **Overweight or obesity**: 3.7%
- **Retained puppy teeth** (persistent deciduous teeth): 3.6%

The authors name dental disease, retained puppy teeth and slipping kneecaps as problems the breed appears predisposed to.

**Slipping kneecaps** (patellar luxation): 3.4% of the Yorkies were diagnosed in one year, against about 1% of dogs overall in an earlier UK study. The kneecap slips out of its groove, which causes lameness and pain and later arthritis (UFAW). If your dog skips on a back leg or limps, see [limping in dogs](/animals/dog-limping).

**Tracheal collapse:** the rings of cartilage in the windpipe soften, the airway narrows and the dog has a dry, harsh "honking" cough and noisy, laboured breathing. It mostly affects middle-aged and older toy dogs, and according to UFAW, Yorkshire Terriers make up about 65% of cases. It cannot be cured, but UFAW lists ways to manage it: avoid exercise in hot weather, keep the dog slim, and avoid collars or harnesses that press on the windpipe. A dog that turns bluish or struggles to breathe needs a vet straight away.

**Liver shunt** (portosystemic shunt): an abnormal blood vessel lets blood bypass the liver, so toxins from digestion reach the brain. Puppies may fail to grow; signs such as dullness, trembling, drooling, head-pressing and seizures are often worse after meals. In the USA, about 3% of Yorkshire Terriers were found to be affected (UFAW).

The PDSA also lists bladder stones, progressive retinal atrophy and lens luxation (eye diseases), allergic skin disease, diabetes, Legg-Perthes disease of the hip, dry eye and hydrocephalus.

## Which health tests matter for a Yorkshire Terrier puppy?

The Royal Kennel Club currently recommends no specific pre-breeding health tests for the breed, and says this may change as new evidence comes in. The PDSA advises checking whether the parents have been screened under the BVA/Kennel Club Canine Health Schemes, and asking about the health of the parents and grandparents.

## How much exercise does a Yorkshire Terrier need?

The Royal Kennel Club gives up to 30 minutes a day; the PDSA says around 30 to 40 minutes, and that Yorkies enjoy running, fetching and games as much as bigger dogs.

## Is a Yorkshire Terrier right for me?

The FCI standard describes an alert, intelligent toy terrier, spirited and with an even disposition, and notes the terrier's hunting instinct, "be it for a toy in the house or a rodent in the garden". That only tells you what breeders aim for: every dog is an individual. The long coat needs grooming every day (Royal Kennel Club). The PDSA says Yorkies suit families with older children best, because their bones are fragile. Plan for daily tooth care and regular dental checks.
