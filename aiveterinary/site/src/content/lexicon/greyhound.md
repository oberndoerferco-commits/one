## How long do Greyhounds live?

A little shorter than the average dog. A 2024 UK study that included 3,964 Greyhounds found a median lifespan of 11.5 years, against 12.0 years for crossbred dogs (McMillan et al., supplementary data). A VetCompass study of 5,419 Greyhounds under UK vet care in 2016 found a median of 11.4 years, based on 474 dogs that died; females lived a little longer (11.8 years) than males (11.2 years) (O'Neill et al.). The Royal Kennel Club gives over 10 years.

## How big does a Greyhound get?

Large and lean. The FCI breed standard gives an ideal height of 71 to 76 cm for males and 68 to 71 cm for females. In UK vet records the mean adult weight was 29.7 kg: 32.3 kg for males and 27.2 kg for females (O'Neill et al.). The Royal Kennel Club notes that show Greyhounds are larger and heavier than racing Greyhounds. The coat is fine and close; black was the most common colour in the VetCompass study.

## What is a Greyhound like?

The FCI standard describes a dog of remarkable stamina and endurance, intelligent, gentle, affectionate and even-tempered. That is the breeding goal; every dog is an individual. Greyhounds were bred for racing and coursing, and many pet Greyhounds in the UK are former racers (O'Neill et al.). The PDSA notes a strong chase instinct, so exercise them in enclosed areas and take care around other pets.

## How much exercise does a Greyhound need?

Less than many people think. The Royal Kennel Club gives up to an hour a day. The PDSA says Greyhounds are not overly energetic and need about an hour daily; they prefer a couple of good runs to miles of walking, in a secure area because they are extremely fast.

## What health problems do Greyhounds have?

**Teeth:** dental disease stands out. In the VetCompass study, periodontal disease was recorded in 39.0% of Greyhounds in a single year, the most common problem by far (O'Neill et al.). The authors suggest rehoming organisations tell adopters about preventive care.

**Injuries and joints:** other common problems were overgrown nails (11.1%), wounds (6.2%), osteoarthritis (4.6%) and claw injuries (4.2%). For lameness see [limping in dogs](/animals/dog-limping).

**Causes of death:** the most common recorded causes were cancer (21.5%), collapse (14.3%) and musculoskeletal disorders (7.8%).

**Other conditions** listed by the PDSA: gastric dilatation-volvulus ("bloat"), in which the stomach twists, which is an emergency and is often seen in large, deep-chested breeds; bone cancer (osteosarcoma); progressive retinal atrophy; and von Willebrand disease, a bleeding disorder.

## Which health tests matter for a Greyhound puppy?

The Royal Kennel Club lists prioritising genetic diversity as good practice before breeding and a DNA test for Greyhound neuropathy as best practice. Many pet Greyhounds are retired racers rehomed as adults; ask the rehoming organisation about the dog's health history.

## Is a Greyhound right for me?

Plan for a large, gentle dog that needs about an hour of exercise with a safe place to sprint, a secure lead outside, and regular dental care. The coat is short, needs grooming once a week and sheds (Royal Kennel Club).
