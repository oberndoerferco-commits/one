## What is special about the Icelandic horse?

Its history and its gaits. According to FEIF, the international federation of Icelandic horse associations, the breed descends from horses brought to Iceland by settlers between 874 and 930 and has been isolated there for more than 1,000 years without genetic input from other breeds. More than 280,000 Icelandic horses worldwide are registered in the WorldFengur studbook database.

Besides walk, trot and canter, Icelandic horses have the tölt, and many also have pace; FEIF calls tölt and pace the most desirable gaits. It describes the horses as friendly, adventurous, intelligent and quick to learn, generally very easy to handle and willing to work. That is the breed federation's description; individual horses vary.

We found no published lifespan study for Icelandic horses.

## What is sweet itch, and why does it affect Icelandic horses?

Insect bite hypersensitivity (IBH), also called sweet itch or summer eczema, is the most common allergic skin disease of horses. It is an allergy to the saliva of biting midges (Culicoides). Horses itch intensely, mostly at the mane, tail and withers, rub themselves until the skin is raw, and get open wounds, hair loss and secondary infections (Björnsdóttir et al., 2006; Birras et al., 2021).

The midges do not live in Iceland, so the disease is unknown in horses there. The problem comes when horses leave:

- More than 50% of Icelandic horses exported to continental Europe as adults develop IBH within the first two years of exposure to midges (Birras et al., 2021).
- In a study of 330 horses exported to Germany, Denmark and Sweden, 114 (34.5%) had summer eczema, most often two years after export (Björnsdóttir et al., 2006).
- In a Swedish owner survey, 26.2% of horses imported from Iceland were affected, against 6.7% of Icelandic horses born in Sweden.

Icelandic horses born in Europe do not have a higher rate than other breeds, which is 3 to 10% across Europe. Horses exported and exposed to midges before seven months of age also have the same low risk, which suggests that meeting the allergens early helps the immune system tolerate them (Birras et al., 2021).

## Is sweet itch inherited?

Not clearly. In the export study the estimated heritability was not significantly different from zero, but the authors stress that environmental effects were so strong that the study was too small to rule out a genetic influence (Björnsdóttir et al., 2006). The main risk factor was exposure to the midges.

## How can I protect an imported Icelandic horse?

The 2006 study says prevention has to be based on avoiding the midges:

- keep the horse on windy, dry pasture
- stable it when the midges are most active
- use an eczema blanket
