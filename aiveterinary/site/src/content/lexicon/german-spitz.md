## Is the Pomeranian a German Spitz?

Yes, according to the FCI. Its German Spitz standard covers five varieties that differ mainly in size: the Wolfsspitz (called Keeshond in non-German-speaking countries), the Giant, Medium-size and Miniature Spitz, and the Toy Spitz. The standard states that outside German-speaking countries the Toy Spitz (Zwergspitz) is known as the Pomeranian. The UK's Royal Kennel Club treats them differently: it recognises the German Spitz in two sizes, Mittel and Klein, which share one standard, and lists the Pomeranian as a separate toy breed that was bred down from the German Spitz.

## How big does a German Spitz get?

It depends on the variety. The FCI standard gives these heights at the withers:

- Wolfsspitz/Keeshond: 49 cm ± 6 cm
- Giant Spitz: 45 cm ± 5 cm
- Medium-size Spitz: 35 cm ± 5 cm
- Miniature Spitz: 27 cm ± 3 cm
- Toy Spitz/Pomeranian: 21 cm ± 3 cm

It gives no fixed weights and asks for a weight that matches the size. The PDSA gives typical weights of 5 to 8 kg for the small (Klein) and 7 to 11 kg for the medium (Mittel) German Spitz, and 2 to 3 kg for the Pomeranian.

## How long do German Spitz and Pomeranians live?

A 2024 UK study found a median lifespan of 12.2 years for 2,941 Pomeranians, about the same as the 12.0 years for crossbred dogs (McMillan et al., supplementary data). Medium-size German Spitz had a median of 14.0 years, but that figure comes from only 211 dogs, 96 of which had died, so it is less certain; the Keeshond's 12.3 years in the same study rests on just 171 dogs. The Royal Kennel Club gives over 12 years for both the German Spitz and the Pomeranian.

## What is a German Spitz like?

The FCI standard describes all varieties as always attentive, lively and extraordinarily attached to their owner, very teachable and easy to train, neither timid nor aggressive, and highlights indifference to weather, robustness and longevity. For the larger sizes it mentions a natural distrust of strangers and no hunting instinct, which make it a watchdog for home and farm; for the Toy Spitz/Pomeranian it describes a companionable, cheerful dog suited as a watchdog for house and apartment. That is the breeding goal, not a promise for every dog.

## How do I care for the coat?

All varieties have a double coat: a long, straight topcoat that stands away from the body over a short, thick, cotton-wool-like undercoat, with a thick mane around the neck and a bushy tail carried over the back (FCI standard). The standard says the coat must not look modelled. The Royal Kennel Club lists grooming every day for the German Spitz Klein and more than once a week for the Mittel and the Pomeranian; all of them shed.

## How much exercise does a German Spitz need?

Not a lot. The Royal Kennel Club gives up to 30 minutes a day for the Pomeranian and up to 1 hour for the German Spitz Klein and Mittel; the PDSA gives around 30 minutes for both.

## What health problems do German Spitz and Pomeranians have?

**Broken forearms in the smallest dogs:** the UFAW genetic welfare database says toy breeds including the Pomeranian easily fracture the lower forearm (radius and ulna), often from only minor trauma, most commonly a jump or fall from a height such as the owner's arms. The bones are relatively weaker for their body size, and the fractures often heal slowly or not at all; they usually need surgery and can take months to heal. Lift and carry small dogs securely, and see your vet if your dog suddenly will not use a front leg; see [limping in dogs](/animals/dog-limping).

**Kneecaps:** in a VetCompass study in England, Pomeranians had 6.5 times the odds of patellar luxation, a kneecap that slips out of place, compared with crossbred dogs (O'Neill et al.). The PDSA lists it for both German Spitz and Pomeranians.

**Other problems in Pomeranians** listed by the PDSA: progressive retinal atrophy (a gradual loss of sight), Chiari-like malformation and syringomyelia (a painful condition with fluid-filled cavities in the spinal cord), hydrocephalus, tracheal collapse, an underactive thyroid, cataracts and inward-turning eyelids. The Royal Kennel Club lists eye testing under the BVA/KC/ISDS Eye Scheme as best practice before breeding for German Spitz and Pomeranians.

## Is a German Spitz right for me?

Choose the size that fits your home. All varieties were bred as watchdogs and need regular grooming of a thick coat that sheds. With a Toy Spitz/Pomeranian, plan for careful handling to avoid falls, and ask for the parents' eye test results.
