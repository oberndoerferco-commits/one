## How long do Australian Shepherds live?

There is little good UK data. A 2024 UK study found a median lifespan of 13.7 years, longer than crossbred dogs (12.0 years), but it was based on only 286 Australian Shepherds, of which 62 had died, so the figure is uncertain (95% range 12.8 to 14.6 years) (McMillan et al.). The Royal Kennel Club gives the breed's lifespan as over 10 years.

## How big does an Australian Shepherd get?

Medium. The FCI breed standard gives a preferred height at the withers of 51 to 58 cm for males and 46 to 53 cm for females, and says quality should not be sacrificed for size. It gives no weight; the PDSA gives an average of 18 to 29 kg. Despite its name, the Royal Kennel Club explains that the breed was developed in America as a ranch sheepdog.

## What health problems do Australian Shepherds have?

**Eyes:** collie eye anomaly and choroidal hypoplasia are inherited abnormalities at the back of the eye; the Royal Kennel Club lists a DNA test for them as good practice before breeding. The PDSA also lists inherited cataracts and progressive retinal atrophy, which can lead to blindness. DNA tests exist for one form of hereditary cataract (HSF4) and for prcd-PRA.

**MDR1:** dogs with this gene variant are particularly sensitive to certain medicines (PDSA). If your dog has been DNA tested, tell your vet the result.

**Joints and other conditions:** the PDSA lists hip and elbow dysplasia, which lead to arthritis, and cobalamin (vitamin B12) malabsorption, which causes poor growth, weakness and blood problems. If your dog limps, see [limping in dogs](/animals/dog-limping).

## Which health tests matter for an Australian Shepherd puppy?

The Royal Kennel Club lists the DNA test for collie eye anomaly / choroidal hypoplasia (CEA/CH) as good practice. As best practice it adds DNA tests for hereditary cataract (HC-HSF4), MDR1 and prcd-PRA, hip and elbow scoring under the BVA/KC schemes, and eye testing under the BVA/KC/ISDS Eye Scheme. Ask to see both parents' results.

## Is an Australian Shepherd right for me?

The FCI standard describes an intelligent working dog with strong herding and guarding instincts, a loyal companion with the stamina to work all day, even-tempered, good-natured and seldom quarrelsome, though it may be reserved at first meeting. That only tells you what breeders aim for. The Royal Kennel Club gives more than 2 hours of exercise a day, a large house and garden and grooming more than once a week. The PDSA says Aussies suit experienced owners in very active homes, get bored quickly and should not be left alone for long.
