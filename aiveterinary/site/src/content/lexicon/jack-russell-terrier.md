## How long do Jack Russell Terriers live?

Longer than most dogs. A 2024 UK study of 19,166 Jack Russell Terriers found a median lifespan of 13.3 years, against 12.0 years for crossbred dogs (McMillan et al.). In VetCompass life tables, built from UK vet records, the Jack Russell had the highest life expectancy at birth of the 18 breeds studied, 12.72 years, and its life table ran to 19 years of age (Teng et al.). The Royal Kennel Club gives the breed's lifespan as over 10 years.

## How big does a Jack Russell get?

Small. The FCI breed standard gives an ideal height at the withers of 25 to 30 cm and a weight of about 1 kg for every 5 cm of height: about 5 kg for a 25 cm dog and 6 kg for a 30 cm dog. The coat can be smooth, broken or rough, but must be weatherproof, and white must predominate. The Royal Kennel Club notes that the FCI breed was developed in Australia from British working terriers; the similar but taller Parson Russell Terrier is a separate breed.

## What health problems do Jack Russell Terriers have?

**Eyes:** primary lens luxation, in which the lens of the eye slips out of place, is painful and causes blindness (PDSA). The Royal Kennel Club lists a DNA test for it as good practice before breeding. A suddenly red, cloudy or painful eye is an emergency.

**Balance:** late-onset ataxia and spinocerebellar ataxia are inherited nerve diseases that cause gradually worsening loss of balance and coordination; there is no treatment (PDSA). DNA tests exist for both.

**Joints:** the PDSA also lists slipping kneecaps (luxating patellas) and Legg-Perthes disease, in which the head of the thigh bone breaks down. Both cause lameness in small dogs; see [limping in dogs](/animals/dog-limping).

## Which health tests matter for a Jack Russell puppy?

The Royal Kennel Club lists the DNA test for primary lens luxation (PLL) as good practice before breeding, and DNA tests for late-onset ataxia (LOA) and spinocerebellar ataxia (SCA) plus eye testing under the BVA/KC/ISDS Eye Scheme as best practice. Ask to see both parents' results.

## Is a Jack Russell right for me?

The FCI standard describes a lively, alert and active terrier with a keen, intelligent expression, bold and fearless, friendly but quietly confident. That only tells you what breeders aim for. The Royal Kennel Club gives up to 1 hour of exercise a day. The PDSA says Jack Russells love to chase, can squeeze through the smallest gap in a fence, tend to bark when excited and need someone at home for company, and recommends early reward-based training.
