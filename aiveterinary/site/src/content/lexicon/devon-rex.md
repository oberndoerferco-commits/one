## How long do Devon Rex cats live?

The GCCF, the UK's main cat registry, gives a typical lifespan of 12 to 16 years. There is no figure from vet records: the UK VetCompass life tables for cats that died in 2019 analysed only the 12 breeds with 15 or more deaths, and the Devon Rex was not among them (Teng et al., 2024).

## How big does a Devon Rex get?

Not large. The GCCF describes a medium-sized, well-muscled cat with a broad chest, slim legs, very large ears set low on the head and big oval eyes, weighing 6 to 9 pounds (about 2.7 to 4.1 kg) on average, with males heavier than females. The PDSA gives an average weight of 3.6 kg.

## How do I care for the curly coat?

Gently. The GCCF explains that the waved coat has little guard hair. Coats vary from a full coat of loose curls to a thin, suede-like coat, some cats have almost bare patches, and when moulting the coat often breaks off until new hair grows back. Groom gently so the delicate hairs do not break; often a hand stroked over the coat is all it needs. The PDSA goes further and says the fur should never be brushed. Devons feel warm because their coat insulates less, and they love heat. Contrary to popular belief they shed and produce allergens like other cats, so people with cat allergies should spend time with a Devon before getting one.

The whiskers and eyebrows are crinkled and may be stubbly (GCCF). That matters, because whiskers are an important sense organ for cats (see the German expert report below).

## What health problems do Devon Rex cats have?

**Hereditary myopathy.** UFAW describes an inherited disease in which signals from the nerves do not reach the muscles properly. Affected cats have general muscle weakness, cannot hold their head up properly, tire easily and may tremble or collapse, especially when stressed or excited. Signs start between 3 and 23 weeks of age and usually stabilise at around nine months. Many have trouble swallowing, so food can block the airway, which can cause choking, pneumonia or sudden death. There is no cure. It is recessive, and UFAW recommends DNA testing of all cats that may be bred from; the GCCF says a reliable test is available.

**Joints.** The GCCF says slipping kneecaps (luxating patella) appear more common in the breed; the PDSA also lists hip dysplasia. Both can cause lameness and later arthritis.

## Is a Devon Rex right for me?

The GCCF describes an intelligent, mischievous and highly active cat with a huge personality, a powerful jumper that wants to be involved in everything and to share your lap and bed. Devons are great with children and other pets and thrive on company, but they should not be left alone for long periods, as they can become destructive when bored. Ask for the parents' myopathy test results.

## Rules in Germany

§ 11b of the German Animal Welfare Act bans breeding when the offspring are expected to suffer because inherited body parts or organs are missing, unsuitable or altered; it names no breeds. A 1999 expert report on how to interpret § 11b, written for the federal agriculture ministry, discusses rex cats including the Devon Rex. It considers missing whiskers a bodily harm that causes lasting suffering and recommends a breeding ban for cats that lack whiskers. It also asks breed clubs to change their standards so that cats with strongly shortened or curled whiskers are avoided. The report is a guideline for breeders and authorities, not law.
