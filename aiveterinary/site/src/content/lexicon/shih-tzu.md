## How long do Shih Tzus live?

Longer than the average dog. A 2024 UK study of 12,007 Shih Tzus found a median lifespan of 12.8 years, against 12.0 years for crossbred dogs (McMillan et al.). A VetCompass study of UK vet records found a median age at death of 12.7 years, but from only 89 deaths (Dale et al., 2024). VetCompass life tables gave a life expectancy at birth of 11.05 years for 638 Shih Tzus, close to the 11.23 years for dogs overall (Teng et al.). The Royal Kennel Club gives the breed's lifespan as over 10 years.

## How big does a Shih Tzu get?

Small. The FCI breed standard gives a height at the withers of no more than 27 cm and a weight of 4.5 to 8 kg, ideally 4.5 to 7.5 kg. In UK vet records the mean adult bodyweight was 7.9 kg (Dale et al.).

## Why do Shih Tzus have breathing and eye problems?

Because of the short face. The UFAW genetic welfare database explains that in flat-faced (brachycephalic) dogs the upper jaw and nose are shortened while the soft tissue inside is not, so it is squeezed into a smaller space. This causes brachycephalic airway obstruction syndrome (BOAS): snoring, noisy and open-mouth breathing, struggling for breath, poor tolerance of exercise, a high risk of heatstroke and disrupted sleep. It is progressive and lifelong. UFAW says there are no published figures for Shih Tzus, but because it is directly linked to the head shape, many are likely to have it to some degree.

The same shape affects the eyes. Shallow eye sockets and large, prominent eyes can lead to eyelids that do not close fully, lashes or nose-fold hair rubbing on the eye, poor tears and long-term damage to the cornea, with constant discomfort and possible blindness. A minor knock can push the eye out of its socket, which is an emergency (UFAW).

Snoring and noisy breathing in a Shih Tzu can be signs of BOAS, not just a quirk of the breed; mention them to your vet. A dog that struggles to breathe or collapses in the heat needs a vet straight away: see [heatstroke in dogs](/animals/heatstroke).

## What other health problems do Shih Tzus have?

Among 2,423 Shih Tzus in VetCompass records, the most common problems were periodontal (dental) disease (9.5%), anal sac impaction (7.4%) and ear disorders (5.5%). Anal sac impaction, umbilical hernias and eye problems were more common than reported for dogs overall, and the authors highlight dental care as a priority (Dale et al.). The PDSA also lists dry eye, cataracts, progressive retinal atrophy, retinal detachment, crowded teeth, renal dysplasia (a kidney defect from birth), intervertebral disc disease and Cushing's disease.

## Which health tests matter for a Shih Tzu puppy?

The Royal Kennel Club lists eye testing under the BVA/KC/ISDS Eye Scheme as good practice before breeding. In August 2026 it recognised the breathing test of the Respiratory Function Grading Scheme as best practice for the breed, to be added formally to its Health Standard from January 2027. Ask for both parents' eye results and breathing grades. UFAW advises seeing the puppy and both parents, and not buying a puppy that shows signs of BOAS or whose parents have signs or have had surgery for it.

## Is a Shih Tzu right for me?

The FCI standard describes an intelligent, active and alert dog, friendly and independent; it also says the hair must not affect the dog's ability to see. That only tells you what breeders aim for. The Royal Kennel Club gives up to 1 hour of exercise a day and daily grooming of the long coat. Plan for daily coat and eye checks, tooth brushing and careful exercise in warm weather.

## Rules in Germany

German law bans breeding vertebrates when it is to be expected that the offspring will suffer pain, suffering or harm because inherited body parts or organs are missing, unsuitable or altered (§ 11b Tierschutzgesetz). The law describes the harm and does not list breeds.
