## How long do Boston Terriers live?

A little shorter than the average dog. A 2024 UK study that included 1,984 Boston Terriers found a median lifespan of 11.8 years, against 12.0 years for crossbred dogs (McMillan et al., supplementary data). The Royal Kennel Club gives over 10 years.

## How big does a Boston Terrier get?

Small and compact. The FCI breed standard describes a lively, smooth-coated, short-headed, compactly built, short-tailed dog with a square outline. It divides the breed into three weight classes: under 6.8 kg, 6.8 to 9 kg, and 9 to 11.35 kg. The coat is short, smooth and fine, brindle, seal or black with a white muzzle band, a white blaze between the eyes and a white chest.

## What is a Boston Terrier like?

The FCI standard describes a friendly and lively dog with an excellent disposition and a high degree of intelligence, "an incomparable companion". That is the breeding goal, not a promise for every dog. The PDSA calls Boston Terriers lively and intelligent dogs that enjoy games.

## Does a Boston Terrier have breathing problems?

Often, though usually less severely than Bulldogs and French Bulldogs. The breed's short face squeezes the soft tissue of the nose and throat into a smaller space. The UFAW genetic welfare database describes narrowed nostrils, an over-long soft palate and a narrow windpipe that make breathing harder; this brachycephalic obstructive airway syndrome (BOAS) causes snoring, noisy breathing, struggling for breath, poor exercise tolerance, disrupted sleep and a high risk of heatstroke. It is lifelong, gets worse over time and can affect puppies under 6 months. UFAW considers that probably all Boston Terriers are affected to some degree, and that dogs with BOAS have a higher risk of dying under anaesthesia.

A 2024 UK study graded the breathing of 104 Boston Terriers before and after a 3-minute exercise test. 37.5% showed no signs of BOAS (grade 0), compared with 15.2% of Bulldogs and 10% of French Bulldogs in an earlier comparable study. 35.6% had mild signs (grade 1), 26% had moderate, clinically relevant BOAS (grade 2) and one dog had severe BOAS (grade 3) (Tomlinson et al.). The FCI standard asks for well-opened nostrils.

Snoring and noisy breathing can be signs of BOAS (UFAW). Heatstroke is an emergency: see [heatstroke in dogs](/animals/heatstroke). A dog that is struggling to breathe needs a vet straight away.

## What other health problems do Boston Terriers have?

**Spine:** the screw tail is formed by abnormally shaped tail bones, and the same malformation (hemivertebrae) can affect the spine higher up. UFAW describes it as a common problem in the breed: the misshapen bones can squash the spinal cord and cause pain, wobbliness, loss of hind-leg function and incontinence, most often in young, growing dogs. Only X-rays can show whether a dog has hemivertebrae.

**Skin and eyes:** the PDSA lists atopy (allergic skin disease that causes itching), dry eye and cherry eye. For itching see [itching in dogs](/animals/dog-itching).

## Which health tests matter for a Boston Terrier puppy?

The Royal Kennel Club lists eye testing under the BVA/KC/ISDS Eye Scheme as good practice before breeding and a DNA test for hereditary cataract (HC-HSF4) as best practice. In August 2026 it recognised breathing grading under the Respiratory Function Grading Scheme for the breed, to be added formally to its health standard from January 2027. Ask for both parents' breathing grades and eye results.

## How much exercise does a Boston Terrier need?

The Royal Kennel Club and the PDSA give up to an hour a day. The PDSA warns never to exercise a Boston Terrier in the heat of the day, because the breed is prone to overheating.

## Rules in Germany

The FCI standard allows the ears to be natural or cropped. In Germany, ear cropping is banned unless a vet finds it necessary in the individual case (§ 6 Tierschutzgesetz), and dogs whose ears have been cropped in breach of animal welfare law may not be exhibited or entered in other events where dogs are judged (§ 10 Tierschutz-Hundeverordnung).

## Is a Boston Terrier right for me?

Plan for a lively, friendly small dog that needs up to an hour of exercise in cool parts of the day and only weekly grooming. UFAW advises examining any puppy and both its parents for signs of BOAS before buying; ask for breathing grades and eye results, and talk to your vet about breathing early.
