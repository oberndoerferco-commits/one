## How long do Beagles live?

About as long as the average dog. A 2024 UK study of 4,678 Beagles found a median lifespan of 12.5 years, which did not differ statistically from crossbred dogs (12.0 years) (McMillan et al.). A 2025 VetCompass study of UK vet records found a median age at death of 11.28 years across 322 deaths, with females living almost a year longer than males (O'Neill et al.). VetCompass life tables gave a lower life expectancy at birth of 9.85 years, but that figure rests on only 172 Beagles (Teng et al.). The Royal Kennel Club gives the breed's lifespan as over 12 years.

## How big does a Beagle get?

The FCI breed standard asks for a sturdy, compact hound with a height at the withers of 33 to 40 cm; it gives no weight. In UK vet records the median adult Beagle weighed 18.19 kg, males about 3 kg more than females (O'Neill et al., 2025).

## What health problems do Beagles have?

**Weight and teeth:** in the 2025 VetCompass study of 3,729 Beagles, the most common problems were obesity (24.27%), dental (periodontal) disease (17.78%), overgrown nails (11.61%), ear infection (11.18%) and impacted anal sacs (10.59%). The authors say owners and vets should put special emphasis on weight control and dental care in this breed. Cancer was the most common cause of death (19.26%).

**Thyroid:** the UFAW genetic welfare database says Beagles have long been known to be prone to hypothyroidism, too little thyroid hormone. Typical signs are tiredness, reluctance to exercise, weight gain and skin problems. It is lifelong and usually found in middle age; blood tests are needed to diagnose it, and there is no genetic test. See [thyroid problems in dogs and cats](/animals/thyroid-dogs-cats).

**Other conditions** listed by the PDSA include epilepsy, meningitis, intervertebral disc disease, cherry eye and ear infections. If your Beagle shakes its head or scratches its ears, see [ear infections in dogs](/animals/dog-ear-infection).

## Which health tests matter for a Beagle puppy?

The Royal Kennel Club lists as good practice before breeding DNA tests for Lafora's disease and for neonatal cerebellar cortical degeneration (NCCD), which affects movement and balance. As best practice it adds DNA tests for factor VII deficiency (a clotting disorder), Imerslund-Gräsbeck syndrome (poor vitamin B12 absorption) and Musladin-Lueke syndrome (which affects how puppies develop). Ask to see the parents' results.

## How much exercise does a Beagle need?

The PDSA says at least an hour and a half a day, mixing walking and running, plus play, training and time in a secure garden; the Royal Kennel Club gives up to 1 hour. Beagles are scenthounds: the PDSA says they enjoy scent games, are good escape artists and like to climb and dig, so the garden must be secure.

## Is a Beagle right for me?

The FCI standard describes a merry hound bred to hunt hare by following a scent: bold, active, with stamina and determination, amiable and alert, with no aggression or timidity. That only tells you what breeders aim for. The PDSA adds that Beagles thrive on company, can be vocal when left alone and may not suit homes with small pets because of their hunting instinct. Plan for plenty of exercise, careful weight control and regular dental care.
