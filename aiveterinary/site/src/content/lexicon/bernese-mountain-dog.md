## How long do Bernese Mountain Dogs live?

Shorter than most dogs. A 2024 UK study of 1,565 Bernese Mountain Dogs found a median lifespan of 10.1 years, against 12.0 years for crossbred dogs (McMillan et al.). The Royal Kennel Club gives the breed's lifespan as under 10 years, and the PDSA as up to 10 years.

## How big does a Bernese Mountain Dog get?

Large. The FCI breed standard gives a height at the withers of 64 to 70 cm for males (ideal 66 to 68 cm) and 58 to 66 cm for females (ideal 60 to 63 cm). It gives no weight; the PDSA gives an average of 48 to 63 kg for males and 42 to 53 kg for females. The breed comes from the Swiss canton of Berne, where it was used as a draught and farm dog (Royal Kennel Club).

## What health problems do Bernese Mountain Dogs have?

**Elbows:** the UFAW genetic welfare database describes fragmented medial coronoid process, one form of elbow dysplasia, as a common disease of the breed: it has been suggested that 36 to 70% of Bernese have it, and the breed has the greatest predisposition of all breeds. Pieces of bone and cartilage break off inside the elbow and cause arthritis, often by 4 to 6 months of age. Without successful treatment, the pain lasts for life, and treatment is difficult. Genes account for about 24 to 43% of the chance of the disease, and affected puppies can be born to unaffected parents. A young dog that limps or is stiff on its front legs should see a vet: see [limping in dogs](/animals/dog-limping).

**Other conditions** listed by the PDSA include hip dysplasia, bloat (gastric dilatation-volvulus, where the stomach swells and twists, an emergency), degenerative myelopathy (a spinal disease that slowly paralyses the hind legs), progressive retinal atrophy, cruciate ligament disease, sore "hot spots" on the skin and certain cancers.

## Which health tests matter for a Bernese puppy?

The Royal Kennel Club lists hip scoring and elbow scoring under the BVA/KC schemes as good practice before breeding. UFAW adds that dogs with elbow disease should not be bred from and that choosing breeding dogs with a better breeding value than the breed average could help. Ask to see both parents' hip and elbow scores.

## Is a Bernese right for me?

The FCI standard describes a self-confident, attentive and vigilant dog, fearless in everyday situations, good-natured and devoted to its own people, self-assured and placid towards strangers, and docile. That only tells you what breeders aim for. The Royal Kennel Club gives up to 1 hour of exercise a day, a large house and garden, and grooming more than once a week. Plan for a large, heavy dog with a shorter life than many breeds.
