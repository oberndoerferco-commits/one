## How long do Siamese cats live?

Often a long time. VetCompass life tables for cats that died in the UK in 2019 put a Siamese cat's life expectancy at birth at 11.69 years, close to the 11.89 years of crossbred cats and higher than most of the other pedigree breeds studied (Teng et al.). The authors also note that, in an earlier UK study, the median lifespan of Siamese was among the longest of all breeds, at about 14 years. The GCCF, the UK's main cat registry, gives 10 to 15 years, and says Siamese often live well into their late teens, and some into their twenties.

## How big does a Siamese cat get?

Medium-sized. The GCCF describes a long, svelte, muscular body on fine legs, with a long, tapering tail free from kinks, and says Siamese should feel much heavier than they look. Males weigh 10 to 15 lb (about 4.5–6.8 kg), females 8 to 12 lb (about 3.6–5.4 kg).

The coloured points come from a gene similar to albinism that makes pigment only where the body is cooler: the mask, ears, legs and tail. Kittens are born pale, and Siamese living in cold climates have darker bodies than those in warm ones (GCCF).

## What health problems do Siamese cats have?

**Asthma** (chronic bronchial disease): the UFAW genetic welfare database calls it the most common lower airway disease in cats and particularly common in Siamese. The airways become inflamed and narrowed, with coughing, wheezing, tiredness and difficulty breathing that may come and go; most affected cats need lifelong treatment. International Cat Care says coughing in cats can look like trying to vomit or bring up a hairball, and suggests filming it for your vet. A sudden attack, with open-mouth breathing and struggling for breath, is an emergency.

**Cancers:** the GCCF says some lines may be predisposed to mediastinal lymphoma, a cancer in the chest that typically causes fluid around the lungs, coughing and breathing difficulty (PDSA), and to intestinal tumours.

**Amyloidosis:** misfolded proteins build up, mainly in the liver, which enlarges and can eventually fail; deposits can also cause sudden internal bleeding. UFAW says Siamese are more susceptible than most breeds: in one Dutch study, 12 of 194 Siamese cats (6.2%) were diagnosed. It is often found only after death.

**Eyes:** progressive retinal atrophy (PRA) causes gradual blindness; the PDSA says genetic tests are available. The original Siamese often had squints and kinked tails, which the GCCF now treats as serious faults.

**Other problems** the GCCF lists are vomiting more than other breeds, eating wool, plastic and other non-food items (pica), and a possible predisposition to hip dysplasia; rare inherited storage diseases (Niemann-Pick disease, mucopolysaccharidosis, GM1 gangliosidosis) have been described. International Cat Care also names pyloric stenosis, a narrowing of the stomach outlet that causes vomiting.

A cat that has swallowed string, wool or plastic may need a vet urgently.

## Is a Siamese cat right for me?

The GCCF describes Siamese as outgoing and extrovert, and as well known for being extremely noisy as for their looks. It says they demand attention, do not like being left on their own, and are not ideal for someone out at work all day, though two Siamese can keep each other company. The short coat needs little grooming. Ask the breeder about the health of the parents and grandparents, including PRA test results (PDSA).
