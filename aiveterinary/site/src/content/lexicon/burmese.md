## How long do Burmese live?

Longer than most cats. VetCompass life tables for cats that died in the UK in 2019 put the Burmese's life expectancy at birth at 14.42 years, the longest of the 12 breeds analysed and well above the 11.89 years for crossbred cats; the figure is based on 45 deaths (Teng et al., 2024). An earlier VetCompass study of cats in England (2009–2012) found a median age at death of 14.3 years from 31 Burmese, similar to crossbred cats at 14.0 years (O'Neill et al., 2015). The GCCF, the UK's main cat registry, gives 10 to 17 years in its breed summary and 16 to 18 years in its care notes.

## How big does a Burmese get?

The GCCF describes an elegant cat with a medium-length, hard, muscular body on slender legs, which feels much heavier than it looks. Males are larger: usually 11 to 14 pounds (about 5.0 to 6.4 kg), against 6 to 9 pounds (about 2.7 to 4.1 kg) for females. The short, satin-like coat needs only a weekly rub with a rubber brush.

## Why are Burmese prone to diabetes?

UFAW says some families and populations of Burmese in some parts of the world appear predisposed to diabetes mellitus, probably because their tissues respond less to insulin; the genes involved are not known. This type of diabetes is more common in older cats, and most affected cats are over eight years old. The PDSA says Burmese are the most common cat breed to develop it. The GCCF adds that the risk rises with age and body weight, so an older Burmese should not be allowed to get fat.

Signs to watch for are drinking and urinating more, and weight loss despite a good appetite (UFAW). See [diabetes in dogs and cats](/animals/diabetes-dogs-cats).

## What other health problems do Burmese have?

- **Hypokalaemic polymyopathy (HK):** low blood potassium causes muscle weakness, sometimes in the neck and legs, so affected cats have trouble walking or holding their head up; in severe cases it can lead to early death. It is recessive, and kittens from two DNA-tested negative parents are free of it. The GCCF strongly recommends testing all breeding Burmese.
- **Burmese head defect:** UFAW describes a gene that, in two copies, causes severe malformations of the head in kittens that are incompatible with life; one copy gives a shortened face. Because a short face is wanted by some breeders, there may be many carriers, especially in "Contemporary" lines and in the USA. A carrier test is available.
- **Other problems:** International Cat Care lists feline orofacial pain syndrome (a painful condition of the mouth and face) and flat-chested kittens among problems more common in Burmese.

## Is a Burmese right for me?

The GCCF calls the Burmese the extrovert of the cat world: cuddly, confident and sociable, playful, energetic and mischievous, and very good with children, other cats and dogs. They want to be part of everything and do not like to feel neglected, so if you enjoy a quiet life, the GCCF says, this breed probably is not for you. Ask for proof of the parents' HK DNA tests.
