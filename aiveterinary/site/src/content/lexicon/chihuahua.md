## How long do Chihuahuas live?

The figures vary a lot. A 2024 UK study of 14,636 Chihuahuas found a median lifespan of 11.8 years, a little shorter than the 12.0 years of crossbred dogs (McMillan et al.). The Royal Kennel Club gives the breed's lifespan as over 12 years.

UK vet records give lower numbers. In a VetCompass study of Chihuahuas in 2016, the median age at death was 8.2 years; females lived longer than males (10.2 against 6.9 years) (O'Neill et al., 2020). VetCompass life tables put a Chihuahua's life expectancy at birth at 7.91 years, yet Chihuahuas were also the breed whose life expectancy stayed above 1.5 years the longest, until the age of 15–16. The authors read this as a very wide spread of lifespans: many Chihuahuas die young, and many live to an old age (Teng et al.). The UK population was young and growing fast when these records were collected, which can pull the figures down.

## How big does a Chihuahua get?

Very small. The FCI breed standard calls the Chihuahua the smallest pedigree dog in the world and judges it by weight only, not height: 1 to 3 kg, ideally 1.5 to 2.5 kg. Dogs under 1 kg or over 3 kg are disqualified, and so are dogs with an open fontanelle (a soft spot in the skull).

Many pet Chihuahuas are heavier than the standard: adults in UK vet care had a median bodyweight of 3.4 kg, with a range of 0.8 to 9.8 kg, and 5.9% were recorded as obese (O'Neill et al., 2020).

## What health problems do Chihuahuas have?

In UK vet records of 2016, the most common problems were (O'Neill et al., 2020):

- **Dental (periodontal) disease**: 13.5%
- **Obesity**: 5.9%
- **Retained puppy teeth** (retained deciduous teeth): 5.7%
- **Anal sac impaction**: 4.9%
- **Aggression**: 4.2%

The most common causes of death were heart disease (18.8%), lower airway disorders (16.3%) and traumatic injuries (13.8%). The authors suggest that vets consider extra training in anaesthesia and dentistry for very small dogs.

**Heart:** in another VetCompass study, Chihuahuas were among the breeds with higher odds of degenerative mitral valve disease, a wearing of a heart valve that is often first heard as a heart murmur (Mattin et al.).

**Hydrocephalus:** the UFAW genetic welfare database lists Chihuahuas as predisposed to too much fluid in the skull, as other toy breeds are. Signs usually show in the first weeks or months of life: a strongly domed skull, being smaller than littermates, slow learning, odd behaviour such as pressing the head against a wall, seizures and blindness. UFAW knows of no published figures on how common it is.

The PDSA also lists slipping kneecaps (luxating patellas), hip dysplasia, dry eye, inward-rolling eyelids (entropion), a weak windpipe (tracheal collapse), Legg-Perthes disease of the hip, and difficult births. If your dog limps or skips on a back leg, see [limping in dogs](/animals/dog-limping).

## Which health tests matter for a Chihuahua puppy?

The Royal Kennel Club lists an eye test under the BVA/KC/ISDS Eye Scheme as best practice before breeding, for both coat varieties. In August 2026 it also recognised breathing tests for brachycephalic obstructive airway syndrome (BOAS) under the Respiratory Function Grading Scheme for the breed; the test joins its Health Standard from January 2027. The PDSA adds that parents can be checked for slipping kneecaps, hip-scored, and heart-scanned for valve problems. Ask to see the parents' results.

## How much exercise does a Chihuahua need?

The Royal Kennel Club gives up to 30 minutes a day. The PDSA advises at least half an hour, split into two short walks, plus play, short training sessions and time to run in a safe area.

## Is a Chihuahua right for me?

The FCI standard describes a quick, alert, lively and very courageous dog. That only tells you what breeders aim for: every dog is an individual. The VetCompass authors warn about impulse buying of Chihuahua puppies by owners with little experience of dogs. Plan for daily tooth care and regular dental checks, careful feeding to keep the weight in check, and ask for the parents' health tests.
