## How long do Dachshunds live?

Dachshunds are among the longer-lived breeds. A 2024 UK study found a median lifespan of 13.2 years for Dachshunds and 14.0 years for Miniature Dachshunds; the Miniature Dachshund was among the breeds least at risk of early death (McMillan et al.). The Royal Kennel Club gives the lifespan as over 12 years.

## How big does a Dachshund get?

The FCI breed standard, set by the breed's home country Germany, recognises three sizes, measured by chest girth at 15 months or older rather than by height:

- **Standard Dachshund:** males over 37 up to 47 cm, females over 35 up to 45 cm
- **Miniature Dachshund:** males over 32 up to 37 cm, females over 30 up to 35 cm
- **Rabbit Dachshund** (Kaninchenteckel): males 27–32 cm, females 25–30 cm

Each comes in three coats, smooth-haired, wire-haired and long-haired, so the FCI counts nine varieties. In the UK there are two sizes, and miniatures should preferably weigh under 5 kg (Royal Kennel Club).

## What health problems do Dachshunds have?

The big one is the back. **Intervertebral disc disease (IVDD)** is linked to selection for the breed's short legs. In chondrodystrophic breeds like the Dachshund, the discs between the vertebrae age early: changes can start at 4 months, and by one year about 90% of these dogs have changes in several discs. Damaged discs can bulge or burst into the spinal canal and press on the spinal cord (UFAW genetic welfare database).

- About 25% of Dachshunds develop IVDD badly enough to need veterinary treatment, and more may have back pain that is never recognised.
- About 80% of cases start between 3 and 7 years of age.
- The pain can be severe and last days to weeks, and come back. Some dogs are paralysed, some permanently; IVDD can lead to death or euthanasia.
- Many cases need fast specialist treatment, often major spinal surgery.

A 2026 study of 4,295 Dachshunds in the UK, Norway and Denmark found that smooth-coated Dachshunds had worse spinal health than the other coat types, and small smooth Dachshunds did worst in most comparisons (Hopper et al.).

The RSPCA counts long backs among the extreme body shapes that raise the risk of spinal problems and chronic pain.

## What are the signs of a back problem in a Dachshund?

The UFAW database lists signs of pain such as not wanting to climb steps or jump into the car, yelping for no clear reason, standing with the head low and the back arched, a tense neck, back or belly, restlessness or panting. A problem in the neck mainly causes neck pain; in the middle of the back it causes weakness or wobbliness of the hind legs and, in severe cases, paralysis and incontinence.

How quickly a dog with spinal cord damage gets treatment can greatly affect the outcome, so see a vet promptly if your Dachshund shows signs of back pain, and straight away if it cannot use its hind legs. See also [limping in dogs](/animals/dog-limping).

## Which health tests matter for a Dachshund puppy?

The Royal Kennel Club recommends spine screening under its IVDD screening scheme (supported by Dachshund Health UK) before breeding. According to UFAW, X-rays of the back between 2 and 4 years show how many discs have calcified; it has been recommended not to breed from dogs with more than 4 calcified discs at 2 years of age, and Norway, Denmark and Finland run breeding programmes based on this. There is no genetic test for IVDD yet.

## Is a Dachshund right for me?

The FCI standard describes a dog that is friendly by nature, neither fearful nor aggressive, with an even temperament, and a passionate, persistent hunting dog with an excellent nose. The Royal Kennel Club lists up to 1 hour of exercise a day and says the breed suits a small house with a small or medium garden. Plan for the risk of back disease, which can mean major spinal surgery.
