## How long do West Highland White Terriers live?

Longer than most dogs. A 2024 UK study that included 11,932 Westies found a median lifespan of 13.4 years, against 12.0 years for crossbred dogs (McMillan et al., supplementary data). A VetCompass study of 6,605 Westies under UK vet care in 2016 found the same median of 13.4 years, based on 164 dogs that died; unusually, males outlived females (13.8 against 12.9 years) (O'Neill et al.). The authors note that their method may overestimate longevity in breeds, like the Westie, that are becoming less popular. The Royal Kennel Club gives over 12 years.

## How big does a Westie get?

Small. The FCI breed standard gives a height at the withers of approximately 28 cm and describes a strongly built dog, deep in the chest, with powerful hindquarters. In UK vet records the mean adult weight was 9.6 kg, 10.1 kg for males and 9.0 kg for females (O'Neill et al.).

## What is a Westie like?

The FCI standard describes a small, active, game and hardy terrier "possessed of no small amount of self-esteem", alert, gay, courageous and self-reliant but friendly. That is the breeding goal; every dog is an individual. The PDSA says Westies are clever, loving and surprisingly energetic, can be mischievous, and will look for any gap in a fence.

## How do I care for a Westie's coat?

The FCI standard describes a double coat: a harsh outer coat about 5 cm long without curl over a short, soft, close undercoat, and white is the only allowed colour. The Royal Kennel Club lists grooming more than once a week; the coat sheds. The PDSA mentions grooming costs potentially every six weeks.

## How much exercise does a Westie need?

The Royal Kennel Club gives up to an hour a day. The PDSA says an hour, split into a couple of walks with time to sniff and explore and time off lead in a secure area, plus training and play.

## What health problems do Westies have?

**Skin:** allergic skin disease is the breed's best-known problem. The UFAW genetic welfare database says atopic dermatitis is common in Westies and cites a study suggesting up to 25% are affected. It causes itching from mild to severe, and scratching damages the skin and leads to infections. Signs usually start before three years of age and last for life, although they wax and wane. In VetCompass records, cutaneous (skin) disorders were the most common group of problems in Westies (22.7% in one year), and allergic skin disorder was diagnosed in 6.5% (O'Neill et al.). See [itching in dogs](/animals/dog-itching).

**Ears, teeth and nails:** the most common specific problems in the VetCompass study were periodontal disease (15.7%), ear infection (10.6%), overgrown nails (7.2%), allergic skin disorder (6.5%) and obesity (6.1%). See [ear infections in dogs](/animals/dog-ear-infection).

**Jaw disease in puppies:** craniomandibular osteopathy is an abnormal growth of bone in the skull, mainly the lower jaw, in dogs under a year old. It comes and goes during the first year, often with pain, poor appetite and fever, and can make it hard to open and close the mouth; usually the bone growth slows and regresses by about one year. It is inherited as an autosomal recessive trait, but there is no test for carriers (UFAW). A puppy that is reluctant to eat or open its mouth should see a vet.

**Dry eye:** in keratoconjunctivitis sicca the tear glands are destroyed and the eyes dry out. Westies are predisposed; half of affected dogs develop it before four years of age. It is lifelong and painful, can cause eye ulcers and, without treatment, blindness (UFAW). Redness, a sticky discharge or a dull-looking eye need a vet.

**Joints:** the PDSA also lists slipping kneecaps and Legg-Calvé-Perthes disease, a painful condition in which the hip joint dies and crumbles. See [limping in dogs](/animals/dog-limping).

## Which health tests matter for a Westie puppy?

The Royal Kennel Club lists no specific screening schemes or DNA tests for the breed. UFAW advises not breeding from dogs with craniomandibular osteopathy or their close relatives, and buying only dogs with healthy eyes whose parents and grandparents were free of eye disease. Ask the breeder about skin, eye and jaw problems in the parents.

## Is a Westie right for me?

Plan for a lively, independent terrier that needs about an hour of exercise a day, a secure garden, regular grooming and dental care. Be ready for possible lifelong skin care: allergic skin disease is common in the breed.
