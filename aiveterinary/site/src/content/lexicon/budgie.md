## How long do budgies live?

On average 5 to 10 years, according to the MSD Veterinary Manual; the Royal Veterinary College (RVC) says budgies can live 10 to 15 years in captivity.

The budgerigar is a small member of the parrot family from Australia, where large flocks roam the semi-arid plains (RVC). The RVC adds that adult males usually have a blue cere, the skin above the beak, and adult females a pink-brown one, although this can vary in some colours and in young birds.

## Should budgies be kept in pairs?

Yes. Budgies are flock birds: the Leipzig University bird clinic calls a pair the acceptable minimum and a group the ideal, and says keeping a single budgie for good is not appropriate.

- The Leipzig clinic recommends a male with a female, or two males. Two females often don't get along.
- The RVC says most birds are social and should ideally be paired with another bird of the same species for life.
- The RSPCA lists budgies among the colony-breeding species that can be kept in groups, as long as the birds get on and have room to fly and to move away from each other.

## How big should a budgie cage be?

As big as possible: the Leipzig bird clinic recommends an aviary of at least 1.5 × 1 × 1 m for one pair, with 50% more floor area for each further pair, plus daily free flight.

The RSPCA gives a rule based on wingspan, which in birds is often more than twice the body length:

- if your budgie spends most of its time in the cage, its height, width and depth should be at least **twice the wingspan**; if it spends most of its time outside, at least one and a half times
- for groups of more than two small birds under 50 g, such as budgies, add 5% to the cage size for every bird over the first two
- a cage alone is not enough: the RSPCA says birds need **at least six hours a day** outside the cage with space to fly freely and safely

Before free flight, close windows and doors, switch off ceiling fans and cover large mirrors and windows (RSPCA). The RVC recommends natural tree branches instead of dowel perches, and zinc-free cages and toys, as zinc poisoning is increasingly seen in captive birds. The Leipzig clinic warns about toys with chains, rings, snap hooks and fibres that birds can swallow or get caught in.

## What do budgies eat?

The RSPCA recommends about three-quarters pellets and one quarter washed fruit and vegetables, with some seeds each day but no more than a tenth of the diet; the Leipzig bird clinic instead suggests 70% seeds and 30% greens, vegetables and fruit.

The sources disagree on how much seed is right, so ask your bird vet what suits your budgie:

- **RVC:** seed mixes often give an inadequate balance of nutrients; good-quality complete pellet diets give a better balance.
- **RSPCA:** small parrots such as budgies need some seeds each day, but no more than a tenth of their diet, plus free access to pellets, fresh vegetables and fruit.
- **Leipzig bird clinic:** 70% seeds, 30% vegetables, greens and fruit, with grit and a vitamin and mineral supplement.

Always give fresh water. The MSD Veterinary Manual says to wash food and water bowls every day. Never give **avocado**: the RSPCA calls it highly poisonous to parrots, and the MSD Veterinary Manual lists it among household poisons for birds. The Leipzig clinic also rules out alcohol, tobacco, salty or spiced food, caffeine, sugary or fatty foods such as chocolate, peanuts and raw cabbage. It warns that budgies often become overweight from too much food or energy-rich food such as millet and honey sticks.

## What temperature do budgies need?

The Leipzig bird clinic gives 15–25 °C; in an outdoor aviary, the frost-free shelter should not stay below 10 °C in winter.

The MSD Veterinary Manual says birds usually do well at temperatures that are comfortable for people. Keep the cage away from windows, air conditioning, radiators, fires, stoves and draughts (MSD, RSPCA), and out of the kitchen.

Fumes are a real danger. The RVC says tobacco smoke, Teflon from non-stick frying pans, plug-ins and deodorants can irritate a bird's airways and in some cases be fatal.

## How do I know my budgie is healthy?

A healthy bird is bright and alert with clear eyes, active and not huddled or fluffed up, says the MSD Veterinary Manual; because birds hide illness, any change from normal is a reason to call the vet.

The Leipzig bird clinic advises checking your birds every day and weighing them weekly if you can. The MSD Veterinary Manual lists these signs of illness:

- fluffed-up feathers, sleeping more than usual with closed eyes (see [budgie fluffed up and sleepy](/animals/budgie-fluffed-up))
- not being active, talking or singing less
- sitting low on the perch or at the bottom of the cage, weakness or losing balance
- wheezing or tail bobbing while breathing
- changes in droppings (see [budgie diarrhoea and droppings](/animals/budgie-diarrhoea))
- eating or drinking more or less than usual, or throwing up food

Feathers that are plucked or lost outside the moult also need a vet: see [bird feather plucking](/animals/bird-feather-plucking).

## When should a budgie see a vet?

Go now if your budgie is huddled, weak, unresponsive or lying on the cage floor, which the MSD Veterinary Manual calls an emergency; otherwise book a check-up every year, as the MSD Veterinary Manual and the RVC advise.

Go to a vet who treats birds now, and call ahead, if your budgie:

- is huddled, weak, unresponsive or lying on the cage floor (MSD)
- is bleeding and the bleeding doesn't stop (MSD)
- has difficulty breathing, such as wheezing or tail bobbing with each breath (MSD)
- is losing its balance (MSD)
- has breathed fumes from an overheated non-stick pan, smoke or sprays: birds' airways are very sensitive to fumes (MSD)
- is a hen that strains or sits on the floor with a swollen belly: see [budgie egg bound](/animals/budgie-egg-bound)

On the way, think warmth, darkness and a secure carrier: the MSD Veterinary Manual suggests covering at least three sides of the carrier with a towel and keeping it warm.

For a new budgie, the Leipzig clinic recommends an examination by a specialised bird vet and a quarantine of several weeks.
