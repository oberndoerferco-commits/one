## How long do Rex rabbits live?

There is no study of the breed on its own. In UK vet records from 2013, rabbits of all breeds that died had a median age at death of 4.3 years (370 rabbits; O'Neill et al., 2020). For rabbits in general, see our [rabbit fact sheet](/animals/rabbit).

## How big does a Rex get?

There are two main types. The British Rabbit Council's Rex section is made up mostly of the Standard Rex and the smaller Miniature Rex. In UK vet records, 126 Rex rabbits had a median adult weight of 2.4 kg, a little above the 2.1 kg median for all rabbits (O'Neill et al., 2020).

## What is special about the Rex coat?

It is short, soft and velvety because it has no guard hair (RWAF). The British Rabbit Council says it needs very little grooming: regular checks are enough for most Rex rabbits, with a soft brush or a damp cloth to remove loose fur when they shed. The downside, according to the Rabbit Welfare Association & Fund (RWAF), is that the coat gives less protection against the weather.

## Why do Rex rabbits get sore hocks?

With less protective fur on the feet, the skin is exposed to more pressure and friction and can become irritated, damaged and ulcerated. This is pododermatitis, or sore hocks. The RSPCA says Rex rabbits may be more vulnerable, and the RWAF says they are more likely to suffer from it. The RSPCA adds that it can be hard to treat but may be managed with vet care, suitable flooring and a supportive environment.

What to check, from the RWAF's health check guide:

- a small bare pink patch under a flap of fur on the heel can be normal, especially in large rabbits
- red, broken or infected skin is a sore hock and must be treated by a vet
- never trim the fur on the soles of the hind feet; it cushions the rabbit's weight
- keep the nails trimmed, because overgrown nails change how the foot sits on the ground

## Are there other Rex problems?

The German vets' animal welfare association TVT lists Rex rabbits among breeding forms with welfare problems because of their curled whiskers.

For teeth, the evidence is unclear. A 2025 study of 435 pedigree show rabbits notes that an earlier study had found Rex-type fur protective against dental disease, but in its own data very short fur was not protective, so the authors call the link not robust (Jackson et al., 2025). Dental disease is common in all rabbits; see [overgrown teeth in rabbits](/animals/rabbit-overgrown-teeth).

## Is a Rex right for me?

A Rex is easy to groom, but its feet need attention. Think about suitable flooring, check the soles of its hind feet regularly and see a vet about any red or broken skin. Its other needs are those of any rabbit; see our [rabbit fact sheet](/animals/rabbit).
