## How long do Weimaraners live?

Slightly longer than the average dog. A 2024 UK study that included 2,990 Weimaraners found a median lifespan of 12.8 years, against 12.0 years for crossbred dogs (McMillan et al., supplementary data). The Royal Kennel Club and the PDSA give the breed's lifespan as over 10 years.

## How big does a Weimaraner get?

Large. The FCI breed standard describes a medium to large, sinewy and very muscular hunting dog, with a clear difference in type between males and females. Males stand 59 to 70 cm at the withers and weigh about 30 to 40 kg; females stand 57 to 65 cm and weigh about 25 to 35 kg.

## What coat does a Weimaraner have?

The breed's silver-grey colour earned it the nickname "Grey Ghost" (Royal Kennel Club). The FCI standard allows silver, roe or mouse grey and shades of these, with only small white markings on the chest and toes. There are two coat varieties: the common short-haired Weimaraner, with a short, strong, very dense, smooth-lying coat and little or no undercoat, and the much rarer long-haired variety, with a soft coat of 3 to 5 cm on the flanks and feathering on the ears, legs and tail. The Royal Kennel Club lists grooming once a week; the coat sheds.

## What is a Weimaraner like?

The breed was developed at the court of Weimar as a pointing dog. The FCI standard describes a versatile, easily trained, steady and passionate hunting dog, persevering in its search, with a remarkable nose, ready to seize game, and a good watchdog without aggressiveness. That is the breeding goal; every dog is an individual. The PDSA says Weimaraners can make good-natured family pets in the right hands, but can be strong-willed and need clear, consistent, reward-based training from the start.

## How much exercise does a Weimaraner need?

A lot. The Royal Kennel Club gives more than 2 hours a day, a large house and a large garden. The PDSA calls two hours the minimum, with long walks and off-lead running in a secure area, and suggests activities such as agility.

## What health problems do Weimaraners have?

**Bloat (GDV):** the PDSA lists gastric dilatation-volvulus, in which the stomach fills with gas and twists, as a condition that can be fatal. It advises leaving a gap between eating and exercise, which is thought to reduce the risk. Bloat needs a vet immediately.

**Lumps:** in VetCompass records of 384,284 dogs in the UK, 7.84% of Weimaraners had a fatty lump (lipoma) diagnosed in one year, the highest prevalence of the breeds studied, with 3.16 times the odds of crossbred dogs (O'Neill et al., 2018). Lipomas are benign, but have every new lump checked, because a VetCompass study in England found mast cell tumours, a skin cancer, in 0.85% of Weimaraners, the third highest prevalence after the Boxer and Golden Retriever; with only six affected Weimaraners, the study could not confirm a breed risk (Shoop et al.).

**Joints and eyes:** the PDSA also lists hip dysplasia, certain cancers and distichiasis, extra eyelashes that can rub on the eye. For lameness see [limping in dogs](/animals/dog-limping).

## Which health tests matter for a Weimaraner puppy?

The Royal Kennel Club lists hip scoring and elbow scoring under the BVA/KC schemes and eye testing under the BVA/KC/ISDS Eye Scheme as best practice before breeding. Ask to see the parents' results.

## Is a Weimaraner right for me?

Plan for a large, energetic hunting dog that needs at least two hours of exercise and training a day and a firm, consistent routine. Learn the signs of bloat, keep a gap between meals and exercise, and have new lumps checked.
