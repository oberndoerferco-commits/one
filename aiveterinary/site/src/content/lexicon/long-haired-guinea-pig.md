## Which guinea pigs are long-haired?

The best known are the Peruvian and the Sheltie. In the British Cavy Council's standards, the Peruvian has a long coat that grows from a central parting along the spine, falls forward to cover the head and back over the rump as a "sweep", with two rosettes on the rump. The Sheltie has a smooth, long coat with no parting, and the hair on its face lies towards the back. Both standards ask for a dense, soft, silky coat that is clean and free of mats.

Long-haired breeds are rare in vet records. In UK records from 2019, 97% of guinea pigs had no breed recorded; 113 were Peruvians, with a median adult weight of 1.00 kg, close to the 1.05 kg of all guinea pigs (O'Neill et al., 2024).

## How long do they live?

There is no study of long-haired guinea pigs on their own. In the same UK vet records, the 757 guinea pigs of all types that died had a median age at death of 4.03 years; females lived longer (4.58 years) than males (3.74 years). The authors note that guinea pigs seen by vets may not represent all pet guinea pigs. For guinea pigs in general, see our [guinea pig fact sheet](/animals/guinea-pig).

## How much grooming does a long coat need?

A lot. The British Cavy Council gives about 2.5 cm (1 inch) of growth a month as a guide, and notes that a Peruvian's coat can approach 18 inches.

RSPCA Australia advises:

- brush long-haired guinea pigs every day with a small, stiff brush and a small metal comb, in the direction the hair grows
- comb out any mats every day
- if the coat is hard to manage, trim the long hair so it does not drag on the ground, or ask a groomer
- bathe only when really needed, for example if there is urine or droppings in the fur, and keep the guinea pig somewhere warm until it is completely dry; do not use a hair dryer

The German vets' animal welfare association TVT also says long-haired guinea pigs need coat care.

## Can long-haired guinea pigs live outdoors?

The TVT says guinea pigs can live outdoors all year, but that this mainly suits short-haired breeds, and they must be used to the outdoor climate gradually. In summer, sick or injured guinea pigs indoors or out need fly screens to protect them from maggot infestation.

## Are long coats a welfare problem?

The TVT lists breeding forms that it considers "Qualzucht", breeding that causes suffering. In its opinion these include extreme long-haired forms, alongside hairless guinea pigs and Rex and Alpaca guinea pigs with curled whiskers. In UK vet records, 2.43% of guinea pigs had a haircoat disorder, and males were predisposed (O'Neill et al., 2024).

## What should I watch for?

The TVT advises regular checks of weight, front teeth, claws, all body openings and the coat. Bald patches or scaly skin can mean fungus or mites; see [hair loss and mites in guinea pigs](/animals/guinea-pig-hair-loss-mites). The TVT stresses that a guinea pig that refuses food is always an emergency: see [my guinea pig isn't eating](/animals/guinea-pig-not-eating).
