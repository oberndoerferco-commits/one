## How long do Abyssinians live?

There is little good data. A VetCompass study of cats that died in England between 2009 and 2012 found a median age at death of 10.0 years for Abyssinians, against 14.0 years for crossbred cats, and counted the breed among those with reduced longevity (O'Neill et al., 2015). That figure rests on only 11 cats, and their ages at death varied widely. The newer VetCompass life tables (2019 deaths) give no separate figure, because only the 12 breeds with 15 or more deaths were analysed (Teng et al., 2024). The GCCF, the UK's main cat registry, says Abyssinians live on average 12 to 16 years, and in many cases much longer.

## How big does an Abyssinian get?

Medium to large, lithe and muscular, with large ears and almond-shaped eyes. The GCCF gives an average weight of 4.5 kg, with females slightly smaller than males, and stresses that nothing about the breed is extreme. The short, fine, glossy coat is ticked, with at least four bands of colour on each hair, and needs little grooming.

## What health problems do Abyssinians have?

**Amyloidosis.** In this disease, abnormally folded proteins (amyloid) build up in organs. In Abyssinians they settle mainly in the kidneys, and kidney failure can be fatal. Signs include drinking and urinating more, dehydration, a poor coat, gum inflammation, tiredness, poor appetite and weight loss; they usually start between one and five years of age. UFAW says the disease is more common in Abyssinians than in most other breeds and runs in families, so affected cats and those with severely affected relatives should not be bred from. The GCCF says selective breeding has made it almost unheard of now. For signs of kidney trouble, see [kidney disease in cats](/animals/cat-kidney-disease).

**Pyruvate kinase deficiency** is an inherited disease that causes anaemia. A reliable test exists, and the GCCF advises asking breeders for evidence that their breeding cats are clear.

**Other problems.** The PDSA also lists slipping kneecaps (patellar luxation) and progressive retinal atrophy, an inherited eye disease that leads to blindness and for which genetic tests exist. The GCCF adds that tartar and gum inflammation (gingivitis) are not unusual, so the vet should check the mouth at the yearly visit.

## Is an Abyssinian right for me?

The GCCF describes a vocally quiet cat with a "chirrup" greeting that is very intelligent, learns games quickly and can often be taught to fetch. Some follow their people from room to room; others are more independent. They can be extremely affectionate but also very wilful, and prefer playing to sitting on laps. The PDSA describes an active cat with lots of energy that loves climbing, so a cat tree is a good idea. Ask for the parents' PK deficiency and PRA test results.
