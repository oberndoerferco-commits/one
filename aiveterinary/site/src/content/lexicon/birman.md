## How long do Birmans live?

Longer than most breeds. VetCompass life tables for cats that died in the UK in 2019 put the Birman's life expectancy at birth at 14.39 years, based on 38 deaths; only the Burmese (14.42) was slightly higher among the 12 breeds analysed, and crossbred cats reached 11.89 years (Teng et al., 2024). An earlier VetCompass study of cats in England (2009–2012) also found the Birman the longest-lived breed, with a median age at death of 16.1 years, but that figure rests on only 12 cats (O'Neill et al., 2015). The GCCF, the UK's main cat registry, gives 12 to 16 years.

## How big does a Birman get?

Medium to large and solidly built, with a long, substantial body and strong legs. The GCCF gives typical weights of 6 to 10 pounds (about 2.7 to 4.5 kg) for females and 10 to 15 pounds (about 4.5 to 6.8 kg) for males.

## How much grooming does a Birman need?

Less than its long coat suggests. The GCCF says the semi-long coat has a silky texture that does not mat easily and should be combed weekly; Birmans shed their winter coat in spring, when more combing helps. The PDSA suggests combing twice a week and daily during the spring moult. The breed is colourpointed (darker face, ears, legs and tail) with blue eyes and four white feet; kittens are born white and develop their colour later.

## What health problems do Birmans have?

**Heart disease (HCM).** The GCCF names hypertrophic cardiomyopathy as the most severe threat to the breed and says it is thought to be inherited as a dominant trait in Birmans. It is a progressive disease that can lead to heart failure and sudden death, and breeders use screening to avoid breeding from affected cats. International Cat Care explains that the heart wall thickens, that many cats show no signs in the early stages, and that a vet may hear a murmur or abnormal rhythm. In heart failure, fluid collects in or around the lungs and breathing becomes fast or difficult. A blood clot can also form in the heart and block a blood vessel, most often to the legs.

**Kidneys.** The PDSA lists kidney disease and says blood tests of some young Birmans have shown impaired kidney function. The GCCF says the breed carries a very low risk of polycystic kidney disease. See [kidney disease in cats](/animals/cat-kidney-disease).

A cat that breathes fast or with its mouth open at rest, or suddenly cannot use its back legs, needs a vet straight away.

## Is a Birman right for me?

The GCCF describes a gentle, loving cat that adores being around people, follows you from room to room and uses a soft, chirp-like voice. Birmans are less inclined to hunt, share their home happily with other cats and dogs, and the GCCF calls them perfect family pets and highly suitable for elderly people. The PDSA adds that they are not overly active but like to play with toys. Ask how the parents were screened for heart disease.
