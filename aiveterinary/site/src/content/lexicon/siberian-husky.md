## How long do Siberian Huskies live?

About as long as the average dog, or slightly less. A 2024 UK study of 4,453 Siberian Huskies found a median lifespan of 11.9 years, against 12.0 years for crossbred dogs; the difference is small but statistically significant (McMillan et al.). VetCompass life tables gave a lower life expectancy at birth of 9.53 years, from only 154 Huskies, and Huskies were among the breeds with a much higher chance of dying before adulthood than dogs overall (Teng et al.). The Royal Kennel Club gives the breed's lifespan as over 10 years.

## How big does a Siberian Husky get?

Medium. The FCI breed standard gives a height at the withers of 53.5 to 60 cm for males and 50.5 to 56 cm for females, and a weight of 20.5 to 28 kg for males and 15.5 to 23 kg for females, in proportion to height. It describes a dog built for endurance, carrying a light load at moderate speed over great distances, and penalises excess bone or weight.

## How do you care for a Husky's coat?

The FCI standard describes a double coat of medium length with a soft, dense undercoat, and says that losing the undercoat in the shedding season is normal. Trimming is only allowed for the whiskers and the fur around the feet. All colours from black to pure white are allowed; merle and brindle are disqualified. The Royal Kennel Club gives grooming more than once a week; the coat sheds.

## What health problems do Siberian Huskies have?

**Eyes:** the Royal Kennel Club lists eye testing as good practice before breeding, including gonioscopy for PLA (pectinate ligament abnormality), a check of the drainage angle inside the eye. The PDSA explains glaucoma as raised pressure in the eye that can cause extreme pain and loss of the eye, and also lists cataracts. A red, cloudy or painful eye needs a vet the same day.

**Other conditions** listed by the PDSA include hip dysplasia, epilepsy and certain cancers. The Royal Kennel Club also names genetic diversity as a priority for the breed and places it in Breed Watch category 2, which means particular points of concern have been identified.

## Which health tests matter for a Husky puppy?

The Royal Kennel Club lists eye testing with gonioscopy under the BVA/RKC/ISDS Eye Scheme as good practice and hip scoring under the BVA/RKC Hip Dysplasia Scheme as best practice. The PDSA adds that breeding dogs should have cataract screening every year. Ask to see both parents' results.

## Is a Siberian Husky right for me?

The FCI standard describes a friendly and gentle, alert and outgoing dog that does not guard, is not overly suspicious of strangers and is not aggressive with other dogs; adults may show some reserve and dignity. That only tells you what breeders aim for. The Royal Kennel Club gives more than 2 hours of exercise a day, a large garden and a home in the country. The PDSA says Huskies need a very secure garden with high fences, can become destructive when left alone, howl rather than bark, and suit experienced owners.
