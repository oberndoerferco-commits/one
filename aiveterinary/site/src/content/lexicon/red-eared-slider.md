## How long do red-eared sliders live?

A long time: the RSPCA says terrapins like the red-eared slider have been reported living around 30 to 40 years in captivity. A small hatchling is a commitment of decades.

## How big do red-eared sliders get?

Much bigger than the hatchlings sold in the past. The RSPCA gives about 25 cm for females and around 21 cm for males, and says small hatchlings rapidly grow to be as big as a dinner plate.

That size decides the tank: a turtle that started in a small plastic tub will need hundreds of litres of water as an adult.

## What do red-eared sliders eat?

Red-eared sliders are omnivores. The Royal Veterinary College (RVC) suggests about 70–80% animal food and 20–30% green leafy vegetables or aquatic plants, with calcium and vitamin supplements; the RSPCA suggests a quality dried terrapin food with thoroughly defrosted freshwater fish, plus lots of vegetables containing beta carotene, such as sweet potato.

- Feed every two days, only as much as the turtle eats in five minutes (RSPCA).
- Thaw fish completely, to room temperature, or it can make the turtle very ill (RSPCA).
- Give cuttlefish bone as a source of calcium (RSPCA).
- Don't keep sliders with live fish: they may chase or kill them (RSPCA).

## What tank and water temperature does a red-eared slider need?

A big, heated, filtered tank with a dry basking area. The RSPCA gives about 80 litres of water per 5 cm of shell length, water at about 25 °C for hatchlings and 22 °C for adults, and a basking zone of 30–35 °C.

- **Water volume:** a turtle with a 25 cm shell needs 400 litres of water, and must at least be able to swim without touching the sides or bottom (RSPCA).
- **Water temperature:** about 25 °C for hatchlings, decreasing to 22 °C for adults, with a guarded aquarium heater with a thermostat; check it daily with a waterproof thermometer (RSPCA).
- **Basking area:** a dry area under the heat lamp, big enough to support the turtle, with a ramp up from the water, and a second dry platform in a cooler part of the tank (RSPCA). The lamp must warm the whole body, not just the shell, and the basking zone should reach 30–35 °C (RSPCA).
- **Clean water:** powerful filtration plus 20% water changes every week (RSPCA).
- **Company:** sliders can fight over resources, especially when overcrowded, so each needs its own basking spot and enough food (RSPCA).

## Does my turtle need UVB light?

Yes. Without UVB, turtles can develop metabolic bone disease. The RSPCA recommends a 7–12% UVB reptile lamp over two-thirds of the tank, with a UV index of 3.0–5.0 in the basking zone falling to zero in the shade.

- UVB decreases with distance, so follow the lamp maker's advice on the distance to the turtle's shell (RSPCA).
- UVB output falls over time: check it regularly and replace the lamp as the manufacturer directs (RSPCA).
- Guard UV lamps, like heat lamps, against burns and in case the bulb shatters (RSPCA).

Swellings around the mouth or eyes, an uneven shell, or a soft, rubbery shell in a young turtle can be signs of metabolic bone disease (RSPCA). Read more about [metabolic bone disease in reptiles](/animals/reptile-metabolic-bone-disease).

## How do I know my turtle is healthy?

A healthy terrapin has clear, bright eyes and a smooth shell (RSPCA).

The RSPCA describes these warning signs:

- eye problems, to which terrapins are very prone, which may be due to water quality or vitamin A deficiency
- blisters on the skin, which can be a sign of infection
- changes in the mouth: sliders can get mouth infections (mouth rot)
- a soft shell in a young turtle, a sign of metabolic bone disease

A turtle that is not eating normally may have internal parasites (RSPCA): see [my reptile is not eating](/animals/reptile-not-eating).

## When should a turtle see a vet?

Contact your reptile vet urgently if you have any concerns, the RSPCA says, and check your turtle's health every day. The MSD Veterinary Manual advises a prompt first examination after you get a reptile.

- Register with a reptile vet before you need one (RSPCA).
- Have your turtle checked once a year, with a faecal examination (MSD).
- Go soon if you see any of the warning signs above, a soft shell, or a turtle that stops eating.

## Salmonella: is a turtle safe with children?

Yes, with good hygiene. Reptiles can carry salmonella even when they look clean and healthy, and children under 5, adults over 65 and people with a weakened immune system are more likely to get ill (CDC).

- Wash your hands before and after handling the turtle or its equipment (RSPCA), with soap and running water (CDC).
- Keep the turtle, its water and equipment out of the kitchen and away from food (CDC).
- Don't kiss the turtle or hold it close to your face (CDC).
- Germany's Robert Koch Institute advises not keeping reptiles in households with children under 2.

To pick up a slider, hold it at either side of the shell so it cannot reach round and bite, and don't pet young ones, which are fragile (RSPCA).

## Rules in the EU, Italy and Germany

In the EU, the red-eared slider is an invasive alien species: you may keep a pet you already had, contained so it cannot breed or escape, but you may not breed, sell, exchange or release it.

**EU.** Implementing Regulation (EU) 2016/1141 put Trachemys scripta, the species the red-eared slider belongs to, on the list of invasive alien species of Union concern. Under Article 7 of Regulation (EU) No 1143/2014, these species may not be intentionally brought into the EU, kept, bred, transported, placed on the market, used or exchanged, allowed to reproduce, or released into the environment. These rules apply directly in every EU country, including Italy and Germany.

Article 31 makes an exception for owners of pets not kept for commercial purposes: you may keep your turtle until the end of its natural life if:

- you already had it before the species was put on the Union list, and
- it is kept in contained holding, with all appropriate measures so that it cannot reproduce or escape.

Owners who cannot meet these conditions may not keep the animals; countries may offer to take them in, with due regard to animal welfare (Article 31).

**Never release a slider** into a pond, river or lake, however big it has grown.

**Italy.** Decreto legislativo 230/2017 repeats these bans (Article 6). Owners who had their turtles before the listing could hand them to an authorised public or private facility, or keep them until the end of their natural life, provided they declared possession to the Ministry under Article 26, with the species, sex and age and a description of how the turtles are confined so that they cannot breed or escape (Article 27). If the confinement is found unsuitable or the turtles breed, the animals and their young are confiscated (Article 27). The Environment Ministry (MASE) says owners with a declared animal must notify it of a change of address, a transfer to an authorised centre or a death, with a self-declaration, a copy of the original declaration and an ID.

**Germany.** The EU rules above apply directly, including the Article 31 exception for pets kept before the listing and the ban on releasing them.
