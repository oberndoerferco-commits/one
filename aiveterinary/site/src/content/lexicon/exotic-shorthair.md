## How long do Exotic Shorthairs live?

The GCCF, the UK's main cat registry, gives a life expectancy of 12 to 15 years or more. There is no figure from vet records: the UK VetCompass life tables for cats that died in 2019 analysed only the 12 breeds with 15 or more deaths, and the Exotic was not among them (Teng et al., 2024). For its parent breed, see [Persian](/animals/persian).

## How big does an Exotic get?

Medium to large and heavy. The GCCF describes heavily boned, massive cats with a short, boxy ("cobby") body, short, strong legs and a short tail, and says the standard for head and body is the same as for the Persian. Females usually weigh 7 to 12 pounds (about 3.2 to 5.4 kg), males 12 to 14 pounds (about 5.4 to 6.4 kg).

## Why does the flat face matter?

The Exotic was developed from Persians and has the same round head with a short, broad nose. The GCCF calls the breed brachycephalic (short, broad skull) and warns that this should not be taken to extremes. International Cat Care lists severe brachycephaly in Persians and similar breeds among traits that harm cats and should not be continued through breeding. The RSPCA explains why for flat-faced cats in general:

- **Breathing (BOAS):** the normal amount of soft tissue is squeezed into a shorter skull, with narrow nostrils, a long soft palate and a narrow windpipe. Signs include noisy breathing (wheezing, snorting, snoring) and collapse or fainting after play.
- **Teeth:** the same number of teeth in a smaller jaw, so they overlap or do not come through, which leads to gum and tooth disease.
- **Eyes:** shallow eye sockets make the eyes bulge, so the eyelids do not cover them fully; ulcers, dry eye and watery eyes are more likely. See [eye problems in cats](/animals/cat-eye-problems).

The RSPCA advises regular vet checks, watching breathing, keeping the cat cool in hot weather, keeping weight down, and checking skin and teeth regularly.

## What other health problems do Exotics have?

**Polycystic kidney disease (PKD).** Cysts grow in the kidneys until they fail. International Cat Care says PKD was found in more than a third of all Persian and Exotic Shorthair cats in the 1990s, when screening became available; breeders now test to try to eradicate it. The GCCF says the DNA test is a simple cheek swab, that all Exotics used for breeding should test negative, and that owners should get the clear certificates for both parents. Breeding cats should also be tested for progressive retinal atrophy (PRA), an inherited eye disease. See [kidney disease in cats](/animals/cat-kidney-disease).

## Is an Exotic right for me?

The GCCF describes a gentle, affectionate lap cat that loves to be with people and is playful too, and that gets on well with children and other pets. The coat is easier than a Persian's, but it still needs regular combing, especially in spring and summer, or it mats, and the eyes need regular cleaning. Because the GCCF warns against extreme flat faces, look at the parents' faces too, and ask for their PKD and PRA test results.
