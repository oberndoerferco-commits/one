## How long do Bichons Frisés live?

Longer than the average dog. A 2024 UK study of 8,802 Bichons Frisés found a median lifespan of 12.5 years, against 12.0 years for crossbred dogs (McMillan et al.). The Royal Kennel Club and the PDSA give the breed's lifespan as over 12 years.

## How big does a Bichon Frisé get?

Small. The FCI breed standard gives a height at the withers of 25 to 29 cm, allowing males to be 1 cm taller and females 2 cm smaller, and a weight of about 5 kg in proportion to size. Its eyes and nose should form three black points on a white head.

## How do you care for a Bichon's coat?

The FCI standard describes a profuse coat whose outer hair forms loose spiral curls over a soft, dense undercoat; it must be neither flat, corded, woolly nor matted. The colour is pure white, although before 12 months the coat may be slightly beige (champagne) over no more than 10% of the dog. The Royal Kennel Club says the coat does not shed but needs grooming every day, and the PDSA adds a trim every couple of months, often by a professional groomer.

## What health problems do Bichons Frisés have?

The PDSA lists slipping kneecaps (luxating patellas), allergies, cataracts, bladder stones, portosystemic shunt (an abnormal blood vessel that lets blood bypass the liver) and diabetes mellitus. Signs to take to your vet include limping or skipping on a back leg (see [limping in dogs](/animals/dog-limping)), itchy skin (see [itching in dogs](/animals/dog-itching)) and drinking much more than usual (see [diabetes in dogs and cats](/animals/diabetes-dogs-cats)).

## Which health tests matter for a Bichon puppy?

The Royal Kennel Club lists eye testing under the BVA/KC/ISDS Eye Scheme as good practice before breeding. The PDSA advises checking that the parents have been screened under the BVA/Kennel Club Canine Health Schemes and discussing the medical history of the parents and grandparents.

## Is a Bichon Frisé right for me?

The FCI standard describes a true companion dog that can be taken anywhere, is neither nervous nor barks often, is very sociable with people and dogs, even strangers, adapts well and is very attached to its owners. That only tells you what breeders aim for. The Royal Kennel Club gives up to 30 minutes of exercise a day and says the breed can live in a flat. The PDSA calls Bichons affectionate and good with children and other pets. Plan for daily grooming and regular trims.
