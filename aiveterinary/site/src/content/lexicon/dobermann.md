## How long do Dobermanns live?

A little shorter than the average dog. A 2024 UK study that included 3,531 Dobermanns found a median lifespan of 11.2 years, against 12.0 years for crossbred dogs (McMillan et al., supplementary data). The Royal Kennel Club and the PDSA give the breed's lifespan as over 10 years.

## How big does a Dobermann get?

Large by most measures, although the FCI breed standard calls it medium-sized: a strong, muscular dog with an almost square body, 68 to 72 cm at the withers for males and 63 to 68 cm for females, weighing about 40 to 45 kg and 32 to 35 kg. The coat is short, hard and close-lying with no undercoat, black or brown with rust-red markings.

## What is a Dobermann like?

The FCI standard describes a friendly and calm dog, very devoted to the family, easy to train and keen to work, with courage, self-confidence and the adaptability to fit in socially. It asks for a medium temperament and a good bond with the owner, and lists aggressive or overly shy dogs as disqualifying faults. That is the breeding goal, not a promise for every dog. The PDSA says Dobermanns suit active families with an experienced owner and plenty of space.

## How much exercise does a Dobermann need?

A lot. The Royal Kennel Club gives more than 2 hours a day, a large house and a large garden. The PDSA says a minimum of two hours, spread over the day, with off-lead running in a secure area, training and play; a bored Dobermann may damage furniture and garden.

## What health problems do Dobermanns have?

**Heart disease:** dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) is the breed's best-known problem. The heart muscle becomes thin and the heart enlarges, which leads to heart failure or dangerous heart rhythms. The UFAW genetic welfare database cites a European study that found DCM in 58% of Dobermanns. Dogs often look healthy for years (typically from about two to six years of age) before they collapse, die suddenly, or develop fluid on the lungs that makes breathing laboured. Ultrasound of the heart and 24-hour ECG (Holter) monitoring can find the disease before signs appear; ask your vet about regular heart checks. Weakness, collapse or laboured breathing need a vet straight away.

**Wobbler syndrome:** in cervical vertebral malformation, malformed neck vertebrae and degenerating discs press on the spinal cord. Dogs become wobbly on their hind legs, scuff their feet and may hold their head low because of neck pain; it can progress to weakness in all four legs. It usually starts in middle age, though it can appear from 18 months; one US database survey found about 5.5% of Dobermanns affected (UFAW). There is no test to predict it.

**Bleeding disorder:** in von Willebrand disease the blood does not clot properly. UFAW says about a quarter of Dobermanns are at risk of abnormal bleeding, but the breed's form is usually mild; it shows mostly after surgery or injury. A DNA test identifies affected dogs and carriers. Tell your vet if your dog has been tested before any operation.

**Lumps and other problems:** in VetCompass records 6.96% of Dobermanns had a fatty lump (lipoma) diagnosed in a year, among the highest of all breeds, with 3.55 times the odds of crossbred dogs (O'Neill et al.). The PDSA also lists hip dysplasia, certain cancers, an inherited eye defect (PHPV) and an underactive thyroid. For lameness see [limping in dogs](/animals/dog-limping).

## Which health tests matter for a Dobermann puppy?

The Royal Kennel Club lists a DNA test for von Willebrand disease (vWD1) as good practice before breeding, and as best practice an eye test at least once (for PHPV), a DNA test for DINGS, an early-onset neurological disease, and hip scoring under the BVA/KC scheme. Ask for the parents' results and whether they have had heart screening.

## Rules in Germany

German law bans amputating body parts of vertebrates. For dogs, this means ear cropping and tail docking are banned unless a vet finds it necessary in the individual case; the law has a narrow exception for hunting dogs (§ 6 Tierschutzgesetz). Dogs whose ears or tail have been cropped in breach of animal welfare law may not be exhibited or entered in other events where dogs are judged (§ 10 Tierschutz-Hundeverordnung). The current FCI standard itself asks for natural ears and a natural tail.

## Is a Dobermann right for me?

Plan for a large, athletic dog that needs over two hours of exercise and training every day, weekly grooming, and an owner with time and experience. Ask the breeder about heart screening and the parents' DNA results, and learn the signs of heart failure and wobbler syndrome.
