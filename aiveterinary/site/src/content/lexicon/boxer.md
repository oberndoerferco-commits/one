## How long do Boxers live?

A little shorter than the average dog. A 2024 UK study of 11,294 Boxers found a median lifespan of 11.3 years, against 12.0 years for crossbred dogs (McMillan et al.). In UK vet records (VetCompass), the median age at death of 346 Boxers was 10.46 years (O'Neill et al., 2023), and VetCompass life tables gave a life expectancy at birth of 10.04 years (Teng et al.). The authors of the 2023 study say this is in line with other breeds of a similar size. The Royal Kennel Club gives the breed's lifespan as over 10 years.

## How big does a Boxer get?

Medium to large. The FCI breed standard gives a height at the withers of 57 to 63 cm for males and 53 to 59 cm for females, with males over 30 kg at about 60 cm and females about 25 kg at about 56 cm. In UK vet records the mean adult bodyweight was 30.43 kg (O'Neill et al., 2023).

## What health problems do Boxers have?

**Common problems:** among 3,219 Boxers in VetCompass records, the most common diagnoses were ear infection (7.15%), epulis, a growth on the gums (5.84%), corneal ulcers (5.00%) and dental disease (4.63%). Ear infections and dental disease are common in all dogs, but the authors say epulis and corneal ulcers suggest strong predispositions in the Boxer. If your dog squints or its eye waters, take it to a vet the same day.

**Heart:** the UFAW genetic welfare database describes arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy (ARVC), an inherited disease of the heart muscle with irregular heartbeats. Some dogs show no signs; others cannot exercise normally, faint or die suddenly. A gene linked to at least some cases has been found and can be tested for. Boxers also have a higher risk of aortic stenosis, a narrowing at the heart valve that leads to the aorta. Mild cases may live normally, but severe cases can cause fainting, weakness, breathing problems and death in early adulthood. UFAW says dogs should be screened for heart murmurs and that dogs with a grade 3 or louder murmur should not be bred from.

**Short face:** the Royal Kennel Club notes conformation concerns for the breed and points to its information on flat-faced dogs. The PDSA lists breathing problems (BOAS) in Boxers with shorter faces, eye ulcers linked to the shape of the skull, skin fold soreness, epilepsy, hip dysplasia and certain cancers. Avoid hard exercise in hot weather and watch for heatstroke: see [heatstroke in dogs](/animals/heatstroke).

## Which health tests matter for a Boxer puppy?

The Royal Kennel Club lists hip scoring, elbow scoring and eye testing under the BVA/KC schemes as best practice before breeding, and the breed has its own breed club health scheme. Given the heart diseases above, ask whether the parents have had their hearts checked, as UFAW and the PDSA advise.

## Is a Boxer right for me?

The FCI standard describes a fearless, self-confident, calm and equable dog, devoted and loyal to its family, harmless with them but distrustful of strangers, playful, and easy to train. That only tells you what breeders aim for. The Royal Kennel Club gives more than 2 hours of exercise a day and a large garden. The PDSA says Boxers mature slowly and can be boisterous, so it does not recommend them for first-time owners.
