## How long do English Bulldogs live?

Much shorter than most dogs. A 2024 UK study that included 11,065 Bulldogs found a median lifespan of 9.8 years, against 12.0 years for crossbred dogs; the Bulldog was among the breeds most at risk of early death (McMillan et al.). VetCompass life tables, built from UK vet records of 478 English Bulldogs, gave a life expectancy at birth of 7.39 years, the second shortest of the 18 breeds studied, and the English Bulldog had a much higher chance of dying before adulthood than dogs overall (Teng et al.). The authors note that this sample was relatively small. The Royal Kennel Club gives the breed's lifespan as under 10 years.

## How big does an English Bulldog get?

Medium-sized, low and heavy. The FCI breed standard (where the breed is simply called "Bulldog") describes a thick-set, broad, powerful and compact dog weighing 25 kg for males and 23 kg for females, "in hard condition with no tendency towards obesity". The coat is fine, short, close and smooth.

## Why do Bulldogs have breathing problems?

Because of the short, flat face. The UFAW genetic welfare database calls the English Bulldog the most severely affected of the flat-faced breeds. The jaws and nose are shortened but the soft tissue inside is not, so narrowed nostrils, an over-long soft palate and a narrow windpipe obstruct the airway. Constant effort to breathe causes further narrowing and can lead to collapse of the larynx. This is brachycephalic obstructive airway syndrome (BOAS). It starts young, lasts for life and gets worse; affected dogs snore, breathe noisily or with an open mouth, cannot manage even moderate exercise, sleep badly and overheat easily. UFAW says probably all English Bulldogs have it to some degree. Dogs with BOAS also have a higher risk of dying under anaesthesia.

The current FCI standard states that dogs showing respiratory distress or excessive breathing noise are unacceptable. Under the Respiratory Function Grading Scheme, a trained vet grades a dog's breathing before and after exercise; the Royal Kennel Club lists this test as good practice before breeding.

Heatstroke is an emergency: see [heatstroke in dogs](/animals/heatstroke). A dog that is struggling to breathe needs a vet straight away.

## What other health problems do English Bulldogs have?

A VetCompass study compared 2,662 English Bulldogs with 22,039 other dogs in UK vet records. Bulldogs had twice the odds of being diagnosed with at least one disorder, and much higher odds of skin fold dermatitis (38 times), cherry eye, a prolapsed gland of the third eyelid (27 times), a protruding lower jaw (24 times), BOAS (19 times) and cysts between the toes (13 times) (O'Neill et al., 2022). The authors conclude that many of these problems come from the breed's extreme body shape and call for an urgent move towards a moderate shape.

The PDSA also lists eyelids that roll in or out (entropion, ectropion), dry eye, hip and elbow dysplasia, and a corkscrew tail that can need surgery and whose skin fold easily becomes infected. For ear and skin problems see [ear infections in dogs](/animals/dog-ear-infection) and [itching in dogs](/animals/dog-itching).

**Birth:** Bulldog puppies have large heads and the mothers relatively narrow pelvises. UFAW cites a UK study in which 86% of English Bulldog births were by caesarean section; without help, most bitches could not give birth safely.

## Which health tests matter for a Bulldog puppy?

The Royal Kennel Club lists breathing testing under the Respiratory Function Grading Scheme and eye testing under the BVA/KC/ISDS Eye Scheme as good practice before breeding, and as best practice a DNA test for hyperuricosuria (HUU) and hip and elbow scoring. Ask for both parents' breathing grades.

## How much exercise does an English Bulldog need?

The Royal Kennel Club and the PDSA give up to an hour a day. The PDSA stresses never exercising a Bulldog in the heat of the day, because the breed overheats easily.

## Rules in Germany

German law bans breeding vertebrates when it is to be expected that the offspring will suffer pain, suffering or harm because inherited body parts or organs are missing, unsuitable or altered (§ 11b Tierschutzgesetz). Dogs with such features may also not be exhibited or entered in other events where dogs are judged (§ 10 Tierschutz-Hundeverordnung). The law describes the harm and does not list breeds.

## Is an English Bulldog right for me?

The FCI standard describes an alert, bold, loyal, dependable and courageous dog, fierce in appearance but affectionate; the Royal Kennel Club calls Bulldogs calm, people-loving and sometimes strong-willed. Weigh that against the health data: a short average life, lifelong breathing difficulty in many dogs, and frequent skin and eye problems. If you choose a Bulldog, look for parents with good breathing grades and eye results, plan for daily care of skin folds, careful exercise in cool weather and a healthy weight, and talk to your vet early about breathing.
