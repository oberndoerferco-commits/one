## First: go to a vet now if you see these signs

Go to a vet straight away, and call ahead, if your dog is bringing up yellow liquid or foam and:

- keeps retching or trying to be sick but brings up nothing, or only a little froth, especially with a **swollen or tight tummy** and restlessness. This can be a twisted stomach (gastric dilatation-volvulus, or GDV), a life-threatening emergency, most often in large, deep-chested dogs
- has **blood** in the vomit, bright red or dark like coffee grounds
- is weak, collapses, seems disorientated, or has pale, grey or bluish gums
- also has **bloody diarrhoea** or a painful tummy
- can't keep water down, or shows signs of dehydration such as sticky gums and sunken eyes
- may have eaten something poisonous, a bone or an object
- is vomiting and is a young puppy, elderly, or has a long-term illness such as diabetes, kidney, liver or heart disease
- is coughing hard, breathing badly or has bluish gums as well as bringing up foam

## What does it mean if my dog vomits yellow foam?

Yellow or greenish, foamy vomit is usually bile, which irritates the stomach when it has been empty for a long time.

**Bile** is a yellow or greenish fluid, often foamy, that helps digest fat. It normally flows into the gut, but when the stomach has been empty for many hours, some of it can flow back into the stomach and irritate it. That is why many dogs bring up yellow fluid early in the morning or late at night, before a meal.

When a dog keeps bringing up bile early in the morning but is otherwise completely well, vets call it **bilious vomiting syndrome**. It is generally not an emergency, but vets only consider it once other causes have been ruled out.

## What does white foam mean?

White foam is usually saliva mixed with stomach juices and air, but it is sometimes coughed up rather than vomited.

White froth often appears on an empty stomach, when a dog feels sick, after eating grass, or after gulping water. But be careful: what looks like "vomited foam" may actually be **mucus brought up after coughing**. Kennel cough, for example, causes fits of harsh, dry coughing that may end in retching and gagging. If your dog coughs first and then brings up a little froth, it is more likely to be a cough. Film it if you can: a short video helps your vet tell the difference.

Neither the colour nor the foam tells you how serious it is. What matters is how your dog is overall and how often it happens.

## What can I do at home if my dog vomits yellow foam once?

If your dog is bright and has been sick once, offer small amounts of water, then a small bland meal, and watch closely.

If there are no urgent signs:

- **Offer fresh water often, in small amounts.** Gulping a whole bowl can set the vomiting off again.
- **Offer small portions of a bland, low-fat meal**, such as plain boiled rice with cooked chicken breast or white fish. Go back to normal food gradually once the vomiting has stopped.
- **Let your dog rest.** Skip walks for now.
- **Don't give human stomach remedies or painkillers.** Ask your vet first.
- **Write down** when it happens, how often, what it looks like, and whether your dog ate anything unusual. A photo helps.
- **Note whether a cough comes first.**

If your dog regularly brings up bile in the early morning, ask your vet whether a **small meal just before bedtime** might help. Many dogs with bilious vomiting syndrome respond well to a change in feeding schedule, but this is a way to manage it, not a cure for an underlying cause.

## When should I take my dog to the vet for yellow vomit?

The same day if your dog vomits more than once or twice in a day, isn't right after 24 hours, gets worse, or has other signs.

Book an appointment today if:

- your dog vomits more than once or twice in the same day
- your dog doesn't seem right after 24 hours, or gets worse at any point
- the vomiting keeps coming back over several days, even if it is "only in the morning"
- your dog is off food for more than a day, or drinking much more or less than usual
- there is diarrhoea as well
- your dog is quiet, losing weight or has a tender tummy
- you think the foam may be coming from a cough rather than the stomach

## What causes a dog to vomit yellow bile?

Most often an empty stomach, but also stomach irritation, eating the wrong thing and other conditions your vet may need to rule out.

| Common | Less common but important |
|---|---|
| Empty stomach for a long time (bile) | Something swallowed and stuck in the stomach or gut |
| Stomach upset from grass, scraps or scavenging | Pancreatitis |
| Eating too fast or a sudden change of food | Gut blockage |
| Cough mistaken for vomiting (kennel cough) | Kidney or liver disease, Addison's disease, diabetes |
| Travel sickness | Twisted stomach (GDV) |
| Mild stomach bug | Poisoning |

Before diagnosing bilious vomiting syndrome, vets may look for parasites, a foreign body, a partial blockage, long-term pancreatitis and stomach cancer, using poo tests, blood tests and X-rays or ultrasound. This list is to help you follow the conversation, not to diagnose your own dog.

## What will the vet want to know?

When and how often your dog vomits, how long after a meal, what it looks like, whether a cough came first, and how your dog is otherwise.

- what time of day it happens, and since when
- how long after the last meal
- what the vomit looks like (yellow liquid, foam, food, blood), with a photo or video if you have one
- whether a cough came first
- what your dog eats, any recent change, and how many meals a day
- whether your dog eats grass, rubbish or things found on walks
- medicines, supplements, worming and flea or tick treatments
- appetite, thirst, poo and energy levels
- whether other pets at home are unwell
