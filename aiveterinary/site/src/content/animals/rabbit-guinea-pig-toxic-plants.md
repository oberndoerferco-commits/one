## Go now if your rabbit or guinea pig

- has stopped eating or passing droppings, is sitting hunched, or is grinding teeth loudly
- is twitching, wobbly, having a seizure, or breathing with effort
- may have eaten rat or mouse bait, avocado, or has been treated with a dog or cat flea product

Small herbivores hide illness well and go downhill fast, so a rabbit that "just seems quiet" after chewing a plant is a same-day call. If no vet answers, call a poison line: in the UK, Animal PoisonLine (run by VPIS) on 01202 509 000, 24 hours, for a fee; in the US, ASPCA Animal Poison Control on (888) 426-4435. Your local clinic is always the first call.

## What is dangerous

| Substance | Why it is dangerous | What to do first |
|---|---|---|
| Avocado (fruit, skin, stone, leaves) | Contains persin, toxic to rabbits: fluid around the heart and in the lungs, breathing distress, sudden death. Nursing does can get painful mammary inflammation. | Call now. |
| Rat and mouse bait (rodenticides) | Most stop blood clotting; bleeding may be internal and shows days later. Small animals need very little. | Call now, bring the packet. Treatment works best before bleeding starts. |
| Dog or cat spot-on flea products containing fipronil | One of the most severe rabbit poisonings: tremors, seizures, weakness, sometimes delayed by days. | Never apply one. If you have, call now. |
| Ivy, foxglove, lily of the valley, rhododendron, yew, hemlock, nightshade, laburnum | Heart rhythm changes (foxglove, lily of the valley, yew), nerve signs and collapse (ivy, hemlock). Any amount of foxglove is potentially serious. | Remove the plant, call. |
| Rhubarb (raw, all parts), potato plants and green potatoes, tomato leaves and stalks | Mouth irritation, loss of appetite, diarrhoea, dehydration. | Call the same day. |
| Spring bulbs: tulip, daffodil, hyacinth, crocus, snowdrop, iris, plus amaryllis | All parts toxic; bulbs most of all. Drooling, gut upset, in larger amounts heart and breathing changes. | Call with the plant name. |
| Pothos, philodendron, peace lily, calla lily, dieffenbachia | Crystals that burn the mouth; reduced appetite, drooling, rarely throat swelling. | Call. |
| Onions, garlic, leeks, chives | Damage red blood cells. Because these animals are so small, little is needed. | Call. |
| Chocolate, sweets, biscuits, bread, dairy | Chocolate upsets the gut and in quantity affects heart and nerves; sugar and starch unbalance the gut flora and can trigger stasis. | Call if appetite or droppings change. |
| Grapes and raisins | Kidney effects are suspected but poorly documented in rabbits; poison lines advise avoiding them. | Call. |
| Lawn-mower clippings, sprayed weeds (glyphosate), citrus in quantity, grocery produce that is not fresh | Fermenting clippings cause bloat; herbicide residue causes weakness and breathing trouble; acid fruit and old produce upset the gut. | Remove, watch appetite, call if anything changes. |

## What not to do

- **Do not try to make them vomit.** Rabbits and guinea pigs physically cannot vomit, and anything you push into the mouth can go into the lungs.
- **Do not give salt water, milk, yoghurt, oil or bread.** Dairy and sugar make the gut problem worse.
- **Do not give any human or dog medicine**, including painkillers; several are poisonous to rabbits.
- Do not "watch and wait". The RSPCA puts it in exactly those words. Your vet calculates the risk from your animal's weight, the substance and the amount; a quick phone call is cheaper than a sick rabbit.

## What the vet will ask

1. **What** was eaten or applied: plant name or photo, product packet, bait colour.
2. **How much**: a nibbled leaf, a whole plant, how long the animal had access.
3. **When**, and whether droppings and appetite are normal since.
4. **Weight**, age, and any medication.

Bring a sample of the plant in a bag, and the packet of any product.

## Prevention

Walk the run and garden before you let them out: foxglove, ivy and bulbs are common in UK gardens, and rabbits find rat bait attractive. Keep house plants above cage height and pick up dropped leaves. Buy flea and mite treatments only from your vet and only ones labelled for rabbits or guinea pigs. Feed hay, fresh water, a small portion of leafy greens and a measured pellet ration; the kitchen scraps that cause trouble are the treats, not the greens. Store avocado and chocolate out of reach, and do not mow the lawn with the animals on it.

If you think your rabbit or guinea pig has eaten something on this list, phone your own vet today; they know the animal and will tell you whether to come in.
