## First: go to a vet now if you see these signs

Go to a vet straight away, and call ahead, if your dog is trembling and:

- the trembling turns into a **seizure**: your dog collapses, goes stiff, paddles their legs, drools or froths, or doesn't respond to you. Don't move your dog during a seizure unless your vet tells you to, and film it if you can
- may have eaten something poisonous. Slug and snail pellets, [chocolate](/animals/dog-ate-chocolate) and sugar-free products containing xylitol can all cause tremors, sometimes before other signs.
- is vomiting, has diarrhoea, or has a swollen tummy as well
- is very weak, wobbly, or has pale or bluish gums
- is a **toy-breed puppy** that is weak, unsteady or collapses: the MSD Veterinary Manual notes that toy breeds can have low blood sugar in their first six months, and that dangerously low blood sugar shows as weakness, poor coordination, seizures and collapse
- is a **nursing mother** who is panting, restless, trembling or stiff. This can be **eclampsia** (low blood calcium), which the MSD Veterinary Manual calls life-threatening and in need of immediate treatment. It usually appears 2 to 3 weeks after the birth, most often in small breeds with large litters. Keep the puppies off her until your vet says otherwise
- has been in the heat or exercising hard and won't settle, or has been out in cold water or weather and feels cold
- seems in obvious pain: crying out, a hunched back, not wanting to move, yelping when picked up

## Is it normal for a dog to tremble?

Often, yes: dogs tremble from cold, fear, anxiety or excitement, while dreaming, and older dogs' back legs often shake from weakness.

Many causes are not illness:

- **Cold**, especially in small, thin, short-haired or elderly dogs. If it stops when you warm them up, that was probably it.
- **Fear or anxiety**: fireworks, thunderstorms, the vet's. Look for ears back, tail tucked, panting or hiding.
- **Excitement**, for example while waiting for a walk or food.
- **Dreaming**: twitching and paddling in sleep is normal.
- **Back legs shaking in older dogs**, often linked to muscle weakness or arthritis. Mention it to your vet, but it's rarely an emergency.

Context is the key. A dog that trembles but is eating, playing and walking normally, and stops when the situation changes, is very different from a dog that trembles at rest, in a warm house, and seems flat or sore.

## What should I do at home while I wait?

Film the trembling, note where and when it happens, check gently for pain, and keep your dog warm and quiet.

- **Film it.** Trembling often stops or changes at the clinic, and a short video is worth more than any description.
- Note **where** it happens: the whole body, the back legs, the head, one area.
- Note **when**: at rest, standing, after exercise, after meals, at night.
- **Check gently for pain** by touching the back, neck and tummy. Note any spot where your dog flinches or cries.
- **Check the gums.** Pink is reassuring; pale, dark red or bluish is not.
- If you can, take your dog's temperature: see [how to take your dog's temperature](/animals/dog-fever).
- Keep your dog warm, quiet and with water available.
- **Don't give human painkillers or calming medicines.**

## When should I take my trembling dog to the vet?

The same day if your dog suddenly trembles all over with no clear reason, if it's getting worse or other signs appear, and within a few days if mild trembling is new and keeps coming back or happens at rest.

Call your vet the same day if your dog suddenly starts trembling all over and there is no cold, fear or excitement to explain it. In a study of 198 dogs with whole-body tremors, poisoning was the most common cause (46%), and many poisoned dogs showed no other sign than the trembling.

Book an appointment in the next few days if:

- the trembling is new, has no clear explanation and keeps happening
- it happens at rest, not just with cold or excitement
- it comes with limping, stiffness, or trouble with stairs or jumping
- your dog is older and the back-leg shaking is getting worse
- your dog is drinking or weeing more, losing weight, or eating differently
- you notice behaviour changes: hiding, irritability, staring into space

Go the **same day** if the trembling gets worse over a few hours, if your dog has a fever or clear signs of pain, is vomiting, or if it lasts more than a few hours.

## What causes trembling in dogs?

Most often cold, fear, excitement, or weakness and arthritis in older dogs, but also poisoning, pain, low blood sugar and nervous system problems.

| Common | Less common but important |
|---|---|
| Cold or wet | Poisoning (slug pellets, chocolate, xylitol) |
| Fear, anxiety, excitement | Back or neck pain |
| Muscle weakness and arthritis in older dogs | Low blood sugar in toy-breed puppies |
| Mild muscle or joint pain | Low blood calcium in nursing mothers (eclampsia) |
| Feeling sick | Seizures, liver or kidney disease, infections |
| | Tremor syndromes |

One example of a tremor syndrome is **shaker syndrome** (idiopathic generalised tremor syndrome). A 2024 review in *Frontiers in Veterinary Science* explains that it was first described in small white dogs such as the Maltese, West Highland White Terrier and Bichon Frise, and has since been seen in other breeds and coat colours. Most affected dogs are under five years old, and the fine whole-body tremor gets worse with anxiety and excitement. There is no single test for it: vets reach the diagnosis from the signs and by ruling out other causes of whole-body trembling.

This table is to help you follow the conversation with your vet, not to diagnose your own dog.

## What will the vet want to know?

When and where the trembling happens, whether you have a video, anything your dog might have eaten, any injuries, eating and drinking, and medicines.

- when it started and in which situations
- which part of the body shakes, and your video if you have one
- whether your dog could have eaten anything unusual, even days ago
- any recent falls, jumps or injuries
- appetite, thirst, weeing, poo and energy
- medicines, supplements and recent flea or tick treatments
- whether your dog is already being treated for back, joint, kidney, liver or hormone problems
- for a nursing mother, when she gave birth and how many puppies she's feeding
