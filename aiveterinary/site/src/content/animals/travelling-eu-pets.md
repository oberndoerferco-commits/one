## What does my pet need to travel within the EU?

A microchip, a valid rabies vaccination and an EU pet passport.

For dogs, cats and ferrets moving between EU countries with their owner (non-commercial travel), the rules are set at EU level:

1. **A microchip** that meets the EU technical requirements (if it does not, you must carry a reader so the chip can be checked). The rabies vaccination must not be dated before the chip was implanted or read: a vaccination given before the chip does not count. Chips implanted under the previous rules before 22 April 2026 remain valid.
2. **A valid rabies vaccination**, given by an authorised vet. The animal must be at least 12 weeks old when vaccinated, and the primary vaccination must have been completed at least 21 days before travel. Boosters must be given within the validity period of the previous vaccination to stay valid without a new waiting period.
3. **An EU pet passport**, issued by an authorised vet, recording the chip number, the rabies vaccination and the owner's details.

## Which EU rules apply to pet travel since April 2026?

Since 22 April 2026 these rules come from Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131, which took over from Regulation (EU) 576/2013 (applied to pet travel until 21 April 2026). The core requirements stayed the same. Points worth knowing: the EU pet passport is issued only to animals whose owner habitually resides in the EU; a non-commercial movement covers at most five dogs, cats or ferrets, travelling in a single vehicle unless you use public transport (more are allowed only for competitions, exhibitions or sporting events, with written proof of registration and animals over six months old); and passports issued under the old model before 22 April 2026 remain valid. ## Do some EU countries have extra rules for pets?

Yes: for example tapeworm treatment for dogs or conditions for young animals, and national rules on dogs also apply.

Some countries add requirements. Check the destination's official rules before every trip, because they change. Well-known examples:

- **Tapeworm treatment for dogs** (not cats or ferrets) entering Finland, Ireland, Malta, Northern Ireland and Norway: a product against *Echinococcus multilocularis* given by a vet between 24 and 120 hours before arrival and recorded in the passport. Dogs travelling directly between these countries are exempt.
- **National dog rules**: rules on certain breeds, muzzles or leads are not part of the EU pet-travel rules, so check them separately with the destination's authorities.
- **Young animals**: puppies, kittens and young ferrets under 12 weeks without a rabies vaccination, or between 12 and 16 weeks whose vaccination is not yet 21 days old, can travel only to the countries that allow it, and only with an owner's declaration that they have had no contact with wild animals susceptible to rabies, or together with their mother, on whom they still depend, if her passport shows a valid rabies vaccination given before their birth. Other countries do not allow it.

## How should I prepare for travelling with my pet in the EU?

Book the vet appointment well before the trip, check the passport, carry any medication with a prescription, and keep the microchip phone number up to date.

- Book the vet appointment for the passport or booster **well before** the trip. The 21-day wait after a first rabies vaccination can easily catch you out.
- Check the passport with your vet: chip number correct, vaccination dates legible, the vet's stamp and signature present. The passport may be checked when you travel.
- Carry any medication your animal takes with the prescription or a note from your vet.
- Make sure the phone number registered to the microchip is one you will answer while abroad. A found animal is only returned quickly if the registry has a working number.

## Does a microchip store my pet's medical records?

A microchip is an identification number. It does not hold your animal's medical history, and scanning it does not tell a vet abroad what vaccinations or treatments your animal has had. The passport and any records you carry do that. This is one of the problems AI Veterinary is interested in solving over time, always with the owner's control over who sees what.
