## First, the signs that mean go now

A bald patch on a guinea pig that is otherwise well needs a vet appointment, not an emergency dash. Intense itching is different. The Merck Veterinary Manual says the sarcoptic mite *Trixacarus caviae* often causes such severe itching that the guinea pig can appear to be having a seizure. The PDSA advises that a seizure in a guinea pig could be an emergency, and to contact your vet straight away.

Go to a vet immediately, and call ahead, if your guinea pig:

- scratches so violently that it falls over, twitches or seems to be having a fit
- has stopped eating (see [guinea pig not eating](/animals/guinea-pig-not-eating))
- is weak, very quiet, or hiding more than usual

## Why is my guinea pig losing hair?

Mites, lice, ringworm and fur pulling are common causes; in females, ovarian cysts can cause hair loss on both sides without itching.

| Cause | What you might notice |
|---|---|
| Sarcoptic mange mite (*Trixacarus caviae*) | Severe itching; the scratching damages the skin, which can become infected |
| Fur mite (*Chirodiscoides caviae*) | Often no signs; severe cases itch and lose hair |
| Lice | Usually milder signs; heavy infestations cause hair loss and crusty skin |
| Ringworm (a fungal skin infection) | Scaly, patchy, often round bald patches on the face, feet or back |
| Over-grooming (fur pulling) | Bald patches where the guinea pig, or a companion, pulls out the fur |
| Ovarian cysts in females | Hair loss on both flanks and the belly, without itching |

Where this comes from: the descriptions of mites, lice and ringworm are from the Merck Veterinary Manual, which says ringworm is common in young to middle-aged guinea pigs. The PDSA describes over-grooming as a guinea pig or one of its companions grooming too much and pulling out the fur, and lists stress among the causes of skin problems. For ovarian cysts, the MSD Veterinary Manual says they are common in females over one year old, usually affect both ovaries and can also cause a poorer appetite and low energy. In females, the Merck Veterinary Manual notes that ovarian cysts can cause hair loss on both flanks and the belly without itching. A rough coat can also be a sign of [vitamin C deficiency](/animals/guinea-pig-vitamin-c), according to the MSD Veterinary Manual.

## How can I tell if my guinea pig has mites?

Usually you can't be sure at home: your vet confirms mites, lice or ringworm by looking at samples under the microscope.

- The Merck Veterinary Manual says the diagnosis is made by examining samples under the microscope and seeing the parasites or their eggs.
- Mites spread through direct contact between guinea pigs and through infested bedding and other enclosure materials, according to the Merck Veterinary Manual. It advises treating guinea pigs that live with an affected animal too, even if they look healthy, and disinfecting the environment.
- The PDSA lists signs of skin problems to look out for: bald patches, sore red or flaky skin, a lot of scratching or over-grooming, scabs, black specks in the fur or on the skin, and insects in the fur.
- The PDSA warns that skin problems in guinea pigs can be very uncomfortable or painful and tend to get worse over time.

## Can I catch mites or ringworm from my guinea pig?

Yes: the mange mite *Trixacarus caviae* and ringworm can both spread from guinea pigs to people.

- The Merck Veterinary Manual says lice and fur mites usually stay on their own species, but the mange mite *Trixacarus caviae* can spread to people.
- Ringworm can be passed from guinea pigs to people, the Merck Veterinary Manual warns. The fungus can survive in the environment and cause reinfection, and other guinea pigs in the household can carry it without signs.
- A German study of guinea pigs from breeders, published in the Journal of Fungi in 2020, describes the guinea pig as the main carrier of the ringworm fungus *Trichophyton benhamiae*, and most of the guinea pigs it found carrying the fungus showed no signs. The authors write that the fungus causes highly inflammatory infections in people, especially children who have had contact with these animals.

If you or your children develop itchy, red or ring-shaped patches on the skin, see your doctor and mention that you keep guinea pigs.

## Can I treat guinea pig mites at home?

No: several different problems look alike, so the cause has to be confirmed, and the treatment, including for companions, is a vet decision.

- **Treat parasites only as your vet advises.** The RSPCA advises getting guinea pigs treated for parasites such as mites as advised by your vet.
- **Do not use products meant for other animals**, or home remedies, without your vet's advice.
- **Do not give leftover medicines.** The MSD Veterinary Manual warns that many common antibiotics are dangerous for guinea pigs.
- **Keep new or affected guinea pigs apart** until a vet has sorted out the skin problem, as the PDSA suggests.

## How can I prevent skin problems in my guinea pig?

A clean enclosure, dust- and mould-free hay and bedding, regular health checks, a good diet and checking new guinea pigs before they join the group.

The PDSA recommends:

- keeping the enclosure clean
- hay and bedding free of dust, parasites and mould
- regular health checks
- a suitable diet, including enough vitamin C
- checking new guinea pigs before introducing them to your group
- gentle grooming for long-haired guinea pigs, started early

The RSPCA adds that long-haired breeds need grooming every day.

## When should I take my guinea pig to the vet for hair loss?

This week for a bald patch on a guinea pig that is otherwise well; today if it is scratching a lot, has sore skin or scabs, or is eating less.

See a vet today if your guinea pig:

- is scratching a lot, or seems uncomfortable
- has sore, red, flaky or scabby skin, or wounds from scratching
- is eating less or losing weight
- lives with other guinea pigs that are losing hair or scratching too

If someone in your household develops an itchy rash, see your doctor, and have your guinea pigs checked by a vet soon.

Book a visit this week if your guinea pig has a bald patch but is bright, eating normally and not scratching, or if your female guinea pig is losing hair on both sides of her body.

## What will the vet want to know about my guinea pig's skin?

When it started and where, how much it itches, whether companions or people are affected, hay and bedding, diet, and any product you have used.

- When the hair loss or scratching started, and where on the body
- How much it itches, and whether it has had anything like a fit
- Whether its companions have hair loss or itch too, and whether a new guinea pig joined recently
- Whether anyone in your household has an itchy rash
- What hay and bedding you use, and whether anything has changed
- Its diet, especially fresh vegetables and pellets
- For a female: her age, and whether she has been spayed
- Any shampoo, spot-on or other product you have used

If your guinea pig is scratching so hard that it falls over or has a fit, stop reading and call your vet now.
