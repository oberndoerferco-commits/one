## First, the signs that mean go now

Breathing problems in guinea pigs can become serious quickly. The MSD Veterinary Manual says pneumonia is a leading cause of death in guinea pigs, especially young ones, and is usually caused by bacteria. The PDSA calls difficulty breathing an emergency.

Go to a vet immediately, and call ahead, if your guinea pig:

- is struggling to breathe, or breathing fast or heavily
- is wheezing, or its breathing is noisy
- has stopped eating, or seems weak
- is young and sneezing, with a runny nose or eyes
- is breathing heavily after being in the heat or the sun

Do not wait to see if it settles. The PDSA says a respiratory infection won't go away on its own and can quickly become fatal if left untreated. The Merck Veterinary Manual even lists sudden death among the signs of pneumonia in guinea pigs.

Heat can cause breathing problems too: the PDSA warns that a guinea pig that gets too hot can develop severe breathing problems, and that heat stroke can be fatal.

## Why is my guinea pig sneezing?

Most often because of an infection of the airways; the PDSA notes that breathing problems can also come from heart problems, stress or heat stroke.

The PDSA says breathing problems in guinea pigs are most commonly caused by pneumonia or infections, but can also be caused by other issues such as heart problems and stress.

According to the MSD Veterinary Manual, pneumonia is usually caused by bacteria. Some guinea pigs carry respiratory bacteria in their nose and throat without showing any signs, and spread the infection to others by sneezing or coughing. Stress, age or problems with the immune system can then make the infection severe in an otherwise healthy guinea pig.

The Merck Veterinary Manual names *Bordetella bronchiseptica* and *Streptococcus pneumoniae* as common bacterial causes, and lists poor husbandry, including vitamin C deficiency, among the factors that make pneumonia more likely. It also notes that a vet will consider other causes of breathing difficulty, such as lung tumours and heart disease.

The PDSA adds that these infections spread between guinea pigs, through contaminated items and through unsuitable bedding.

## What are the signs of a respiratory infection in guinea pigs?

Sneezing, a runny nose or eyes, noisy or laboured breathing, coughing, and a guinea pig that eats less and loses weight.

The MSD Veterinary Manual and the PDSA list:

- sneezing, and sometimes coughing
- discharge from the nose or eyes, or red eyes
- wheezing or noisy breathing
- fast, heavy or laboured breathing
- eating less and losing weight
- weakness and low energy

The MSD Veterinary Manual warns that guinea pigs hide signs of illness until they are very sick, so take even mild signs seriously.

## Can rabbits or dogs give my guinea pig a respiratory infection?

Yes: rabbits and dogs can carry the bacterium *Bordetella bronchiseptica* without being ill themselves.

Rabbits can carry the bacterium *Bordetella bronchiseptica*, which the PDSA says can make guinea pigs very unwell. The PDSA also points out that a type of bacteria harmless to rabbits can cause a serious respiratory illness in guinea pigs. The Merck Veterinary Manual adds that *Bordetella* can be passed on by dogs and rabbits that look perfectly healthy but carry it.

This is one reason the PDSA says rabbits and guinea pigs don't make good companions and shouldn't be kept together.

## How can I lower the risk of respiratory infections?

Keep the enclosure clean, use dust-free bedding, feed enough vitamin C, keep guinea pigs away from rabbits and protect them from heat.

- **Clean, dust-free bedding.** The PDSA recommends cleaning the enclosure regularly and using dust-free bedding, such as paper-based bedding or fleece.
- **Enough vitamin C.** The PDSA explains that low vitamin C levels can affect a guinea pig's ability to fight infection, and suggests feeding vitamin C-rich foods such as red bell peppers regularly. See [guinea pig vitamin C](/animals/guinea-pig-vitamin-c).
- **No rabbits in the same enclosure**, for the reason above.
- **Shade and cool air in hot weather**, because heat stroke can lead to severe breathing problems (PDSA).

## What should I not do at home?

Do not wait for it to clear up by itself, and do not give leftover antibiotics or human medicines.

- **Do not wait and see.** A respiratory infection won't go away on its own, according to the PDSA, so see a vet as soon as symptoms appear.
- **Do not give antibiotics left over** from another pet or an earlier illness. The MSD Veterinary Manual warns that many common antibiotics are dangerous for guinea pigs.
- **Do not give any human medicine.**

Treatment is a vet decision. The MSD Veterinary Manual says it may include fluids, syringe feeding, oxygen therapy, vitamin C and antibiotics.

## When should I take my sneezing guinea pig to the vet?

Today, if the sneezing keeps coming back or comes with any other sign. Go immediately if your guinea pig is struggling to breathe.

See a vet today if your guinea pig:

- sneezes again and again, or the sneezing does not stop
- has a runny nose or eyes, especially if the discharge is thick or the eyes are red
- is coughing or wheezing
- is eating a little less, or losing weight
- lives with other guinea pigs that are sneezing too

The PDSA advises seeing a vet as soon as symptoms appear.

## What will the vet want to know about my guinea pig's breathing?

When it started, whether your guinea pig is still eating, whether companions are ill, contact with rabbits or dogs, bedding, diet, heat and any medicine given.

- When the sneezing or breathing changes started, and whether they are getting worse
- Whether your guinea pig is still eating, drinking and passing droppings
- Whether other guinea pigs in the group are sneezing too
- Whether it lives with, or near, rabbits or dogs
- What bedding you use, and whether anything has changed recently
- Its diet, especially fresh vegetables and pellets, and how old the pellets are
- Whether it has been in the heat
- Any medicine given at home

If your guinea pig is struggling to breathe, stop reading and call your vet now.
