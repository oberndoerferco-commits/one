## When to call the vet today

Patchy hair loss is usually not an emergency, but your cat should see a vet **in the next few days**. The PDSA says some causes are serious and can make your cat miserable, and that bald areas are more at risk of **skin infections**.

Call your vet **today** if your cat:

- has **wounds**, bleeding scabs, or skin that is very red, swollen or **oozing**
- is licking or scratching **non-stop**, to the point of hurting itself
- keeps licking its **lower belly** or inner thighs and is **going to the tray often**, straining or passing little urine. A male cat that **can't pass urine** is an **emergency**: go now (see our [emergency page](/emergency))
- is dull, eating less, **losing weight**, or drinking or peeing much more than usual
- lives with someone, especially a **child**, who has developed **round, red patches** on their skin

## Is my cat losing hair or pulling it out?

Often the cat is removing it by over-grooming: the hairs left at the edge feel spiky, not soft.

International Cat Care explains that with over-grooming, the remaining hair shafts "feel spiky and sharp", while with other kinds of hair loss the remaining hair feels normal. Your vet can look at the hairs under a microscope to tell the difference.

Over-grooming most often affects the **belly**, the **legs** (especially the insides of the back legs) and the **flanks**, and the patches are often **symmetrical** on both sides.

## Why is my cat losing hair in patches?

The common causes are fleas and allergies, ringworm, pain, stress and some hormonal diseases.

### Fleas and allergies

International Cat Care describes **flea allergic dermatitis**, a reaction to flea saliva, as one of the most common skin causes of over-grooming. The PDSA also lists **environmental allergies** (atopy) and **food allergies**.

You may not see fleas: International Cat Care notes that cats often **eat fleas while grooming**. A simple check is to comb your cat over white paper and put any black specks on damp cotton wool. If it's flea dirt, it will slowly dissolve, leaving **red-brown marks**.

International Cat Care says **95% of fleas are in the home**, so it's vital to treat your house as well as your cat. Ask your vet about treating the other pets too.

**Warning:** never use a **dog flea product on your cat**. International Cat Care warns that some contain **permethrin** or related compounds that **can kill cats**, and that even contact with a recently treated dog can be dangerous. Ask your vet for the right product.

### Ringworm (dermatophytosis)

Despite the name, ringworm is a **fungal** skin infection, not a worm. International Cat Care says it is usually caused by ***Microsporum canis***. Typical signs are **roughly round patches of hair loss**, especially on the **head, ears or legs**, with broken hairs and scaly or inflamed skin. Some cats have mild signs or none at all, and ringworm can look very like flea allergy.

**Kittens under one year**, **longhaired cats** and **Persians** are more at risk.

**People can catch ringworm from cats.** If your vet suspects it, International Cat Care advises:

- wearing **disposable gloves** (and an apron) when handling or treating your cat, and washing your hands well
- keeping **children** away from your cat until it's cleared up
- keeping your cat in **one easily cleaned room** if possible
- disinfecting or throwing away bedding, collars, toys and grooming tools, because spores can survive in the home for **up to two years**
- assuming that, in a multi-cat home, **all the cats may be infected**
- seeing **your doctor** as soon as possible if you or a family member notice skin changes

Tests include a **Wood's lamp** (an ultraviolet light), looking at hairs under a **microscope**, and a **fungal culture**, the most accurate test, which can take **up to three weeks**. Treatment, chosen by your vet, usually combines medicine by mouth for **at least six weeks** with creams, shampoos or dips. Don't use human antifungal creams on your cat without your vet's advice.

### Pain

The PDSA notes that a cat may groom a sore area more. International Cat Care gives the example of **lower urinary tract disease**: bladder pain can lead to a bald or thin patch on the **lower belly**.

### Stress

International Cat Care believes most cases of over-grooming have a stress component, and in a few cats stress is the only cause. But your vet will **first check for a medical problem**, with an examination and, if needed, tests on hair, skin, blood or urine. Only then can stress be blamed.

To help a stressed cat, International Cat Care suggests a steady **routine**, making sure your cat doesn't have to **compete** for food, trays and resting places, and keeping it **mentally stimulated**.

### Hormonal causes

The PDSA lists **hormonal diseases**, such as **hyperthyroidism**, among the causes of hair loss. The MSD Veterinary Manual says hair loss from hormonal problems is **not usually itchy at first**, and that your vet may run **blood and urine tests** to look for it. See [thyroid problems in dogs and cats](/animals/thyroid-dogs-cats).

## What will the vet do?

Look for the cause with a history, examination and tests such as flea checks, hair and skin tests, ringworm tests and blood or urine tests, and relieve itching meanwhile.

The MSD Veterinary Manual explains that hair loss is a **sign**, and its cause must be found before it can be treated. That takes a detailed history and a thorough examination. Depending on what your vet suspects, they may suggest a flea control trial, flea combing and skin scrapes, cultures for fungal or other infections, a skin biopsy, or blood and urine tests. Because finding the cause can take time, your vet will often prescribe something to relieve itching or discomfort meanwhile. Our [visit brief](/visit-brief) helps you note when the patches appeared and what has changed at home.

## What should I not do?

Don't use human medicines, creams or shampoos, or dog products, and don't wait for the fur to "just grow back".

Bald patches are your cat's way of telling you something is wrong. Get your cat checked, and if it's ringworm, protect your family too.
