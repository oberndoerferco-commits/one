# Production side of social content for a small luxury leather brand in 2026: low-budget system, platform specs, EU AI-imagery rules, measurement

Research date: 10 October 2026. Scope: practical production for a one- or two-person Milan leather-goods brand (watch boxes EUR 395 to 1,682, valet trays EUR 129) that already re-stages real product photos with OpenAI and Google image models. Everything below is marked with source and date; anything pre-2026 is flagged.

Source-quality note for the writer: official/primary sources used here are the European Commission (digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu), law-firm explainers dated 2026 (Reed Smith, Baker Botts, Covington's Global Policy Watch), Meta Newsroom and Transparency Center, TikTok Creator Academy/Newsroom, Shopify Help Center, IAP's own Code text, and Socialinsider's 2026 benchmark dataset. ## Q1. Low-budget production: gear, setup and shooting approach for leather and small desk objects on a phone

### Takeaway
Practitioner consensus is that leather is a lighting problem, not a camera problem: a large, diffused source (window plus translucent curtain, or a softbox/lantern on an LED), light from the side or slightly behind, a fill card, a tripod with a timer, and small angle changes to walk the specular highlight off smooth calf. Texture reads when light rakes across the grain at a low angle and the lens is close (macro attachment or phone macro mode). For ASMR-style sound, get the phone mic (or a cheap external lav/shotgun) within a few inches of the object in a dead-quiet room.

### Cited Findings

Light quality and glare on smooth leather
- Smooth leather "produces direct reflections that can overpower your image if not controlled," while suede reflects diffusely and is forgiving; whether to use soft, diffused or hard light "will depend solely on the texture and color of the leather products you are photographing" — [Pro Photo Studio, Three Essential Leather Photography Tips (undated)](https://www.prophotostudio.net/blog/learning-center/three-essential-leather-photography-tips/)
- Pixelz (studio retoucher) on leather: diffused light "from a window with curtains or shoot-through umbrellas, spreads light evenly and reduces glaring reflections"; tape translucent white curtains so they do not fold or streak; translucent shoot-through umbrellas placed at several angles diffuse better than reflective ones; leather "changes in contrast, color, and hue with each shift in angle or light position, so test several combinations"; placing a light behind the product (not only front/side) gives a more atmospheric look; glossy leather picks up colour casts from surroundings (a bag can reflect blue sky), so shoot a grey card in the scene for correction — [Pixelz, Leather photography studio tips (undated)](https://pixelz.com/blog/leather-photography-studio-tips-and-retouch-image-editing)
- Texture: use close-ups and, if possible, a macro lens; angle the light to emphasise raised or embossed detail; shoot a plain close-up of the texture as a colour/texture reference — [Pixelz (undated)](https://pixelz.com/blog/leather-photography-studio-tips-and-retouch-image-editing)
- Phone-video glare workflow (Ulanzi, accessory maker, undated): diagnose first (a small bright shape that follows the light means you need a bigger or diffused source; a visible window/phone/room means change position or environment); lock framing and exposure; put diffusion between light and product and watch the highlight move; then "rotate the product slightly in small increments and check the phone preview," or move the light across/away from the reflection path, changing one variable at a time; add a neutral fill card only if it does not create a second bright patch; in two-light setups solve the main reflection before adding the second light; a polarising (CPL) filter "may reduce some glare, but results depend on surface, reflection angle, and mounting," so test filtered vs unfiltered in the actual framing; check the reflection during product movement because it returns as the surface rotates — [Ulanzi, How to fix reflections in phone product videos](https://www.ulanzi.com/en-ca/blogs/knowledges/fix-reflections-phone-product-videos)
- Generic phone product-photo advice (GroupGets blog, undated): move the light source until the glare disappears from the phone's view; diffuse harsh light with sheets or paper; use side light with a white foam board opposite to fill, because front light "flattens everything"; avoid mixing window light with yellow indoor bulbs (colour casts hard to fix); set white balance manually (Pro mode on Samsung, Halide on iPhone); use a ~10 s timer on a tripod to kill shutter shake — [GroupGets, How to take better product photos with a cell phone](https://archive.groupgets.com/blog/posts/45-how-to-take-better-product-photos-with-a-cell-phone)
- Weaver Leather Supply (leathercraft supplier) budget advice: shoot in natural window light rather than indoor lighting; prep belts/wallets with a quick wipe of a damp cloth to remove fingerprints and dust — [Weaver Leather Supply, 5 ways to improve product photography on a budget](https://www.weaverleathersupply.com/blogs/leathercrafting/5-ways-to-improve-your-product-photography-on-a-budget)

Prep, props and retouching for leather
- Clean dust and marks before shooting unless an antiqued look is wanted; fingerprints that appear during the shoot can be removed in post; stuff bags/sleeves with tissue for roundness; use pins or fishing line to hold shapes; keep marks that add character but remove accidental scuffs; "avoid over-retouching texture so the product's look and feel stay accurate"; keep the original shadow or create a natural one — [Pixelz (undated)](https://pixelz.com/blog/leather-photography-studio-tips-and-retouch-image-editing)
- Backgrounds: white/light for front and back shots with the whole product in focus; blur or remove the background to cut distraction; keep a background only if it adds context about the intended customer — [Pixelz (undated)](https://pixelz.com/blog/leather-photography-studio-tips-and-retouch-image-editing)

Gear (vendor pages; treat as leads, not independent reviews)
- Phone macro lens attachments: Neewer's 2026 roundup "Best Macro Lenses for Phones in 2026" positions them as "get incredibly close to your subject while staying sharp" — [Neewer blog (2026)](https://neewer.com/blogs/blog?page=9); Moment M-series 10x macro is a widely stocked example retailing in the EU — [Masterfoto.lv listing](https://www.masterfoto.lv/en/for-smartphones/54100-moment-m-series---macro-10x-120-002.html)
- Tripods/rigs: SmallRig's 2026 accessory guide highlights the TRIBEX SE tripod (pan/tilt) and a mobile video cage with wireless remote — [SmallRig, Best photography accessories guide (2026)](https://www.smallrig.com/blog/best-photography-accessories-guide.html)
- Softboxes and lanterns are the modifier types to test for making the source appear larger — [Ulanzi (undated)](https://www.ulanzi.com/en-ca/blogs/knowledges/fix-reflections-phone-product-videos)

Audio for ASMR-style sound
- "Position your phone's microphone as close as possible to the sound source without causing distortion or popping sounds. This might mean holding your phone just a few inches away from the object"; experiment with distance and angle per trigger; record in a room "as quiet as humanly possible" because sensitive mics amplify small noises; prop the phone or use a tripod — [Cal Poly Grand Ave Housing, Create ASMR videos: step-by-step (undated)](https://grandavehousing.calpoly.edu/news/create-asmr-videos-a-step)
- "While phone mics lack the depth of a condenser mic, external accessories like lav mics or even a simple foam windscreen can drastically improve clarity" — [Sandmarc, Creating ASMR with iPhone (undated)](https://www.sandmarc.com/blogs/iphone-filmmaking/creating-asmr-bliss-with-iphone-tips-techniques-and-more)
- A boom-arm "low-altitude" setup with the mic inches above the desk surface for tapping, and "vertical hover" pointed straight down for paper/leather sounds — [Nearstream (mic vendor, promotional)](https://www.nearstream.us/fr/blog/best-asmr-microphone-relaxation-sleep-no-studio)

### Inferences
- For Oberndörfer's smooth calf and Saffiano: the practical loop is (1) north-facing window with a taped sheer curtain or a single bi-colour LED panel through a softbox/lantern, (2) light from the side or 3/4 behind at a low angle to rake the Saffiano cross-hatch, (3) a white or warm-grey fill card opposite, (4) rotate the box a few degrees at a time until the hot spot leaves the lid, (5) black card ("negative fill") on the camera side if the smooth calf mirrors the room. A CPL is worth a EUR 20 test but is not a guaranteed fix on curved lids.
- Shoot a grey card in the first frame of every setup; luxury leather colour accuracy matters legally (see Q4) as well as aesthetically.
- Record the ASMR sound separately with a lav or small shotgun a few inches from the hinge/lid and sync in the editor; the phone's built-in mic on a tripod one metre away will not deliver the lid-close "thunk."
- A kit of phone + tripod + one LED panel with softbox + fill/flag cards + macro clip-on + lav mic is plausibly under EUR 400 based on the vendor categories above, but I found no 2026 source pricing a complete kit, so treat that as an inference.

### Gaps
- No 2026 independent practitioner review comparing window light vs continuous LED specifically for leather on a phone; all leather-specific sources were undated studio/retouching guides or supplier blogs.
- No source gave tested recommendations for a specific overhead/flat-lay rig for small desk objects.
- No reliable source on which phone macro attachment performs best on leather grain in 2026 (only vendor copy).

## Q2. A sustainable weekly content system for a one- or two-person team

### Takeaway
2026 vendor and agency guidance clusters around 2 to 4 Reels per week plus a few feed posts and near-daily Stories for a small account, produced in monthly or fortnightly batch sessions organised by 3 to 5 content pillars, with each shoot cut into a Reel, a carousel built from the same script, stills for Pins and the Shopify PDP. Socialinsider's 2025 dataset shows the average brand already posts about 8 Reels, 5 carousels and 7 images per month; a tiny luxury team should aim lower and more consistent.

### Cited Findings
- Hopper HQ (scheduler vendor, 2026): recommends 3 to 5 feed posts per week, 2 to 4 Reels per week, Stories "every day (or almost)," and argues "posting once a week with strong engagement is far better than posting daily with no response" — [Hopper HQ, How often should you post on Instagram in 2026](https://www.hopperhq.com/blog/instagram-posting-frequency-2026/)
- Kontentino (scheduler vendor): feed posts and Reels combined about 3 to 5 times per week; cadence depends on account size and what the team can sustain with consistent quality — [Kontentino, How often to post on Instagram](https://www.kontentino.com/blog/how-often-to-post-on-instagram/)
- SocialCal (scheduler vendor, most aggressive): for 1K to 10K accounts, 3 to 4 Reels per week within 5 to 7 total posts; claims that 1 to 2 posts a week "weakens how the algorithm classifies an account" — unverified by any platform source — [SocialCal posting frequency calculator](https://www.socialcal.app/how-often-to-post-on-instagram)
- Socialinsider 2026 benchmark (35M posts, 447,613 profiles, Jan to Dec 2025 data; published 20 Feb 2026): average posts per month were Reels 8 (up from 6), carousels 5 (up from 4), images 7 (down from 10) — [Socialinsider, 2026 Instagram benchmarks](https://www.socialinsider.io/instagram-benchmarks)
- Socialinsider's social benchmark page: brands post about 5 times a week on TikTok and on Instagram — [Socialinsider, 2026 social media benchmarks](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks)
- Sprout Social: "Instagram content batching allows small teams to plan, create and schedule a full month's worth of posts in a single dedicated session. Grouping tasks like scripting, visual production and scheduling ensures a cohesive brand aesthetic" — [Sprout Social, Batch content for Instagram](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/batch-content-instagram/)
- Planoly's worked repurposing example: one Reel script becomes a carousel ("a title slide, one slide per point, and a closing save-this call to action") and then a blog post; "Carousels are perfect for bite-sized education that encourages saves + shares" — [Planoly, One post, five ways](https://planoly.com/blog/one-post-five-ways)
- Organise ideas into "three to five content pillars" (e.g. education, behind-the-scenes, social proof, product promos) and match each idea to a format — [Late, How to batch social media content](https://getlate.dev/blog/how-to-batch-social-media-content)
- Pinterest community practitioner: shoots video for Idea Pins in batches, cuts several Idea Pins from one batch, and makes static Pins that link back because static Pins schedule easily — [Pinterest Business Community thread](https://community.pinterest.biz/t/do-you-create-batch-content-on-pinterest/1111?page=2)
- Burnout caution from a creator: batching six looks in one go "felt overwhelming and burned out... Too much batching can definitely feel impersonal" — [Jasmine Star, Types of content you can batch](https://jasminestar.com/content-to-batch/)
- Carousels can hold up to 20 slides with up to 60 s of video per slide; Instagram sets a carousel's aspect ratio from the first slide — [Decktopus, Instagram carousel size (2026)](https://www.decktopus.com/blog/instagram-carousel-size-dimensions)

### Inferences
- Realistic target for a two-person Milan atelier: one half-day shoot every two weeks, each yielding 4 to 6 Reels (15 to 35 s), 2 carousels, 10 to 15 stills for Stories/Pins/PDP, and 2 ASMR audio captures. That supports 2 to 3 Reels, 1 to 2 carousels and 3 to 5 Stories per week, which sits inside the Hopper HQ/Kontentino range and below Socialinsider's brand average, which is defensible for a restrained luxury register.
- Pillars that map cleanly to the brand's "Art of Living" register and to the formats above: craft/process (hands, tools, stitching), the object in use (valet tray at night, box opening ASMR), materials and care (leather education; strong carousel/save material), and the Milan setting.
- Shoot vertical 9:16 masters at the highest resolution the phone allows and crop 4:5 and 1:1 derivatives afterwards; the 3:4 profile-grid crop (Q3) means the hero object must sit in the centre of frame.

### Gaps
- No 2026 agency or luxury-specific source on cadence; all cadence advice comes from scheduling-tool vendors who have an interest in higher volume.
- No published case study of a one- or two-person luxury leather brand's weekly system in 2026.

## Q3. Current technical specs and best practices per platform (2026)

### Takeaway
One 1080x1920 (9:16) H.264 MP4 master serves Reels, TikTok and YouTube Shorts; keep the hero object inside roughly the central 950x980 px of a Reel and inside the 3:4 grid crop; caption hooks must land in the first ~125 characters. Instagram Reels now run to 3 min in-app, TikTok sweet spot is 21 to 34 s for views, Shorts accept up to 3 min. Cross-posting the same file is fine; cross-posting with another platform's watermark or logo is penalised in recommendations on Instagram (official, longstanding) and reportedly on YouTube and TikTok (vendor claims).

### Cited Findings

Instagram Reels
- Dimensions 1080x1920, 9:16; accepted ratios 1.91:1 to 9:16 but only 9:16 fills the screen; min 720 px wide; MP4 or MOV (MP4 recommended), H.264, AAC 128 kbps+, 30 fps min; max 4 GB; length 3 min recorded in-app (some accounts capped at 90 s), up to 15 min uploaded, 20 min for selected creator/business accounts — [Hopper HQ, Instagram Reel size in 2026 (18 Sep 2026)](https://www.hopperhq.com/blog/instagram-reel-size/)
- Safe zones (Hopper HQ's rendering of Meta's percentages): top 14% (~270 px), bottom 35% (~670 px), bottom-right 40% (action rail covers the rightmost ~21% / ~230 px), 6% (~65 px) per side, leaving roughly 950x980 px centred; ads with a disclaimer should keep the bottom 40% clear — [Hopper HQ (18 Sep 2026)](https://www.hopperhq.com/blog/instagram-reel-size/). Other vendors give looser figures (Fliki ~1080x1420 centred; Outfy 996x1400), so the safe-zone numbers conflict between third parties — [Fliki](https://fliki.ai/blog/instagram-reel-dimensions-and-size-guide); [Outfy](https://www.outfy.com/blog/instagram-safe-zone/)
- Cover: upload at 1080x1920; profile grid crops to 3:4 (1080x1440), removing ~240 px top and bottom; covers cannot be changed after posting — [Hopper HQ (18 Sep 2026)](https://www.hopperhq.com/blog/instagram-reel-size/)
- Grid switched from square to 3:4 thumbnails in January 2025 — [Hopper HQ](https://www.hopperhq.com/blog/instagram-reel-size/); [PostSyncer image sizes guide](https://postsyncer.com/tips/instagram-image-sizes-complete-guide)

Instagram carousels and captions
- Carousel sizes 1080x1080 (1:1), 1080x1350 (4:5), landscape listed as 1080x566 or 1080x608 depending on source (rounding); max 20 slides; aspect ratio locked by the first slide — [Socialbu, Instagram post aspect ratio 2026](https://socialbu.com/blog/instagram-post-aspect-ratio); [Decktopus](https://www.decktopus.com/blog/instagram-carousel-size-dimensions)
- Captions max 2,200 characters; only the first ~125 characters show before "...more"; up to 30 hashtags counting caption plus comments; bios 150 characters — [Sendible, Instagram character limit](https://www.sendible.com/insights/instagram-character-limit?hsLang=en); [GrowthScribe, Instagram caption limit 2026](https://growthscribe.com/instagram-caption-character-limit/)
- Recommended hashtag count disagrees across 2026 guides: 3 to 5 (most common), 3 to 10, or 10 to 15 — [GrowthScribe](https://growthscribe.com/instagram-caption-character-limit/); [Outfy](https://www.outfy.com/blog/instagram-character-limit/); [Boomp](https://boomp.net/blog/social-media-caption-length)

Instagram ranking signals and recycled content
- Mosseri's January 2025 statement named watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach as the key Reels metrics for both connected (followers) and unconnected reach — [OnlineMarketing.de, Instagram Algorithmus 2025](https://onlinemarketing.de/social-media-marketing/instagram-algorithmus-2025); echoed in 2026 guides — [Blckalpaca, Instagram algorithm 2026](https://blckalpaca.at/en/knowledge-base/social-media/social-media-algorithms-distribution/instagram-algorithm-2026). The "sends weigh 3 to 5x likes" multiplier circulating in 2026 blogs has no primary source — [Contentgrip (2026)](https://www.contentgrip.com/instagram-influencer-marketing/)

TikTok
- Length: in-app camera up to 10 min, uploads up to 60 min — [Socialbu, Ideal TikTok length 2026](https://socialbu.com/blog/ideal-length-of-tiktok-videos)
- Sweet spots (vendor data, 2026): 21 to 34 s most reliable for engagement/views; 20 to 45 s for views; 30 to 90 s for save-and-share content; Creator Rewards requires 60 s+ (verify regionally); ads start at 20 to 35 s — [Socialbu](https://socialbu.com/blog/ideal-length-of-tiktok-videos); [Stack Influence, Best TikTok lengths 2026](https://stackinfluence.com/blog/best-tiktok-video-lengths-for-creators-in-2026); [Quso, Best TikTok length 2026](https://quso.ai/blog/best-video-length-for-tiktok)

YouTube Shorts
- Vertical or square uploads up to 3 min count as Shorts (raised from 60 s in October 2024, per YouTube Help as cited); longer vertical video goes to the regular feed — [SocialNewsDesk, YouTube Shorts 3-minute videos](https://socialnewsdesk.com/?p=18258); [Socialk](https://socialk.it/en/how-to/how-to-cross-post-tiktok-reels-shorts)

Pinterest for luxury home objects
- Treated as a visual search engine, not a social feed; brands that "treat it like Instagram... [get] minimal results" — [Heropost, Pinterest marketing 2026](https://heropost.io/pinterest-marketing-for-brands-2026/)
- Pins keep driving clicks for 6 to 12 months, sometimes 2+ years — [Limelight Marketing (2026)](https://limelightmarketing.com/blogs/pinterest-marketing-tips/); [Metricool Pinterest guide 2026](https://metricool.com/pinterest-marketing/)
- 97% of Pinterest searches are unbranded (vendor-cited) — [W3era](https://www.w3era.com/blog/seo/pinterest-seo-traffic-strategy/)
- Home decor is consistently named a strong category: "still highly effective for ecommerce marketing in 2026, especially for... home decor" — [Fibre2Fashion, Pinterest for ecommerce 2026](https://emerge.fibre2fashion.com/blogs/11133/pinterest-marketing-for-ecommerce-brands-ultimate-guide-to-drive-sales-in-2026)
- Vendor benchmarks: organic pin CTR 0.10 to 0.20%; Idea Pins 0.5 to 1% engagement; MAU figures conflict (498M vs 600M) — [Improvado](https://improvado.io/blog/pinterest-marketing-tactics); [Outfy Pinterest SEO](https://outfy.com/blog/pinterest-seo/)

### Inferences
- Build every Reel with the watch box or tray centred and all text inside the ~950x980 px centre; then the same file is safe for TikTok and Shorts, and the 3:4 grid crop still shows the object.
- Export from the editor (Edits, CapCut, VN) with watermarks off and never download from TikTok to repost; upload the clean master to each platform natively.
- Pinterest is worth a low-effort lane for this brand: static 2:3 pins (1000x1500) of styled stills with keyworded titles ("leather watch box," "valet tray," "Milan atelier"), linking to the Shopify PDP; the long tail suits EUR 395+ considered purchases.
- Hashtags: 3 to 5 specific ones; the register argues against 30.

### Gaps
- No Meta or TikTok primary spec sheet confirmed the safe-zone pixel figures; three vendors disagree.
- No official 2026 text on penalties for cross-posting an identical, un-watermarked video to multiple platforms; found no source claiming that is penalised.
- No luxury-specific Pinterest guidance (pricing signals, premium imagery standards) found.

## Q4. AI imagery rules: EU AI Act Article 50, platform labelling, Italian self-regulation and consumer law

### Takeaway
Since 2 August 2026, anyone in the EU who publishes AI-generated or AI-manipulated images that could pass as real ("deepfakes" in the Act's broad sense, which includes objects and places, not only people) must label them clearly and at first exposure; the Commission's guidelines say a real product on an AI-generated background is out of scope but an AI image that makes the product look better than reality is a deepfake, and advertising never gets the artistic carve-out. Platforms label independently of the law (Meta "AI info," TikTok "AI-generated," both reading C2PA Content Credentials). Italy adds IAP Art. 2 (no misleading representation) and the Codice del Consumo on unfair practices, which AGCM has used against sites whose product images did not match what shipped.

### Cited Findings

EU AI Act Article 50: timeline (official and law-firm sources, 2026)
- Article 50 transparency obligations apply from 2 August 2026 — [European Commission, Quick facts: transparency rules for AI systems](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/factpages/quick-facts-transparency-rules-ai-systems)
- Commission adopted final non-binding Article 50 Guidelines on 20 July 2026; the Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) entered into force 27 July 2026; obligations enforceable 2 August 2026; grace period to 2 December 2026 for generative AI systems already on the market before 2 August to implement machine-readable marking (Art. 50(2)) — [Baker Botts, EU AI Act Article 50 obligations go live (Sep 2026)](https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2026/september/eu-ai-act-article-50-transparency-obligations-go-live)
- Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content: Commission FAQ says published June 2026 (Reed Smith: 10 June 2026); Baker Botts says the AI Office published it 31 July 2026 (conflict; the Commission's own page supports June). Commission and AI Board found it adequate on 8 and 9 July 2026. Signing encouraged by 27 July 2026 but possible any time; non-signatories must show compliance by other means — [Reed Smith, Transparency obligations: CoP adequacy and final Guidelines (2026)](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102nbz0/transparency-obligations-for-ai-generated-content-the-code-of-practice-adequacy/); [Commission FAQ on signing the Code](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/signing-code-practice-transparency-ai-generated-content); [Baker Botts](https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2026/september/eu-ai-act-article-50-transparency-obligations-go-live)
- No retroactive labelling: for image/audio/video the relevant date is generation; content generated before 2 August 2026 needs no label though labelling is encouraged — [Reed Smith (2026)](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102nbz0/transparency-obligations-for-ai-generated-content-the-code-of-practice-adequacy/); [Commission Quick Facts](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/factpages/quick-facts-transparency-rules-ai-systems)
- Fines up to EUR 15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher; "Proportionality for SMEs and SMCs" stated without detail; adherence to the Code can be a mitigating factor — [Commission Quick Facts](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/factpages/quick-facts-transparency-rules-ai-systems); [Baker Botts](https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2026/september/eu-ai-act-article-50-transparency-obligations-go-live); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102nbz0/transparency-obligations-for-ai-generated-content-the-code-of-practice-adequacy/)

Who is the deployer and what is a deepfake
- Deepfake = "AI-generated or manipulated image, audio, or video content that resembles existing persons, objects, places, entities or events and would falsely appear to a person to be authentic or truthful" — [Commission Quick Facts](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/factpages/quick-facts-transparency-rules-ai-systems)
- Deployer intent to deceive is irrelevant; the assessment considers the possible audience including people with lower digital literacy; content qualifies if it resembles something that "can exist or could have existed in reality" (Guidelines para. 107), so a photorealistic invented person counts — [Covington Global Policy Watch, 10 takeaways on draft Guidelines (May 2026)](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/05/10-takeaways-european-commission-draft-guidelines-on-ai-transparency-under-the-eu-ai-act/); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102nbz0/transparency-obligations-for-ai-generated-content-the-code-of-practice-adequacy/)
- Deployer status depends on exercising "authority" over the AI system; platforms/hosts that merely disseminate third-party content are not deployers — [Covington (May 2026)](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/05/10-takeaways-european-commission-draft-guidelines-on-ai-transparency-under-the-eu-ai-act/); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102nbz0/transparency-obligations-for-ai-generated-content-the-code-of-practice-adequacy/)
- Clearly fantastical or physically impossible content (dragons) falls outside the definition — [Future of Life Institute, Practical guide to Article 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/transparency-rules-article-50/)

Marketing and product images specifically (the key practical rule)
- Per Reed Smith's reading of the final Guidelines: "A product shown against an AI-generated background is out of scope, but an AI image making the product look better than reality is a deepfake." Content can be "evidently creative" only in "specific situations"; "All advertising examples in the Guidelines require standard labeling"; reduced labelling "does not reliably cover advertising" — [Reed Smith (2026)](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102nbz0/transparency-obligations-for-ai-generated-content-the-code-of-practice-adequacy/)
- Artistic/creative carve-out does not apply to content that "serves primarily an informative or commercial purpose and is recognisable as such" — [Covington (May 2026)](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/05/10-takeaways-european-commission-draft-guidelines-on-ai-transparency-under-the-eu-ai-act/)
- Film analogy: AI backgrounds and special effects unlikely to need labels; AI actors, digital replicas and de-aging do — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102nbz0/transparency-obligations-for-ai-generated-content-the-code-of-practice-adequacy/)
- Edit thresholds (for provider marking under Art. 50(2)): exempt "standard editing" includes grammar correction, spellchecking, noise reduction and "minor colour adjustments"; substantive changes needing marking include object removal, face alteration, colourising black-and-white, AI translations/summaries — [Covington (May 2026)](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/05/10-takeaways-european-commission-draft-guidelines-on-ai-transparency-under-the-eu-ai-act/); the Act exempts systems with an assistive function for standard editing or that "do not substantially alter the input data or its semantics" — [Commission Quick Facts](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/factpages/quick-facts-transparency-rules-ai-systems)
- Territorial reach: posting on the global internet may trigger Art. 50(4) if the content is "used in the EU" — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102nbz0/transparency-obligations-for-ai-generated-content-the-code-of-practice-adequacy/)
- Technical feasibility is "an objective notion that is not dependent on the specific resources and capabilities of individual providers," i.e. cost alone is no excuse — [Covington (May 2026)](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/05/10-takeaways-european-commission-draft-guidelines-on-ai-transparency-under-the-eu-ai-act/)

How to label (deployer side)
- Disclosure must be "clear" and "distinguishable," at the latest at "first interaction or exposure," for each person exposed; disclosures buried in T&Cs or layered menus fail; a tiny footer or a label that flashes for an instant does not qualify — [Covington (May 2026)](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/05/10-takeaways-european-commission-draft-guidelines-on-ai-transparency-under-the-eu-ai-act/); [FLI practical guide](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/transparency-rules-article-50/)
- Draft Code specifies icons/labels "shown consistently throughout short videos and at the beginning and regular intervals in long videos," and proposes a common EU icon with capitalised "AI" — [Covington (May 2026)](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/05/10-takeaways-european-commission-draft-guidelines-on-ai-transparency-under-the-eu-ai-act/)
- EU icons (Commission page, last updated 24 Sep 2026): three icons, Basic, Fully AI-Generated, Partially AI-Modified, each in black, white and 50%-transparent variants, SVG and PNG downloadable; "The use of these EU icons is optional, but the labelling requirements under Article 50 AI Act are not"; icon must be visible no later than first exposure, not beneath overlays, embedded in the content (except creative works) unless an equivalent UI overlay is used, and remain visible when reshared or downloaded; testing showed the basic icon works best paired with a text label such as "modified"; detailed specs in Section 2 of the Code — [Commission, EU icons for labelling AI-generated content](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/eu-icons-labelling-ai-generated-content)
- Where a VLOP (e.g. Instagram, TikTok) provides labelling tools, deployers may rely on them to meet Art. 50(4) — [Covington (May 2026)](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/05/10-takeaways-european-commission-draft-guidelines-on-ai-transparency-under-the-eu-ai-act/)
- Art. 4 AI-literacy duty is linked to Art. 50 compliance for staff doing transparency work — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102nbz0/transparency-obligations-for-ai-generated-content-the-code-of-practice-adequacy/)

### Inferences (practical safe/unsafe guide for the brand's existing AI re-staging script)
- Safe without an AI label under the Act (but still subject to IAP Art. 2 and consumer law on accuracy): minor colour/exposure/noise work, dust and fingerprint removal, background cleanup that does not change the product. These are "standard editing"/"not substantially altering" edits in the Guidelines' examples.
- Safe but worth disclosing in the caption: a real, unaltered product photo composited onto an AI-generated lifestyle background (desk, Milan interior). Reed Smith's reading of the Guidelines says this is out of scope of Art. 50(4). Caveat: the AI model must not touch the product's grain, colour, stitching or proportions; if the re-staging model regenerates the product pixels, you are in "AI-manipulated object that falsely appears authentic" territory.
- Must be labelled (Art. 50(4)) and is also risky under IAP Art. 2 / Codice del Consumo: any image where the model re-renders the product (re-lighting that regenerates the leather surface, upscales that invent grain, "perfected" versions). "An AI image making the product look better than reality is a deepfake" is the one-line test.
- Must be labelled and reduces Instagram recommendation eligibility if done repeatedly without the profile label: photorealistic AI people (hands, models) in the scene. Given the brand's register, avoid generated people entirely.
- Labelling mechanics that satisfy both the Act and the platforms: switch on Instagram's and TikTok's own AI disclosure toggle (deployers may rely on VLOP tools), plus a short caption line ("Scene created with AI; product photographed in our Milan atelier") and, for the Shopify site and Pinterest where no platform tool exists, embed the optional EU "Partially AI-Modified" icon or a visible text label on the image. Keep the real, unretouched product shots as the PDP hero images.
- Keep a simple log: for each published asset, the source photo, the tool, what was changed, and the label applied. That is proportionate Art. 50 evidence for a micro-business and supports the Art. 4 AI-literacy expectation.
- The Omnibus grace period to 2 December 2026 relieves the tool providers (OpenAI, Google) on machine-readable marking; it does not relieve the brand's own deployer labelling duty, which has applied since 2 August 2026.
