# Comparable brands: how small and mid-sized luxury leather, watch-accessory and craft brands run social content (2025–2026 case notes)

Scope note for the report writer. These notes were compiled 10 October 2026 from founder interviews, trade press, brand sites, podcast listings, benchmark reports and third-party directories. **Direct observation of Instagram and TikTok profile pages was not possible from this environment: instagram.com returned HTTP 429 on every handle tried (delugs, wolf1834, craighill, bennettwinch), and mirror/analytics sites (socialblade, imginn) returned 404/403.** Follower counts below are therefore only given where a third-party dated source states them; otherwise they are listed as gaps. Material older than 2024 is marked "(older)".

---

## Key question 1: Per-brand case studies — platform mix, scale, cadence, format, visual signature, content pillars, posts that performed

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence of content-led growth in this peer set comes from three patterns: (a) watch-strap/storage brands whose founder is personally active in the collector community (Delugs, Bulang & Sons, Everest), (b) craft creators whose YouTube/TikTok process content built audiences of 200K–1.2M that are then monetised with products, kits and patterns (Corter, Hahns Atelier, Stock & Barrel, Tanner Leatherstein), and (c) design-object brands that keep a single, highly consistent visual system and use cinematic "how it's made" reels as a differentiator against lo-fi content (Craighill, Grovemade). Heritage leather houses (Ettinger, Smythson, Serapian, Il Bisonte, WOLF, Rapport) lean on heritage, retail and press rather than documented social tactics.

### Inferences
- In this peer set, documented social traction correlates with a visible human (founder or maker) and with a recurring, nameable content series (Tanner's "Chopping Block", Craighill's "behind the form", WatchGecko's "60 second gems", Hahns' object-remix concept), not with polished product stills alone.
- The heritage box/winder players (WOLF, Rapport) are retail- and catalogue-led; the social-native players in watch storage are the strap brands that moved into cases (Delugs, Bosphorus). This leaves the "quiet, material, atelier" register for a leather watch box relatively uncontested on Instagram, based on what the sources describe.

## Key question 2: Who shows process / atelier / hands-at-work content, how prominently, and how is "made in Italy" or craft provenance handled visually?

### Takeaway
Process content is the core product for the creator-brands (Corter, Hahns, Stock & Barrel, Tanner Leatherstein) and a differentiating feature for Craighill; for the Italian and British heritage houses it is expressed through signed maker tags, workshop/atelier access, craft fairs (Homo Faber) and named tanneries rather than through documented social series. Trade press reports that factory and "hands of artisans" videos displaced glossy editorial as the trust signal for younger buyers, and that scrutiny of "Made in Italy" labelling is part of why.

### Cited Findings
- "Videos focused on artisans' hands and workshops have replaced extravagant editorial shoots and idyllic press trips"; creators Labwearstudios and Garment Circle get "millions of views" for factory videos; as logos lose impact "traceability and craftsmanship have become a status symbol, linked to the rise of quiet luxury"; scrutiny of Made in Italy labelling "where some products were labeled Italian with little Italian input" led brands to bring production in-house, "which exposed the marketing value of factories"; Gen Z 18–25 on TikTok drives the interest — [nss magazine, 17 May 2024](https://www.nssmag.com/en/fashion/36724/manufacturing-fashion-factories-on-tiktok-popularity)
- Tanner Leatherstein's credibility rests on a family tannery background in Turkey; his format is literally destructive process-reveal (cutting stitches, acetone tests) — [The Nod, 12 May 2024](https://thenodmag.com/content/tanner-leatherstein-influencer-dissecting-luxury-handbags)
- Hahns Atelier edits out repetitive steps and builds each video around a story and a twist, not a tutorial — [YouTube Korea blog, 4 Sept 2026](https://blog.youtube/intl/ko-kr/creator-and-artist-stories/creator-of-the-month-sep26/)
- Corter's provenance hook is "no electricity used in construction", solar-powered Cape Cod workshop — [Feedspot, 2 July 2026](https://videos.feedspot.com/leather_youtube_channels/); [AAM](https://www.americanmanufacturing.org/blog/no-electricity-needed-for-corter-leather-cloth/)
- Craighill's cinematic making-of reel (July 2025) and "behind the form" series — [Algorithm Victim, 20 July 2025](https://algorithmvictim.substack.com/p/013-monday-scroll-starter-everything); [Craighill](https://craighill.co/pages/about)
- Delugs names tanneries (Tanneries Haas Baranil, Tanneries du Puy Epsom) and hours per strap (4–6) in collab copy; production is in Vietnam and stated openly — [Delugs, 5 July 2025](https://delugs.com/blogs/news/delugs-and-vacheron-constantin-a-simple-partnership-that-speaks-volumes)
- Serapian: maker-signed inner tag; bespoke atelier in Villa Mozart; participation in Homo Faber 2026 — [Richemont](https://www.richemont.com/our-maisons/serapian); [Homo Faber](https://www.homofaber.com/fr/artisans/serapian-milan)

## Key question 5: Evidence of content driving revenue

### Takeaway
Hard revenue attribution is thin, but there are several credible, dated signals: Tanner Leatherstein's unpaid videos as Pegai's "best marketing campaign" and his 2025 equity role at Stow London; Hahns Atelier's product/kit/pattern income funding month-long videos; Ugmonk's $1M+ Analog line built without paid ads; Carl Friedrik's organic TV placements producing "strong press coverage"; Delugs' Instagram-led community producing a Vacheron collab and a viral concept post; and benchmark data showing luxury video views on Instagram up 45% YoY in H2 2025.

### Cited Findings
- "It ended up being the best marketing campaign for Pegai indirectly" (no ads, no sponsorships) — [The Nod, 12 May 2024](https://thenodmag.com/content/tanner-leatherstein-influencer-dissecting-luxury-handbags); Stow London made him director of craftsmanship and shareholding partner — [FashionUnited, 5 Aug 2025](https://fashionunited.uk/news/people/stow-london-names-tiktoks-tanner-leatherstein-as-director-of-craftsmanship/2025080583200)
- Hahns Atelier: finished products, DIY kits and pattern templates "provide steady income" — [YouTube Korea blog, 4 Sept 2026](https://blog.youtube/intl/ko-kr/creator-and-artist-stories/creator-of-the-month-sep26/)
- Ugmonk Analog "more than $1M in revenue"; brand built "without any paid advertising" — [Rephonic, 22 Jan 2025](https://rephonic.com/podcasts/yo-podcast); [Player FM](https://vi.player.fm/1BKbvTE)
- Carl Friedrik: 261% luggage revenue growth in two years (company-reported); TV placements produced "strong press coverage" — [The Manual](https://themanual.com/?p=1390613); [Chapter Digital, c. July 2025](https://chapter.digital/en/design-carl-friedrik/)
- Delugs: social is "the biggest asset"; Singapore interest rose after the Sheeran episode via Instagram, website and showroom; team of 26 plus ~40 crafters — [Tatler Asia, 13 May 2024](https://www.tatlerasia.com/style/watches/local-watch-strap-brand-delugs-founders-interview)
- Everest: Facebook credited with spreading the word; $15k Kickstarter (older) — [Worn & Wound, 2015](https://wornandwound.com/real-estate-rolex-story-everest-horology/)

- Cross-industry: Emplifi reports median Instagram engagement fell from 16.9% (early 2024) to 9.7% (end 2025) and carousels/Reels generate ~44% more interactions than single images; Dash Hudson says top TikTok brands post ~6 times/week — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/social-media-concentrating-on-fewer-platforms-2026); [Lindsey Gamble on Dash Hudson](https://www.lindseygamble.com/blog/uncover-key-insights-for-your-2025-social-media-strategy-with-dash-hudsons-free-social-media-benchmark-report) (both vendor-sourced; Emplifi and Dash Hudson define engagement differently from Dash Social, so the figures are not comparable)
- Unverified vendor claim: "fashion brands that have mastered the art of Reels are reporting sales increases of up to 300%" (no named brands) — [Fibre2Fashion](https://emerge.fibre2fashion.com/blogs/11083/how-fashion-brands-are-using-instagram-reels-to-drive-300-more-sales)

### Inferences
- The revenue stories all share one mechanism: an audience built on an owned, repeatable content format first, with product launched or expanded into that audience afterwards (Leatherstein → Pegai/Stow; Hahns → kits; Ugmonk → Analog; Delugs → cases and maison collabs).

### Gaps
- No Shopify case study for a leather-goods brand with attributable social revenue was found. No revenue figures for WOLF, Rapport, Bosphorus, Bennett Winch, Grovemade or Craighill.

## Key question 6: What the best ones do NOT do; common mistakes among small luxury brands on social

### Takeaway
The documented successes avoid: chasing seasonal colour or trends (Carl Friedrik), generic unverifiable "handmade in Italy" claims (challenged at Hard Graft; devalued per nss), ad-dependence (Ugmonk, Leatherstein), lo-fi sameness (Craighill's cinematic contrast), plain tutorials without a story (Hahns), and generic replies to criticism (a complaint about Bosphorus). The main observed failure modes among peers are price perceived as unjustified, slow or inconsistent production quality exposed in reviews, and treating catalogues/retail as a substitute for a social voice.

### Cited Findings
- "We'll intrinsically favor a chestnut brown over a bold seasonal or trend-led color"; products "do not follow trends or seasons, but a design principle" — [Chapter Digital, c. July 2025](https://chapter.digital/en/design-carl-friedrik/)
- Craighill praised precisely because "social tends to lean lo-fi these days" — [Algorithm Victim, 20 July 2025](https://algorithmvictim.substack.com/p/013-monday-scroll-starter-everything)
- Hahns: effort goes into "planning and storytelling rather than plain tutorials" — [YouTube Korea blog, 4 Sept 2026](https://blog.youtube/intl/ko-kr/creator-and-artist-stories/creator-of-the-month-sep26/)
- Bosphorus: complaints of 5-week lead time vs advertised, "uneven, crooked stitching, and scuff marks", leather smell, and "the company's replies to negative reviews are generic" — [Trustpilot](https://nl-be.trustpilot.com/review/www.bosphorusleather.com)
- Delugs: price is "the most common complaint"; a 1-star Delcro review called a one-size strap "not worth $50" — [Trustpilot](https://pt.trustpilot.com/review/www.delugs.com); [Shop listing](https://shop.app/products/6936512069745/delcro-black)
- Hard Graft: unverified "machine-made despite handmade in Italy label" accusation and complaints of unanswered emails; Trustpilot 2.7 — [Carryology, 2012 (older)](https://www.carryology.com/insights/insights-1/5-minutes-with-hard-graft); [InsideHook](https://www.insidehook.com/?p=215412)
- Scrutiny of Made in Italy labelling "led to a supply chain crisis" — [nss magazine, 17 May 2024](https://www.nssmag.com/en/fashion/36724/manufacturing-fashion-factories-on-tiktok-popularity)
- Rapport credits catalogues and website rather than social (older) — [WatchPro](https://www.watchpro.com/?p=6484); WOLF's coverage mentions "digital methods" without specifics — [FashionUnited, 24 Sept 2024](https://fashionunited.com/news/business/wolf-opens-first-physical-store-in-190-years-the-business-behind-luxury-watch-cases/2024092462029)
- Instagram's stated direction: prioritise "creative and original posts over reposts or low-quality material" (Adam Mosseri) — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/digital/how-instagram-plans-to-lead-the-social-media-landscape-by-2025-53450.htm)
- Luxury Instagram engagement averages 0.2% against a 1.5–2.0% target, meaning most luxury accounts underperform their own benchmark — [Dash Social, 2026](https://dashsocial.com/social-media-benchmarks/luxury-industry)

### Inferences
- For a EUR 395–1,682 watch box, the peer evidence suggests the two risks that content must pre-empt are "why this price" (answered by named materials, hours, provenance and the maker) and "is it really handmade in Milan" (answered by checkable specifics and a visible atelier, not a label).

### Gaps
- No systematic study of small-luxury-brand social mistakes was found; the above is assembled from reviews and individual profiles. The Vogue Business coverage I searched for did not surface.
